**When Lights Go Up, Insects Go Down**

光之殇：消逝的夜行者



> Long before humans learned to make fire, moths were already flying by starlight—for billions of years, they navigated by the Milky Way, gliding effortlessly through the darkness. Then artificial **light** changed everything. Electric lamps spread like wildfire, burning outward from the hearts of cities to every **suburb**, then reaching the most remote **settlement**s. In less than a century, that ancient compass was shattered. But moths are not the only ones who have lost their way. Look up, and the **magnificent** Milky Way—the sky that humanity gazed upon for tens of thousands of years—now lies hidden behind a **veil** of electric glow, its brilliance dimmed to nothing.

> A 2020 study in *Biological Conservation* documented the scale of the crisis—and the findings were stark. Researchers found that insect populations near street lamps dropped by half, with the **casualty** count rising nightly. **Juvenile** fireflies, whose lights **twinkle** to attract mates, suffered worst; their signals drowned out by **radiant** glare. **Curious** to understand the full scope, scientists began to **infer** broader patterns from this devastation. The results painted a grim picture: where cities **glitter**, **rare** species vanish; moths **collide** with bulbs until exhausted, and **crane** flies lose their bearings entirely. The damage extends beyond insects: trees near streetlights **bud** too early, **consequently** falling out of sync with the insects that pollinate them. Researchers **remark** that the disruption is hard to quantify but impossible to ignore.

> A **band** of **specialists** now **urge** action, and their findings **arouse** concern among policymakers. The concern is real—now solutions are needed. Some **suppose** a **levy** on excessive outdoor lighting could fund mitigation; others propose **switching** to amber LEDs, since **ultraviolet** frequencies prove especially harmful; still others recommend shielded fixtures that point **light** downward instead of skyward. Many nations are considering action—and some have already taken the first step. France has led the way: in 2022, it became the first country to **prohibit** illuminated storefronts overnight. Others are following suit: across the Atlantic, a **coalition** of American **governors** has pledged dark-sky corridors. **Eager** **reporters** flock to cover the movement, and momentum is gathering pace. **Nonetheless**, not everyone is on board: conservationists and developers still **clash**, and shop owners **protest** that dimmed signs will seriously hurt their business.

> But beyond the policy debates lies a deeper question. The **moral** argument grows ever stronger—and it extends far beyond wildlife. Those who are ecologically **conscious** understand the threat well, but they also see a crisis of another kind: the **spiritual** bond between humanity and the night sky is breaking. There was a time when people gazed upward to **admire** the vastness of the universe; now, with every new light that flickers on, that connection quietly slips away.

> This marks a turning point for conservation itself. **Orthodox** conservation focused on habitat loss and poaching. Light pollution is different—it is an emerging **frontier**, an issue that has only recently entered public discussion. More importantly, this form of pollution differs fundamentally from chemical contamination: chemical spills leave residue behind, but light pollution does not. **Extinguish** a lamp, and darkness returns at once. It is precisely this reversibility that offers hope for addressing light pollution—but only if we act. The task now is to **integrate** this understanding into urban planning, **lest** convenience trump nature.

> What we **cherish**, we protect—and the night sky is worthy of that protection. Countless creatures **sneak** through the darkness; countless lives **patrol** beneath the veil of night. For their **sake**, we must **refresh** our understanding of illumination. Fortunately, the solution need not be radical: a city that **roars** with traffic need not blaze with **torches** through the night. With a little restraint, we can reclaim all that we have lost. The **soul** of the night is worth protecting.

在人类学会钻木取火之前，飞蛾就已经在星光下飞行了——数十亿年来，它们以银河为罗盘，在黑暗中穿梭自如。然而，人造**光源**的出现改变了一切。电灯像野火一样蔓延，从城市的心脏烧到每一片**郊区**，再蔓延至最偏远的**居民点**。那古老的罗盘，就这样在一个世纪内被彻底击碎。但失去方向的不仅仅是飞蛾。抬头望去，那**壮丽**的银河——人类仰望了千万年的苍穹——如今已隐没在电光织成的**帷幕**之后，黯然无光。


2020年发表在《生物保护》杂志上的一项研究记录了这场危机的规模——而研究结果令人触目惊心。研究人员发现，路灯附近的昆虫数量下降了一半，**伤亡**数字每夜都在攀升。**幼年**萤火虫受害最为严重，它们的光芒通过**闪烁**来吸引配偶，而它们的信号却被**璀璨**的强光所淹没。为了了解全貌，**好奇**的科学家们开始从这场浩劫中**推断**出更广泛的规律。结果描绘了一幅严峻的图景：城市**闪耀**之处，**珍稀**物种便会消失；飞蛾**撞向**灯泡，直至精疲力竭，而**大蚊**则完全失去了方向。破坏不仅限于昆虫：路灯旁的树木过早**发芽**，**因此**与为其授粉的昆虫失去同步。研究人员**指出**，这种干扰难以量化，却不容忽视。

一**群\**\**专家**如今正在**敦促**采取行动，他们的研究发现**引起**了政策制定者的关注。关注已经有了——现在需要的是行动方案。有人**认为**对过度户外照明征收**税费**可以资助缓解措施；另一些人则建议**改用**琥珀色LED灯，因为**紫外线**频率被证明尤其有害；还有人推荐使用遮光灯具，将**光线**向下投射，而非射向天空。很多国家都在考虑采取行动——并且有的国家已经迈出了第一步。法国走在了前列：2022年，它成为第一个**禁止**店面通宵亮灯的国家。其他国家紧随其后：在大西洋彼岸，一个由美国**州长**组成的**联盟**承诺建立暗夜走廊。**热切**的**记者**们蜂拥而至，竞相报道这一运动，势头正在快速形成。**尽管如此**，并非所有人都支持：环保人士与开发商仍在**争执**，店主们**抗议**称，调暗招牌会严重损害他们的生意。

但在政策辩论之外，还有一个更深层的问题。**道德**层面的论据愈发充分——而且远不止关乎野生动物。具有生态**意识**的人深知威胁所在，但他们也看到了另一层面的危机：人类与夜空之间的**精神**纽带正在断裂。曾几何时，人们仰望星空，**欣赏**宇宙的浩瀚；如今，每一盏新亮起的灯，都在让这份联系悄然消逝。

这标志着保护工作本身的一个转折点。**传统**的保护工作聚焦于栖息地丧失和偷猎。光污染则不同——它是一个新兴的**前沿领域**，一个直到最近才进入公众讨论的议题。更重要的是，这种污染与化学污染有着本质的区别：化学泄漏会留下残留物，而光污染不会。**熄灭**一盏灯，黑暗便会立刻回归。正是这种可逆性，为治理光污染带来了希望——但前提是我们必须行动起来。现在的任务是将这一认知**融入**城市规划之中，**以免**便利凌驾于自然之上。

我们**珍视**什么，便会守护什么——而夜空正是值得我们守护的存在。黑暗中有无数生灵在**潜行**，夜幕下有无数生命在**巡游**；**为了**它们，我们必须**更新**对照明的认知。幸运的是，解决方案无需激进：一座**喧嚣**着车流的城市，不必像**火炬**一样彻夜通明。只要稍加克制，我们便能找回曾经失去的一切。夜晚的**灵魂**，值得被守护。
