**The Chemistry of Other Worlds**

------

For centuries, humanity has wondered: are we flying **solo** in the **galaxy**? Thanks to advances in **astronomy**, what was once a question for philosophers is now becoming a question for the laboratory.



Leading this scientific revolution is the James Webb Space **Telescope**, which has **practically** transformed the traditional and relatively outdated methods of searching for extraterrestrial life. Yet what underpins this revolution can be reduced to a simple **equation** in physics—absorption reveals presence. Each gas acts like a special filter, blocking only certain colors of light. When starlight passes through a planet's atmosphere, scientists simply need to use the Webb Telescope to see which colors have been blocked to determine what gases are present. By analyzing which colors are missing from the light, scientists can remotely **sample** alien gases and **vapour**—without traveling light-years to get there in person. This is a realm that earlier technology could not **perceive**. Now, for the first time, scientists can peer into the atmospheres of planets **revolving** around distant stars and reveal what lies beneath the surface—where life may be waiting to be found.



The raw data collected by the telescope is like an undeveloped photograph—the naked eye can make nothing of it. Astronomers must process this raw spectral data, much like developing a photograph in a darkroom—the image gradually emerges, and hidden patterns rise to the surface. When patterns emerge, some signals prove particularly exciting. The **occurrence** of molecules like methane, for instance—a gas produced primarily by living organisms on Earth—could be evidence of extraterrestrial life. But scientists do not jump to conclusions. To verify these signals, ground-based observatories **assist** with additional observations, keeping the global scientific community in the **loop** as findings are confirmed.



K2-18b offers an **apt** example of our progress and limitations in using the Webb Telescope to search for extraterrestrial life. This exoplanet, whose **radius** is 2.6 times Earth's, sits at the **threshold** where conditions **permit** liquid water. Its **interior** may harbor a vast ocean beneath a hydrogen-rich atmosphere. In September 2023, Webb detected carbon dioxide and methane there—and traces of dimethyl sulphide, produced **exclusively** on Earth by marine life, added to the **intrigue**. But discovery is one thing, and proof is another: such findings do not **yield** definitive proof, nor do they offer **certainty**. And here lies the rub: the signals detected by the telescope are extremely faint, while interference from across the cosmos is strong. Faint signals can easily be **dominated** by background noise, leading scientists to believe they have found something when it may be nothing but noise.



But no effort is in vain. Each discovery, however inconclusive, helps push the search forward. For this reason, scientists must be **conscientious** about accuracy, **striving** to scrutinize every detail rigorously—determining whether the **underlying** cause of a signal is life itself or merely noise. When ready to publish, they must **compress** years of painstaking research into concise papers before presenting the results to **peers**. After all, the **nerve** to claim evidence of extraterrestrial life demands both scientific rigor and sharp **wit**—the discernment to judge whether the evidence is reliable.



The **appeal** of this research is **overwhelming**—and it all stems from a single question: Is there other life in the universe? This is the **primordial** question humanity has asked since the dawn of civilization, and it remains the driving force behind this exploration. For this reason, governments around the world **subscribe** to the view that this question must be answered, and institutions **embrace** the search. The **sentiment** is nearly **unanimous**: whether from a scientific or historical perspective, finding an answer is a **necessity**.



Some people question whether the investment is **profitable**—that depends on how you measure returns. If measured by economic gain alone, it may not be worthwhile; but if measured by the value of unlocking the universe's ultimate mystery, it is priceless. Even when funding might **decline**, the quest will not stop, for many firmly believe this work is **worthy** of everything we can give. Consider this: if extraterrestrial life is confirmed, such a discovery would **burst** through the boundaries of science, with **consequences** that would **render** humanity's sense of **destiny** forever altered. Philosophy would have new questions, history would have new narratives, theology would have new interpretations—in short, the very fabric of human thought would be **moulded** anew. As for how society will **adapt** to all of this, no one can **foresee**.



Starlight was once wild as an untamed horse. Now we have learned to **tame** it, compelling it to reveal the secrets of the universe. We have touched **lunar** silence and listened to stars die. This is the **summit** of human reach—not an end, but a vantage point from which to continue exploring the cosmos. As we stand here, the **original** question echoes once more in our minds: Are we alone? In this vast universe, are we the only advanced civilization? Perhaps one day, we will traverse wormholes and set out on a journey to find another civilization.

几个世纪以来，人类一直在思考：我们是否在**银河系**中**独自**飞行？得益于**天文学**的进步，曾经属于哲学家的问题，如今正在成为实验室的课题。

引领这场科学革命的是詹姆斯·韦伯太空**望远镜**，它**几乎**彻底改变了传统且相对过时的地外生命搜寻方法。然而，这场革命的基础可以归结为一个简单的物理**方程**——吸收揭示存在。每种气体都像一个特殊的滤镜，只阻挡特定颜色的光。当星光穿过行星大气层时，科学家只需借助韦伯望远镜观察哪些颜色被阻挡，就能确定存在哪些气体。通过分析光线中缺失的颜色，科学家可以远程**采集**外星气体和**水蒸气**样本——而无需亲自跋涉数光年。这是早期技术无法**感知**的领域。如今，科学家首次能够窥探**围绕**遥远恒星**旋转**的行星大气层，揭示表面之下的秘密——那里或许正有生命等待被发现。

望远镜收集的原始数据就像一张未冲洗的照片——肉眼根本无法辨认。天文学家必须处理这些原始光谱数据，就像在暗房中冲洗照片——图像逐渐显现，隐藏的模式浮出水面。当模式显现时，某些信号尤其令人兴奋。例如，甲烷等分子的**出现**——这种气体在地球上主要由生物产生——可能就是地外生命存在的证据。但科学家不会贸然下结论。为了验证这些信号，地面观测站会**协助**进行额外观测，在发现得到确认的过程中让全球科学界保持**知情**。

K2-18b是一个**恰当**的例子，展示了我们利用韦伯望远镜搜寻地外生命的进展与局限。这颗系外行星的**半径**是地球的2.6倍，正处于**允许**液态水存在的**临界点**。它的**内部**可能在富氢大气层下蕴藏着一片广阔的海洋。2023年9月，韦伯望远镜在那里探测到了二氧化碳和甲烷——而二甲基硫的微量痕迹更增添了**神秘感**，因为这种物质在地球上**仅**由海洋生物产生。但发现是一回事，证明又是另一回事：这些发现并不能**给出**确凿的证据，也无法提供**确定性**。问题就在于此：望远镜探测到的信号极其微弱，而来自宇宙各处的干扰却很强。微弱的信号很容易被背景噪声所**淹没**，导致科学家误以为有所发现，实际上可能只是噪声而已。

但任何努力都不会白费。每一个发现，无论多么不确定，都能推动搜寻向前发展。因此，科学家必须对准确性保持**认真负责**的态度，**努力**严格审视每一个细节——判断信号的**根本**原因究竟是生命本身还是仅仅是噪声。当准备发表时，他们必须将多年艰辛的研究**压缩**成简洁的论文，然后才能向**同行**展示结果。毕竟，声称发现地外生命证据的**勇气**，既需要科学的严谨，也需要敏锐的**判断力**——辨别证据是否可靠的能力。

这项研究的**吸引力**是**巨大的**——而这一切都源于一个问题：宇宙中还有其他生命吗？这是人类自文明诞生以来就在追问的**原初**问题，也是这场探索背后的驱动力。正因如此，世界各国政府都**认同**这个问题必须得到解答，各机构也**积极投身**于这项搜寻。这种**共识**几乎是**一致的**：无论从科学还是历史角度来看，找到答案都是一种**必然**。

有人质疑这项投资是否**有利可图**——这取决于你如何衡量回报。如果仅以经济收益来衡量，或许并不值得；但如果以揭开宇宙终极奥秘的价值来衡量，则是无价的。即使资金可能**减少**，探索也不会停止，因为许多人坚信这项工作**值得**我们倾尽所有。试想：如果地外生命被证实，这一发现将**冲破**科学的边界，其**后果**将**使**人类的**命运**感永远改变。哲学将有新的问题，历史将有新的叙事，神学将有新的诠释——简而言之，人类思想的根基将被重新**塑造**。至于社会将如何**适应**这一切，没有人能够**预见**。
