**THE THIRST TO COME**

Fresh water is a **finite** resource—and it is running out. As populations grow and climate shifts, the **probability** of severe **drought** rises inexorably across much of the world. What was once a disputed **thesis** is now an **astonishing** reality: **consecutive** years of below-average rainfall have led to **disastrous** consequences. Rivers that once flowed strong now shrink to a **feeble** trickle, and groundwater levels **plunge** beyond recovery. Without water, the land falls silent: **wheat** cannot **sprout**, pastures turn barren, and farmers who **rear** cattle and **poultry** watch helplessly as their **stock** weakens and dies. But the suffering does not end with the farms. Across rural regions, **folk** **stumble** toward an uncertain future, their livelihoods crumbling as the crops wither. The **scope** of this crisis stretches far beyond any single village—it threatens the very foundation of how billions live.





What happens when the taps run dry? California found out between 2012 and 2016, when the state endured its worst **drought** in over a millennium. Under the relentless summer **blaze**, reservoirs fell to record lows, and residents faced a reality they had never imagined: water—the most basic necessity—could actually disappear. Facing a crisis of this magnitude, officials at the state's emergency **headquarters** imposed strict rationing and sent inspectors to **supervise** rationing efforts. For the first time, the **majority** of Californians felt true **stress**—the shock of watching their sprinklers fall silent, their lawns turn brown, their taps dribble to nothing. A **mere** handful of the wealthy could **boast** of being spared. Such realities were hard to **swallow**, but the threat left little choice. Most **individuals** and households had no choice but to endure the ordeal.



For years, people had been in **denial** about the limits of their water supply. Yet this cascade of crises finally shattered that illusion, awakening them to a sobering reality: freshwater scarcity could strike at any moment. To address this predicament, California bet on technological innovation. Engineers **recruited** to tackle the problem developed desalination technology that was both **economical** and efficient, capable of turning seawater into drinking water. This breakthrough significantly eased the pressure of public water shortages. **However**, technology alone was not enough. To prevent similar crises from recurring, California took an **uncompromising** stance: water use and distribution must be actively managed by the government to prevent waste and overconsumption. Although this position initially faced resistance from certain privileged groups, strong public support soon forced them to back down. Today, water policies across California **embody** this hard-won principle of conservation.

Even though California's experience offers a blueprint **applicable** to the world, many governments remain **passive** **bystanders**, unwilling to act until disaster strikes. They **reject** the evidence already before them, clinging to the belief that their own water supplies are inexhaustible. This denial exacts a brutal toll. Pipes crack, reservoirs drain, and infrastructure is left to **corrode** through years of neglect—until one day, the taps run dry. When that happens, it is the most vulnerable—the **elderly**, the poor, the forgotten—whose **welfare** is threatened first. To **avert** such disasters, action must **precede** crisis, not follow it. Those who fail to **comprehend** this simple truth will learn it the hard way, just as California once did.



But critics are quick to **stress**: California was only the beginning. Climate change is turning the planet into a **furnace**, and as temperatures rise, every **brook**, river, and aquifer faces mounting pressure. Throughout history, access to water has determined where humans **settle** and whether civilizations thrive or collapse. Yet in many parts of the world, a policy **vacuum** over water rights only invites **ruthless** exploitation—corporations and industries that **benefit** today while draining water resources meant for tomorrow. As supplies tighten, competition for water could **plunge** entire regions into chaos—but fortunately, **diverse** solutions are already emerging: smarter technology, stricter regulation that **bans** wasteful practices, and a growing public will for change. **Poll** after **poll** shows that citizens understand the stakes. The only question is whether the authorities will take action before it is too late.
