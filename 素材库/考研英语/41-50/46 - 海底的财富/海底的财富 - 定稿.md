On the other, critics counter that the ocean floor, though **uniform** in appearance, teems with life we barely understand—and if mining disturbs these ecosystems, no **guarantee** exists that they will ever recover. In **retrospect**, **mankind**'s **mere** presence has often scarred the environments it enters, and the deep sea is unlikely to be an exception. With **viewpoints** so sharply divided, some propose middle-ground solutions, but these turn out to be **pedestrian**—the same recycled ideas that have failed elsewhere. Yet beneath all this political noise lies a truth no one can escape. A planet **reeling** from climate change desperately needs cobalt, nickel, and manganese to **convert** to clean energy. **Rip** these metals from the seabed too hastily, however, and we risk destroying ecosystems we have only just begun to discover.
