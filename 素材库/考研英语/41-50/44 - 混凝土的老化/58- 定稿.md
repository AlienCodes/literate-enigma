**CONCRETE'S SLOW COLLAPSE**

Reinforced concrete—the backbone of modern construction—is **degenerating** faster than engineers ever expected. The danger lurks everywhere: beneath every **overpass**, inside every aging **gymnasium**, within the walls of residential towers. When these structures fail, the consequences can be catastrophic. In 2021, Miami's Champlain Towers **crashed** down into rubble without warning, killing 98 people as they slept in luxury **suites** above. Investigations revealed a slow disaster decades in the making. Over time, moisture had **dripped** through **slits** in the **waterproof** membrane, seeping down to the steel reinforcement below. Salt **lodged** in the concrete accelerated corrosion, and supporting **ribs** weakened year after year. The structure **limped** silently toward collapse—and no one noticed until it was too late.

The chemistry is straightforward. Concrete contains compounds that remain **soluble** in water, which carries chlorides and carbon dioxide deep into its structure. Once inside, these compounds attack the steel reinforcement. As the steel rusts, it expands, creating **wrinkles** and cracks that **peel** away protective layers. These cracks, in turn, allow more water to seep in—and each **quart** of infiltrating water worsens the damage. Engineers call this vicious cycle "concrete cancer."

This cancer has become an epidemic—its symptoms now visible worldwide. Across **Commonwealth countries** and beyond, planners are **hound**ed by the same problem: aging infrastructure crumbling from within. **Stadiums** built decades ago now show **razor**-thin cracks along load-bearing **brackets**—hairline fractures that hint at deeper decay. The warning signs are clear, yet action remains slow. **Anonymous** inspectors do what they can, **rifling** through maintenance records and **despatch**ing warnings to officials, but they receive only perfunctory **replies**—or none at all. In one case, a **deputy** **mayor** recently **vetoed** mandatory assessments under the **pretext** of budget constraints—sheer **hypocrisy** from officials who **insure** their own homes while leaving the public exposed.

Economic pressures compound matters. **Layoffs** and rising **unemployment** have shrunk the workforce—fewer **plumber**s and construction workers remain to do essential work. **Mortgage** holders, already stretched thin, cannot afford repairs. Instead, owners treat the symptoms, not the disease. Their **bandages** cover the cracks, not the cause—fresh **plaster**, new **lumber** for trim—while structural decay continues beneath the surface.

But such measures are nothing but self-deception. Some buildings are so weak that a violent **sneeze** might shake loose ceiling material; a **slamming** door causes visible vibration. Trapped in deteriorating homes they cannot sell and dare not leave, residents are effectively **hostages**, unwilling to **evacuate** yet unable to stay safely.

And nature's fury only compounds the crisis. **Typhoons** and flooding accelerate decay, turning grim situations into full-blown emergencies. After storms, water fills **compartments** to the **brim**, **gumming** up drainage systems and **flinging** debris into gaps and crevices. In the aftermath, engineers **tramp** through flooded basements, **zipping** between **intersections**—where columns meet beams—to check whether these critical joints have been compromised. The damage they document will cost a fortune to repair—and the scale of the problem is **dizzying**. Crumbling buildings, shrinking budgets, and political inaction **knit** together into a crisis that no one seems able to fix.

Yet through it all, the warnings go unheeded. **Pamphlets** warning of these dangers lie unread, forgotten on dusty shelves in neglected corners. However, the concrete does not wait—it cracks, it crumbles, it falls. And when it does, it buries not just buildings, but our excuses along with them.
