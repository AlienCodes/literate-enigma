# Bridging the Organ Gap

The **customary** practice of relying on organ donation suffers from a **significant** **drawback**: demand vastly exceeds supply. In the United States alone, over 100,000 people await organs, yet donors are **conspicuous** by their absence—fewer than 40,000 transplants occur annually. This gap has long **frustrated** doctors and patients alike; every year, thousands with **terminal** **kidney** disease die waiting. Such preventable deaths have fueled widespread **criticism** of the current system and left many feeling **obliged** to demand change. In response, scientists have made finding new organ sources their top **priority**—a **goal** that recent breakthroughs suggest can finally be achieved.




**Ingenious** solutions are emerging on two fronts. The first is xenotransplantation. At the University of Maryland, surgeons **initiated** a groundbreaking trial in 2022, transplanting a pig heart into a human patient. The organ had been genetically modified to **combat** rejection. Though the patient survived only two months, the **exceptional** result **furnished** proof that animal organs could function in human bodies. The second front is **synthetic** organs. Researchers use 3D printers to build **miniature** organ scaffolds, seeding them with human cells. To function properly, their **composition** must match that of natural tissue—remaining **moist** and replicating the **consistency** of **muscular** tissue. The **machinery** required to achieve this is complex, but as the technology advances, the **efficiency**, reliability, and precision of bioprinting all improve each year, leading some **influential** scientists to **state** that printed kidneys could enter trials within a decade. For now, **affluent** nations lead this research, but the **intention** behind it is far broader: to make these breakthroughs accessible to all.

Public **suspicion** is no mere **nuisance**. **Stereotype**s about animal organs—though at times **excessive**—reflect genuine ethical concerns. Regulatory **administration** is **stiff**, which will **necessitate** years of testing. **Elementary** concerns about safety **complicate** approval. Some worry that treatments could **injure** patients; **sovereign** governments may be **reluctant** to approve such procedures.

Yet few truly **oppose** this research. Patients are keenly **aware** that these advances **imply** hope. The **representative** case studies so far **remind** us that **lateral** thinking can **spin** medicine forward. The **casual** observer might still struggle to **distinguish** such therapies from science fiction, but that **illusion** is fading fast. In the end, medicine's greatest **bargain** may be this: a future where no one dies waiting for an organ that science could have built.

# 弥合器官鸿沟

每年，数以千计的**终末期** **肾脏**病患者在等待移植中离世。科学家的**目标**很明确：寻找新的器官来源。近期的突破表明，这一**优先要务**终于触手可及。

移植的**常规** **途径**有一个**明显的** **缺陷**：需求远大于供应。在美国，超过十万人等待器官；而每年进行的移植手术不足四万例。这一缺口长期**困扰着**医生和患者。许多人感到有**责任**发声；对当前系统的**批评**广泛存在。

**巧妙的**解决方案正在涌现。2022年，马里兰大学的外科医生**启动**了一项具有里程碑意义的试验：将一颗猪心脏移植给一名人类患者。该器官经过了基因编辑以**对抗**排斥反应。尽管患者仅存活了两个月，但这次实验**提供了** **非凡的**证据，表明异种移植——使用动物器官——是可行的。

**人造的**替代方案也展现出前景。研究人员使用3D打印机建造**微型**器官支架，并在其上植入人类细胞。其**构成**必须与天然组织匹配：**湿润**，具有**肌肉**般的**质地**。所需的**设备**很复杂，但**效率**逐年提升。一些**有影响力的**科学家**声称**，打印肾脏可能在十年内进入临床试验。**富裕的**国家主导着这项研究，但最终的**意图**是全球共享。

公众的**疑虑**并非单纯的**麻烦**。关于动物器官的**刻板印象**——尽管有时**过度**——反映了真实的伦理关切。监管**机构**的审查**严格**，这将**需要**多年的测试。关于安全性的**基本**考量使批准过程**复杂化**。一些人担心治疗可能**伤害**患者；**主权**政府可能**不愿**批准此类手术。

然而，很少有人真正**反对**这项研究。患者敏锐地**意识到**，这些进展**意味着**希望。迄今为止的**代表性**案例**提醒**我们，**横向**思维能够**推动**医学向前发展。**不经意的**观察者或许仍难以**区分**此类疗法与科幻小说，但这种**错觉**正在迅速消逝。最终，医学所能给予的最大**馈赠**或许正是：一个不再有人因等待科学本可造出的器官而逝去的未来。
