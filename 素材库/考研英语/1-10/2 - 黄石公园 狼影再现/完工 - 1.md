第一段

For decades, the gray **wolf** had been gone from Yellowstone.  
Without **predator**s, this land became a paradise for elk.  
But **with it came a cost**: the park's ecosystem fell into **disorder**.  
The elk **population** exploded, the **surplus** soon straining the ecosystem.
They **gnawed at** the **bark** **without restraint**, leaving trunks bare.  
As a result, many trees died.  
Deprived of shade, the **moss** around the trees slowly **wither**ed and vanished under the **scorching sun**.  
The damage these **herbivores** caused to the ecosystem was like a deep, gaping **wound** that would not **heal**.

### 第二段
To **prevent** the situation **from** getting worse, in 1995, officials decided to **reintroduce** wolves.  
Upon hearing the news, **rancher**s were **indignant**.  
They **were skeptical about** whether this decision would actually be **carried out**.  
**To head this off**, ranchers **banded together**, **objecting** to this plan and demanding **hearings**.  
They claimed this was an **insult** to their way of life, as wolves would **prey on** their **livestock**.  
If wolves returned, the constant struggle to **resist** these **vicious** predators would **torment** them **endlessly**.  
However, ecologists **took the opposite view**.  
They **cared little for** how the **shepherds** felt; all they wanted was for Yellowstone to **revive**.

### 第三段
The plan that many had deemed **dubious** went ahead as scheduled, yet the results far exceeded **estimates**.  
Elk no longer **dare**d **to** **roam** near the open **ridges**, for **cunning** gray wolves now **lurk**ed there, ready to **pursue** them at any moment.  
**Free from** relentless grazing, greenery began to return, its rapid **expansion** soon transforming the entire **panorama**.  Where elk had left the land **desolate**, new life now **stretch**ed across it once more—**lush** and **thriving**.
In a Yellowstone reborn, beavers also **made their way back**—to **swamp** and stream.

### 第四段
Yellowstone's **rebirth** brought unexpected **clarity** on how to **maintain ecological balance** in the future. The key lay not in killing, but in fear. Gray wolves do more than just kill elk and control their numbers; their fierce **disposition** **instills** lasting fear in their **prey**. As a result, elk now **wander** within a limited range, doing everything they can to avoid places where wolves **howl**. This greatly shrinks the area damaged by elk **grazing**, giving other areas time to recover. It is this behavioral shift that **underlies** much of the **restoration**.

### 第五段


------

As conservation efforts proved a **resounding** success, Yellowstone **came back to life**. Visitors arrived in growing numbers, **hiking** along the valley **rim** **at their leisure**, hoping to **catch a glimpse of** the **elusive** gray wolves. Local tourism operators **scored** big—**abundant** revenue **poured** **in**, giving the economy a significant boost. Ranchers, however, were **up in arms**. As wolf populations grew, so did livestock kills. Some ranchers began organizing hunts on their own. To **defuse** the **rising tensions**, authorities were forced to introduce a **compensation** policy: ranchers who lost livestock could now **file claims**. This **concession** helped **soften** the opposition.

------



### 第六段
Other regions now **pursue** similar outcomes. In Germany, wolves have crossed the **border** from Poland—and the boost to ecosystem recovery is already visible. Farmers, though, have **groaned** over their **mounting** losses and **pointed** **fingers** **at** government policy. In response, **Parliament** drew up **clauses** to safeguard their interests and property. The **entity** responsible for overseeing wildlife **conceded** limited culls—but kept core populations **intact**.

### 第七段
Ecologists **counsel** patience.  
**After all**, ecosystem recovery is not **instantaneous**—it takes decades to restore the **delicate bonds** between species.  
Yet for all this complexity, Yellowstone offers a **concise** lesson: remove a keystone species, the balance collapses; **revive** it, nature **heal**s itself.
