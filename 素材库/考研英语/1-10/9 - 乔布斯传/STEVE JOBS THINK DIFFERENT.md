**STEVE JOBS: THINK DIFFERENT**

In 1976, a young **rebel** in California founded Apple Computer. Steve Jobs had a singular **aesthetic** vision: technology should be beautiful. While others saw machines as tools, he saw them as art. This **imaginative** approach would **distill** complexity into elegant simplicity and reshape the world.

Apple's **initial** success established Jobs' **authority** in Silicon Valley. Yet his **arrogant** manner and **furious** **rage** alienated colleagues. When the board **panicked** over declining sales, Jobs was **shoved** aside from his own company—stripped of his **throne** by the very people he had hired.

**Meanwhile**, Apple floundered. **Clone** manufacturers offered cheaper alternatives. The company teetered on the **verge** of going **bankrupt**, its products now **obsolete**. Corporate **inertia** set in. **Due** to poor leadership, the **mess** deepened.

Jobs was not **discouraged**. He founded NeXT and purchased a small animation **studio** that would become Pixar. He learned to **meditate**, seeking calm amid the **hurricane** of his ambitions. He resisted the **temptation** to look back. When Pixar released Toy Story—a **smash** hit—industry rivals could only **envy** his comeback.

In 1997, Apple bought NeXT, and Jobs returned to a company **barely** alive. With ferocious energy, he **renewed** its purpose, eliminated **excess**, and **maintained** relentless standards. He could **gauge** what consumers wanted before they knew themselves. **Associates** who couldn't meet his vision were quickly dismissed. Material **possessions** meant nothing; only the work mattered.

The **harvest** was extraordinary. The iPod. The iPhone. Each launch became a cultural **ritual**, with Jobs delivering **elaborate** presentations in his **trademark** black turtleneck—a **pirate** flag flying over convention. He set **precedents** that made competitors' products **obsolete** overnight. **Favorable** reviews poured in.

In 2003, doctors **diagnosed** him with cancer. He finally **resigned** in 2011, passing **authority** to his chosen **heir**. He died weeks later.

Jobs could **scold** employees harshly, **interrupt** without apology, and be stunningly **rude**. He **exhausted** himself in pursuit of perfection. Yet millions **worship** his legacy. He taught humanity that technology and art could **weave** together—and that defying the **norm** was the highest calling of all.

**史蒂夫·乔布斯：不同凡想**

1976年，加利福尼亚州的一位年轻**反叛者**创立了苹果电脑公司。史蒂夫·乔布斯拥有一种独特的**美学**愿景：科技产品应该是美丽的。当其他人将机器视为工具时，他却视其为艺术。这种**富有想象力的**方式将**提炼**复杂性，成就优雅的简约，并重塑世界。

苹果的**初期**成功确立了乔布斯在硅谷的**权威**。然而，他**傲慢**的作风和**狂怒**的**发作**疏远了同事。当董事会因销售额下滑而**惊慌失措**时，乔布斯被从他自己的公司**排挤**了出去——被他亲自聘请的人赶下了自己的**宝座**。

**与此同时**，苹果步履维艰。**兼容机**制造商提供了更便宜的替代品。公司濒临**破产**的**边缘**，其产品变得**过时**。企业**惰性**开始滋生。**由于**糟糕的领导，**混乱**的局面进一步加深。

乔布斯并未**气馁**。他创立了NeXT，并收购了一家后来成为皮克斯的小型动画**工作室**。他学会了**冥想**，在他雄心壮志的**飓风**中寻求平静。他抵制住了**回首过往的**诱惑**。当皮克斯推出《玩具总动员》这部**轰动**巨作时，行业对手只能**艳羡**他的东山再起。

1997年，苹果收购了NeXT，乔布斯回归时，公司**仅存**一息。凭借惊人的能量，他**重振**了公司的目标，消除了**冗余**，并**维持**着苛刻的标准。他能够**揣度**出消费者自己都尚未意识到的需求。无法跟上他愿景的**同事**很快便被解雇。物质**财产**毫无意义；唯有工作本身才是重要的。

**收获**是惊人的。iPod。iPhone。每一次产品发布都成为一场文化**盛典**，乔布斯身着他**标志性的**黑色高领衫进行**精心准备的**演讲——犹如一面在传统上空飘扬的**海盗**旗。他创下的**先例**让竞争对手的产品在一夜之间变得**过时**。**好评**如潮。

2003年，医生**诊断**他患有癌症。他最终于2011年**辞职**，将**权力**移交给他选定的**继承人**。数周后，他与世长辞。

乔布斯会严厉地**斥责**员工，毫无歉意地**打断**他人，并且可能极其**粗鲁**。他为了追求完美而**耗尽**了自己。然而，数百万人**崇拜**他的遗产。他教导人类，科技与艺术可以**交织**在一起——而**挑战常规**是最高尚的使命。
