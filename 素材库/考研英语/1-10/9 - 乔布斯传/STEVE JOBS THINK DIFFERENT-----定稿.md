**STEVE JOBS: THINK DIFFERENT**

In 1976, a young **rebel** in California founded Apple Computer. That rebel was none other than the legendary Steve Jobs. He had a singular **aesthetic** vision: technology should be beautiful. While others saw machines as mere tools, he saw them as art. He was like a great artist, pursuing absolute perfection in design—tolerating neither redundancy nor flaw. This **imaginative** approach would **distill** complexity into elegant simplicity—and ultimately reshape the world.

In time, the vision paid off. Apple's **initial** success established Jobs' **authority** in Silicon Valley. But with power came hubris. His **arrogant** manner and **furious** temper—prone to sudden **rage**—proved unbearable. Colleagues kept their distance, and his relationships with those around him grew increasingly strained. So when a new product line stumbled and sales began to slide, few rose to defend him. Instead, they seized the opportunity to band together and **shove** him aside, forcing him out. Jobs **panicked**, but it was too late—he was stripped of his **throne** and had no choice but to leave the very company he had founded.

Jobs was not **discouraged**. He founded NeXT and purchased a small animation **studio** that would later become the iconic Pixar. During these years in exile, he learned to **meditate**, seeking calm amid the **hurricane** of his ambitions. He resisted the **temptation** to look back, channeling his time and energy into the art of animation. The gamble paid off. In 1995, Pixar released Toy Story—the world's first fully computer-animated feature film and a **smash** hit that redefined the animation industry. In the face of such a groundbreaking achievement, rivals could only watch from afar, left with nothing but **envy**.

**Meanwhile**, for Apple, the picture was bleak. Ever since Jobs left, the company had drifted like a ship without a captain—directionless and sliding into decline. Without his relentless innovation, the company failed to produce anything that could restore its competitive edge, still peddling products that had long become **outdated**. Even **clone** manufacturers could now rival Apple, offering cheaper alternatives and luring customers away in droves. Leadership lost all sense of direction and corporate **inertia** set in—the **mess** only worsened. **Due** to prolonged weak sales, a cash flow crisis loomed, and the company now teetered on the **verge** of going **bankrupt**.

By 1997, Apple's desperation had reached its peak. To bring Jobs back, the company bought NeXT—and with it, Jobs returned. He came back to the very company he had founded, now **barely** alive, determined to pull it back from the brink. He moved swiftly and decisively: he eliminated **excess**, **renewed** its purpose, and **maintained** relentless standards. His intuition was razor-sharp—he could **gauge** what consumers wanted before they knew themselves. He demanded the same brilliance from his team. **Associates** who failed to meet his demanding expectations were quickly dismissed; there was no room for mediocrity in his world. For Jobs, material **possessions** meant nothing; only the work mattered.

His return yielded a phenomenal **harvest**. The iPod. The iPhone. Each launch became a cultural **ritual**, with Jobs delivering **elaborate** presentations in his **trademark** black turtleneck—a **pirate** ready to conquer the ocean of technology and claim all the market share as his own. Every product he launched set new **precedents**, making competitors' offerings **obsolete** overnight. **Favorable** reviews poured in. Consumers lined up for hours, even days, and Apple's latest products flew off the shelves.

Yet fate is fickle. In 2003, doctors **diagnosed** him with cancer. Even so, he refused to slow down, continuing to innovate year after year. In 2011, his body finally failing, he **resigned** and passed **authority** to his chosen **heir**. Weeks later, he was gone.

Jobs was far from perfect—and he knew it. He could **scold** employees harshly, **interrupt** without apology, and be stunningly **rude**. He **exhausted** himself in pursuit of perfection, driving others just as hard as he drove himself. Yet despite his flaws, countless people still **worship** him. He taught humanity that technology and art could be **woven** together—and those who dare to defy the **norm** are the ones who change the world.

**史蒂夫·乔布斯：不同凡想**

1976年，加利福尼亚州的一位年轻**反叛者**创立了苹果电脑公司。史蒂夫·乔布斯拥有一种独特的**美学**愿景：科技产品应该是美丽的。当其他人将机器视为工具时，他却视其为艺术。这种**富有想象力的**方式将**提炼**复杂性，成就优雅的简约，并重塑世界。

苹果的**初期**成功确立了乔布斯在硅谷的**权威**。然而，他**傲慢**的作风和**狂怒**的**发作**疏远了同事。当董事会因销售额下滑而**惊慌失措**时，乔布斯被从他自己的公司**排挤**了出去——被他亲自聘请的人赶下了自己的**宝座**。

**与此同时**，苹果步履维艰。**兼容机**制造商提供了更便宜的替代品。公司濒临**破产**的**边缘**，其产品变得**过时**。企业**惰性**开始滋生。**由于**糟糕的领导，**混乱**的局面进一步加深。

乔布斯并未**气馁**。他创立了NeXT，并收购了一家后来成为皮克斯的小型动画**工作室**。他学会了**冥想**，在他雄心壮志的**飓风**中寻求平静。他抵制住了**回首过往的**诱惑**。当皮克斯推出《玩具总动员》这部**轰动**巨作时，行业对手只能**艳羡**他的东山再起。

1997年，苹果收购了NeXT，乔布斯回归时，公司**仅存**一息。凭借惊人的能量，他**重振**了公司的目标，消除了**冗余**，并**维持**着苛刻的标准。他能够**揣度**出消费者自己都尚未意识到的需求。无法跟上他愿景的**同事**很快便被解雇。物质**财产**毫无意义；唯有工作本身才是重要的。

**收获**是惊人的。iPod。iPhone。每一次产品发布都成为一场文化**盛典**，乔布斯身着他**标志性的**黑色高领衫进行**精心准备的**演讲——犹如一面在传统上空飘扬的**海盗**旗。他创下的**先例**让竞争对手的产品在一夜之间变得**过时**。**好评**如潮。

2003年，医生**诊断**他患有癌症。他最终于2011年**辞职**，将**权力**移交给他选定的**继承人**。数周后，他与世长辞。

乔布斯会严厉地**斥责**员工，毫无歉意地**打断**他人，并且可能极其**粗鲁**。他为了追求完美而**耗尽**了自己。然而，数百万人**崇拜**他的遗产。他教导人类，科技与艺术可以**交织**在一起——而**挑战常规**是最高尚的使命。
