**The Humble Healer: How Viruses May Save Us from the Antibiotic Crisis**

------





**第一段**

In that **era** without antibiotics, the **bacterium** was a **deadly foe**—even minor infections could **readily** kill. 在那个没有抗生素的**时代**，**细菌**是 **致命的敌人**——即使是轻微的感染也能**轻易**夺人性命。

However, the discovery of penicillin seemed to **eclipse** this **menace** forever—infections that once killed could now be cured with ease. 然而，青霉素的发现似乎 **让** 这一**威胁**永远**黯然失色**——曾经致命的感染如今可以轻松治愈。

With this newfound weapon in hand, hospitals began prescribing **antibiotics** for any **ailment** linked to bacterial infection—even when **unnecessary**. 有了这一新的武器，医院开始为任何与细菌感染相关的**疾病**开具**抗生素**处方——即使在**没有必要的**情况下也是如此。

This **overuse** drove bacteria to develop **ever-stronger** resistance. 这种**滥用**促使细菌产生了**越来越强的**耐药性。

Today, resistant strains **abound**, and health **ministers** warn of a **potential** **crisis** that could **cripple** modern medicine. 如今，耐药菌株**大量存在**，卫生**部长**们警告说，一场 **潜在的 危机** 可能会 **严重削弱** 现代医学。

------

**第二段**

Facing this threat, scientists are **turning** **to** an **unexpected** **ally**: the **virus**. Specifically, they are harnessing bacteriophages—viruses that **infect** and destroy bacteria. These microscopic hunters exist in **abundance**, found everywhere from soil to **sewage**. Researchers **extract** them from such sources and **cultivate** them in **laboratories**, preparing them for medical use.

------

------

**第三段**

A **singular** case at the University of California, San Diego, offered a **glimpse** of phage therapy's promise. 加州大学圣地亚哥分校的一个 **独特的** 病例，让人们得以**一窥**噬菌体疗法的前景。

In 2016, Tom Patterson **lay dying**, his **tissue** ravaged by **drug-resistant** Acinetobacter. 2016年，汤姆·帕特森**奄奄一息**，他的**组织**被 **耐药的** 鲍曼不动杆菌严重侵蚀。

Doctors tried one standard treatment after another—all proved **futile**. 医生们尝试了一种又一种的常规治疗——全都**徒劳**无功。

As a final **gamble**, they decided to **administer** an experimental phage cocktail. 作为最后的**赌注**，他们决定**施用**一种实验性的噬菌体 **鸡尾酒** 疗法。

What happened next **defied all expectations**: a **succession** of doses **coincided** with rapid improvement. 接下来发生的事 **出乎了所有人的预料**：**接连**几剂的用药与病情的快速好转**同时出现**。

Eventually, Patterson made a **miraculous** recovery—his family **rejoiced**. 最终，帕特森 **奇迹般的** 康复了——他的家人**欣喜若狂**。

Word of his survival soon spread, and the case **stirred** **enduring** interest among **journalists**, whose coverage generated considerable **publicity**.

他生还的消息很快传开，这一病例在**记者**中**激起**了**持久的**兴趣，他们的报道引发了相当大的**关注**。



**第四段**

Many **wondered**: how had phages saved Patterson, a patient once **deemed** beyond hope? The **recipe** for success **lies in** precision—and phages deliver exactly that. They can be **tailored** to target specific bacteria while leaving healthy cells in **harmony**, offering a **mutual** guarantee of precision and safety. Unlike their antibiotic **predecessors**, phages do not **saturate** the body with **broad-spectrum drugs**—they strike only where needed. For this reason, the approach has drawn **widespread attention** and praise, **point**ing **toward** a future where medicine fights with precision rather than **brute force**.

------

**第五段**

Although this **treatment approach** shows great promise, challenges **remain**. 尽管这种 **治疗方法** 很有前景，但挑战**依然存在**。

**Legislation** regarding such treatments is **nonexistent**, and reliable **supply chain**s are nowhere to be found. 关于此类治疗的**立法** **根本不存在**，可靠的 **供应链** 也无处可寻。

Even practitioners within the medical **profession** urge **caution**, demanding repeated rigorous trials to ensure **reliability** and safety. 即使是医学**界**的**从业者**也敦促保持**谨慎**，要求反复进行严格的试验来确保**可靠性**和安全性。

The reason is clear: any **breakdown** during treatment could **expose** patients to serious **complications**—not only failing to cure, but actually **worsening** the patient's condition. 原因很清楚：治疗过程中的任何**故障**都可能 **使** 患者 **面临** 严重的**并发症**——不仅起不到治疗作用，反而会**加重**患者的病情。

------

**第六段**

Yet none of this has halted the **earnest** research now **underway**. Researchers are working to **define** protocols, **outline** best practices, and **lay the groundwork for** wider adoption. Already, their efforts are bearing fruit: **clinical trials** are now **operational** in Belgium and Georgia, with more **on the horizon**. After decades of neglect, the **humble** phage may yet **repay** humanity's attention—and **rewrite** the future of medicine.
