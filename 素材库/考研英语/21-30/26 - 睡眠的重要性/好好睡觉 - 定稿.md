Why Do We Sleep?

Sleep is not a lifestyle choice—something we can take or leave at will. We have no such freedom, for it is an **inherent** biological **process**, as essential as breathing itself. Yet modern society treats sleep as negotiable, something to sacrifice for work or study. The evidence is stark: statistics reveal that the **proportion** of people who sleep less than seven hours a night is growing steadily, as they are occupied with the countless tasks of everyday life. Many of them rely on caffeine to stay alert through work and study, but no stimulant can **cancel** out the accumulated debt of chronic sleep loss. Experts who have long devoted themselves to sleep science **claim** that this **systematic** erosion of rest leads to serious health risks and hidden dangers, and can even dramatically increase the likelihood of sudden death.







In Japan, the problem is acute. Office workers bear the brunt of it. They **commute** for hours and **grind** through grueling shifts, leaving little time for rest. At night, they **toss** and turn, their sleep shallow and repeatedly broken by **abrupt** awakenings. Deprived of adequate sleep, some can only **snatch** brief **naps** on the subway to relieve their fatigue. **Physicians** warn that such chronic exhaustion **wrecks** the body's **constitution**. The brain's sleep **circuits**, which **anchor** the body's daily rhythms, are hardwired for precision—but even so, they buckle under chronic sleep disruption. Melatonin is **suppressed**; **spontaneous** waking grows ever more frequent. Each morning, those tired workers **stagger** into offices with **sore** eyes and **numb** minds.





Studies **demonstrate** that sleep loss has profoundly **destructive** effects. Cognition **dips**; reflexes dull; and chronic fatigue can **irritate** the nerves, leaving workers **impatient** and prone to costly mistakes. The toll of sleep deprivation is backed by clear data: workplace accident rates spike during **odd** hours—late nights and early mornings—when people should be asleep, fatigue peaks, and alertness is at its lowest. A RAND report estimated that Japan alone loses two percent of its GDP to sleeplessness—losses that are **universal** across industrial nations, with Japan serving as but one stark **reflection** of a global pattern.





**Mobile** phones share much of the blame. Their powerful features **breach** the darkness that **preceding** generations knew, disrupting natural sleep rhythms and luring countless people into staying up late, endlessly scrolling on their phones. They **indulge** in this **stubborn** habit night after night, then wake **hollow**-eyed, drained before the day begins. Meanwhile, **flexible** work schedules blur the boundary between working hours and time for rest. Emails and work messages arrive at **arbitrary** hours, and the **tick** of notifications sounds from time to time, snapping minds that should be at rest suddenly **alert**. Experts urge the public to **clarify** what is at stake: sleep **poverty** is by no means something to boast about. **Humanity** must recognize the importance of rest—it affects every aspect of our lives. Crushing fatigue robs people of interest and **satisfaction** in everything they do, and chronic sleep deprivation can inflict **perpetual** damage on the body, condemning its victims to **bear** immense suffering



More and more companies are beginning to recognize the importance of employees getting adequate sleep. Companies with a **conscience** and a humane approach now set up nap rooms, granting every employee **access** to them. Their reasoning is sound: science shows that sleep deprivation severely reduces productivity and impairs work performance. And the body itself offers constant reminders. The **outward** signs are unmistakable: dark circles under the eyes, sluggish movements, and a vacant stare. When the body sends signals demanding rest—a **yawn**, mounting fatigue—the best course of action is to rest immediately. Push the body too hard, and it will **revolt**: concentration scatters, emotions spiral out of control, and illness follows. As the old saying goes: those who cannot rest cannot work. If you drag yourself through each day half-asleep, you can only shamble along like a zombie—a soulless walking corpse.
