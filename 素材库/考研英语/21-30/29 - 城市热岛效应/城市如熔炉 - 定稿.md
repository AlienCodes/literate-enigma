CITIES AS FURNACES

Each summer, **thermometer** readings reveal a stark divide: major cities now run up to 10°C hotter than the surrounding countryside. This "heat island" effect has reached **epidemic** proportions across the world's urban centers. This punishing heat inflicts severe **physiological** stress on city dwellers—stress that proves lethal, claiming thousands of lives each year.


Why has it come to this? Scientists place **emphasis** on a crucial point: this crisis is no accident—it is the result of **deliberate** human choices. As cities **industrialized**, green spaces were **abandoned**, and **vertical** walls of concrete rose up, leaving no room for cooling breezes to penetrate this jungle of cement. It is precisely these choices that have shaped the urban landscape we see today: **tar**-paved road **pavement** and dark rooftops, like giant heat-absorbing panels, greedily devour sunlight, and the enormous heat they absorb takes a long, long time to **emit** after sunset. Today, the **range** of this problem spans the globe—yet the suffering it brings is not shared equally. In **slum** neighbourhoods lacking shade and cooling infrastructure, residents endure a dangerous daily **dose** of heat; under the same blazing sun, wealthy districts **flourish** as before while poorer areas quietly **perish**. This **overlap** between heat vulnerability and poverty is no coincidence—it reflects decades of urban planning that **discriminated** against the poorest.





Amid this crisis, Phoenix, Arizona, offers a **promising** and instructive case study in combating urban heat. In this city, deadly heat waves have become a **regular** summer occurrence, claiming hundreds of lives each year. Determined to break this cycle, officials **crafted** a **formal** document detailing a comprehensive heat action plan. Its goals were clearly **articulate**d: plant trees in every **yard**, mandate reflective roofing, and redesign streets to **ventilate** trapped heat. A city **spokesman** recently announced that progress has been measurable, and the **tempo** of implementation continues to accelerate. Yet **controversy** has never ceased. Critics dismiss the plan as a **sham**, arguing that incremental steps cannot substitute for systemic transformation. This criticism has spilled into the political arena, where **Democratic** and Republican leaders alike trade blame with little **restraint**, each side pointing fingers at the other as the **guilty** party. While the two parties are busy trading blame, real action has stalled: city councils **queue** for limited federal funding, while desperate residents **petition** for immediate relief. Yet what is more frightening than the current predicament is the future that awaits: climate projections foretell a future of such **horror** that many can only **exclaim** in disbelief—fearing that today's deadly summers may soon seem mild by comparison.



Addressing this crisis demands **expertise** that spans multiple fields—yet each field has a **curriculum** isolated from the rest, producing specialists who rarely collaborate. It is precisely this fragmentation that leaves cities helpless in the face of heat—and bearing the cost are countless ordinary people. Heat **refugees** are emerging, some fleeing scorching urban **settings** for cooler regions, while others cannot **evade** the cities they must **inhabit** for work, trapped in deadly environments with no way out. To change all this, cities must **seize** any opportunity they can and adopt every **intelligent** and effective strategy available: reflective roofs, strategic planting, crews ready to **hose** down streets during emergencies, and more. Yet whether all this can take root depends on a fundamental shift in mindset—heat must be treated as a core **component** of urban risk, not an afterthought. The **characteristic** official rhetoric, the sanctimonious excuses, the indifference to the suffering of ordinary people—these practices of urban planners have long sparked public outrage. No city-dweller should become a casualty of negligence; they and their **offspring** deserve a safer, more liveable future. Yet to achieve that future, technology alone is not enough—placing complete **faith** in technology is the ultimate self-deception. The only way out is action—coordinated, pragmatic, and unwavering action.



Unlike storms, heat carries no warning—it kills in whispers while cities **tidy** their streets and polish their images. To **underestimate** this silent executioner is to gamble with millions of lives. We stand at a crossroads: liveable cities, or graveyards of concrete. We must choose, and choose soon, for the next summer is already on its way.
