# EN: The Prize That Looked Away

On 29 May 1919, as a total **eclipse** **obscured** the sun, two British **expeditions** photographed stars in its **vicinity**. Arthur Eddington **observed** from Príncipe, an island off West Africa; Andrew Crommelin and Charles Davidson **mounted** **telescopes** at Sobral, in north-eastern Brazil. The **exposures** would **indicate** whether the sun's **gravity** **deflected** starlight.

On 6 November, in London, a **joint** meeting of the Royal Society, Britain's national academy of science, and the Royal Astronomical Society declared the **findings** **consistent** with Einstein's prediction. **Coverage** was **sensational**: on 7 November The Times **proclaimed** a "Revolution in Science", and three days later the New York Times **trumpeted** "Lights All Askew in the Heavens". Einstein became an **overnight** **celebrity**.

The Nobel committee, **unmoved** by the **acclaim**, prized **experimental** **confirmation**. Allvar Gullstrand, a **distinguished** eye **specialist** on the committee and a Nobel laureate, **concluded** that experiment had not **verified** relativity. Finding no **nomination** in 1921 that **fulfilled** Nobel's **criteria**, it **withheld** the prize for a year, as its **statutes** **permit**.

On 9 November 1922 the **postponed** prize was **formally** **conferred** on Einstein "for his services to Theoretical Physics, and especially for his discovery of the law of the photoelectric effect". That law, **governing** how light **ejects** **electrons** from **metallic** surfaces, would **underpin** **quantum** theory. A letter the next day **clarified** that relativity had not been **evaluated**. Einstein was **aboard** a ship **bound** for Japan; he missed the December **ceremony**.

On 11 July 1923, in Gothenburg, a Swedish **harbour** city, he **devoted** his Nobel **lecture** to relativity. A committee may **avert** its **gaze** from a theory, but it cannot **dictate** what its laureate **elects** to discuss.
