# EN: The Glue That Failed

In 1968 Spencer Silver, a scientist at 3M, set out to make a super-strong **adhesive**. The **polymer** he produced did something else. Its tiny **spheres** formed a **sticky** layer with little **tack**, so paper would **adhere** **lightly**, peel off and be **reapplied**. To an **inventor** chasing strength, the **formulation** was a **setback**.

For five years Silver acted as its **promoter** within the **corporate** walls of 3M, in **informal** chats and at **seminars**. His own **summary** called it a solution without a problem. His **perseverance** met **indifference**. The **unconventional** material found no use, and the **pursuit** proved **fruitless**.

In 1974 Art Fry, a colleague who had heard one of those talks, met an **annoyance** at church. The paper **bookmarks** in his **choir**'s **hymn** book kept getting **dislodged** and falling out. The **frustration** gave Fry an idea. The **unwanted** adhesive could make a marker that would **cling** to a **sheet**, stay put and leave it **undamaged**.

In 1977 the notes were test-marketed as Press 'n Peel in four American cities, where **purchasers** showed little **enthusiasm**. In 1978 3M's **marketers** staged a **blitz** in Boise, Idaho, and **dispensed** **complimentary** **packets** to offices. More than 90 per cent of **respondents** who tried them said they would buy. After the **apathy** came a **turnaround**.

On 6 April 1980 the **pads**, **rebranded** as Post-it Notes, went on sale **nationally** in the US, and Canada and Europe followed in 1981. Silver died on 8 May 2021, aged 80. The **stationery** **staple** owes its **signature** **canary** **tone** to an **unplanned** choice. The laboratory next door had only **leftover** yellow paper.
