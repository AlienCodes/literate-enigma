# EN: The Glue That Failed

In 1968 Spencer Silver, a 3M researcher, sought a super-strong **adhesive**. The **formulation** he produced showed the reverse **weakness**. Built from tiny **polymer** **spheres**, it was only lightly **sticky**, offering a **temporary** **cling** that could be peeled away and **reused**. For its **inventor**, the result was a **setback**.

For five years Silver promoted it inside 3M, informally and at **seminars**, calling it a solution in search of a problem. The **promoter** showed real **persistence** and **perseverance**, yet his audiences stayed **unmoved**. There were no **takers** and no **commitment**. His **pursuit** produced no product, and the **corporate** mood was **indifference**.

In 1974 Art Fry, a 3M man who had heard one of those talks, faced a **frustration**. In his church **choir**, the paper **bookmarks** in his **hymn** book kept being **dislodged**. His **objective** was a marker that stayed put. Fry **realised** he could **harness** Silver's invention to **anchor** one **harmless** to the pages.

In 1977 the notes were tried **commercially** as Press 'n Peel in four US cities, where shoppers were **unimpressed** and showed little **enthusiasm**. In 1978 3M sent **complimentary** **handouts** to offices in Boise, Idaho, in a **blitz** that produced a **turnaround**. Among the **testers**, more than 90 per cent were **favourable**, saying they would become **buyers**.

On 6 April 1980 the **stationery** reached US **outlets** **nationally** as Post-it Notes. Canada and the European **continent** joined the **marketplace** in 1981. Silver died on 8 May 2021, aged 80. The **pads** got their **canary** **colouring** by **incidental** choice, because the laboratory next door had only **leftover** yellow paper.
