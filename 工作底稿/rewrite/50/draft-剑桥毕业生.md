# EN: The Glue That Failed

In 1968 Spencer Silver, a 3M scientist, sought a super-strong **adhesive**. His **formulation** produced a **polymer** that was only lightly **sticky**. Its **clinging** hold was **temporary**, so a sheet could be peeled off and **reused**. For the **inventor**, this outcome was a **setback** and a **disappointment** rather than a success.

For the next five years Silver acted as the **promoter** of this **unwanted** glue inside 3M, in corridor talk and at **seminars**. His own **summary** called it a solution without a problem. His **persistence** and **perseverance** met **indifference**. No department adopted the **unconventional** discovery, and the **corporate** **pursuit** yielded nothing.

In 1974 Art Fry, a 3M colleague who had heard one of those talks, faced a small **annoyance**. He sang in his church **choir**, and paper markers kept being **dislodged** from his **hymn** book. The **irritation** and **frustration** **resurfaced** at each **rehearsal**. Fry saw that the glue could keep a **bookmark** **anchored** while leaving every page **undamaged**.

In 1977 3M offered the notes as Press 'n Peel in four US cities. They met **apathy**, as **purchasers** showed little **enthusiasm**. In 1978 **marketers** tried a **promotional** **blitz** in Boise, Idaho, where **complimentary** **handouts** were **dispensed** to offices. More than 90 per cent of **respondents** who tried the notes said they would buy them.

On 6 April 1980 the **repackaged** **stationery** reached **retail** counters **nationally** as Post-it Notes, with **availability** in Canada and Europe from 1981. Spencer Silver died on 8 May 2021, aged 80. Even the **canary** **colouring** was **unplanned**, decided **solely** by a laboratory next door holding nothing but **leftover** yellow paper.
