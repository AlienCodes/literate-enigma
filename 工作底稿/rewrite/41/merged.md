# EN: Thirteen in the Cave

On 23 June 2018, twelve boys aged 11 to 16 from a **junior** football **squad** went on an **outing** with their 25-year-old assistant coach. They entered Tham Luang, a **sprawling** **maze** of **tunnels** in northern Thailand, to **explore** its **darkness**. **Monsoon** rain then **flooded** the passages behind them, and the rising water trapped all of them.

For nine days their **whereabouts** were unknown. On 2 July two **elite** British **divers**, threading through **submerged** passages, reached an **inundated** chamber about 4 km from the **entrance**. All 13 were there, **huddled** on a **muddy** **mound** and **perched** just above the water, every one of them alive.

The only **exit** ran through **kilometres** of **constricted**, **treacherous** passages, and the boys were **inexperienced**, with no **scuba** or **diving** training. **Rescuers** began laying oxygen **cylinders** along the route. On 6 July Saman Kunan, a former Thai Navy SEAL, fell **unconscious** while laying them and died.

The **evacuation** began on 8 July, and the boys came out in **batches**. Each was given **doses** of sedative, **wrapped** **motionless** in a **stretcher** and passed from diver to diver, with **medics** stationed along the route. On 10 July the last four **footballers** followed their **teammates** out, **escorted** alongside their coach.

The **multinational** **mobilisation** was a feat of **logistics**. Some 10,000 people **converged** on the site, including a **contingent** of more than 100 divers, 2,000 soldiers and 900 **policemen**, with teams **deployed** from abroad. The rescue had a **sobering** **postscript**: in December 2019 another Navy SEAL died of a blood infection caught during the operation. Every boy came home alive, and two rescuers were **mourned**.
