# EN: Thirteen in the Cave

On 23 June 2018, twelve boys aged 11 to 16 from a **junior** football **squad** set off on an **outing** with their 25-year-old assistant coach. The **teammates** entered Tham Luang, a **sprawling** **maze** in northern Thailand, to **explore** its **darkness**. **Monsoon** rain poured into the **tunnel**, and rising floodwater trapped them.

Nine days passed without word. On 2 July two **elite** British **divers**, threading through **submerged** passages, surfaced in a **flooded** chamber about 4 km from the **entrance**. Their **torch** **beam** picked out all 13, **perched** on a **muddy** **mound** just above the water, every one of them alive.

But the sole way out lay through kilometres of **treacherous**, narrow passages, and the boys were **inexperienced** in **scuba** **diving**. With another **downpour** **looming**, **rescuers** raced against time. On 6 July Saman Kunan, a former Thai Navy SEAL and military **veteran**, was laying oxygen **cylinders** along the route when he fell **unconscious** and died.

The **evacuation** began on 8 July, ahead of the **impending** rains, with **pumps** running. The boys came out in **batches**. Each was given sedative **doses**, **wrapped** **motionless** in a **stretcher** and **escorted** from diver to diver, with doctors at **checkpoints** along the way. On 10 July the last four boys and the coach emerged, and all 13 survived.

**Multinational** **cooperation** drove a vast **mobilisation** and **logistics** effort. About 10,000 people **converged**, including a **contingent** of over 100 divers, 2,000 **deployed** soldiers and 900 police officers. In the **aftermath**, a Navy SEAL **comrade** died in December 2019 from a blood infection contracted there. Every boy came out **safely**, and two rescuers paid the **ultimate** price.
