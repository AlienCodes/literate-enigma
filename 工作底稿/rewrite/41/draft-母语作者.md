# EN: Thirteen in the Cave

On 23 June 2018, twelve boys aged 11 to 16 from a **junior** football **squad** went on an **outing** with their 25-year-old assistant coach. They passed the **entrance** of Tham Luang, a **sprawling** **maze** of **tunnels** in northern Thailand, to **explore** its **darkness**. A **monsoon** **downpour** then **flooded** the passages behind them.

For nine days nobody knew whether they were still alive. On 2 July two British **divers** reached an **inundated** chamber about 4 km inside and swept a **torch** **beam** through the **dripping** **gloom**. All thirteen were there, **huddled** on a **muddy** **mound** and **perched** just above the water.

Getting them out was harder for **rescuers**. The only route ran through **kilometres** of **submerged**, narrow and **treacherous** passages, and the boys were **inexperienced**, with no **scuba** or **diving** training. More rain was **impending**. On 6 July Saman Kunan, a former Thai Navy SEAL and **veteran** diver, fell **unconscious** while laying oxygen **cylinders** along the route, and died.

The **evacuation** began on 8 July. Each boy was given **doses** of sedative, **wrapped** in a **stretcher** and handed from one **elite** diver to the next, with **medics** stationed along the way. They came out **slowly**, in **batches**. On 10 July the last four **footballers** followed their **teammates** out, **escorted** alongside their coach.

The **multinational** **cooperation** saw some 10,000 people **converge** on Tham Luang, including a **contingent** of more than 100 divers, 2,000 soldiers and 900 **policemen**. That **solidarity** had a **sobering** **postscript**. In December 2019 another Navy SEAL died of a blood infection caught in the rescue. Every boy came home alive, and two rescuers were **mourned**.
