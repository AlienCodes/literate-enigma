# EN: Thirteen in the Cave

On 23 June 2018, twelve **teammates** aged 11 to 16 from a **junior** football **squad** went on an **outing** with their 25-year-old assistant coach. Entering the Tham Luang cave in northern Thailand, they set out to **explore** its **sprawling** **maze**. A **monsoon** **downpour** sent water surging through the **tunnel** behind them, trapping all 13.

For nine days nothing was heard of them. On 2 July two **elite** British **divers** reached a **flooded** chamber about 4 km from the **entrance**. Their **torch** **beam** fell on the group, **huddled**, **perched** on a **muddy** **mound** above the water. All were alive.

The only way out lay through kilometres of **constricted** underwater passages, **treacherous** even for **veteran** **scuba** divers, and each boy was a **novice** at **diving**. As **pumps** drew water out, **rescuers** laid air **cylinders** along the route. On 6 July Saman Kunan, a former Thai Navy SEAL, fell **unconscious** while placing them and died.

From 8 July, with more rain **impending**, the boys came out in **batches**. Each was given calming **doses**, **wrapped** in a **stretcher** and passed from diver to diver, **escorted** through **submerged** passages and **darkness**. **Medics** were stationed along the route. On 10 July the **evacuation** ended with the last four boys and the coach.

Some 10,000 people **converged**, among them over 100 divers and a **complement** of 2,000 soldiers and 900 police, with **contingents** **deployed** from abroad. Such **multinational** **cooperation** rested on **logistics** and **solidarity**. In the **aftermath**, in December 2019, a second Navy SEAL died of a blood infection caught in the rescue. Every boy came out alive, at the cost of two **rescuers**.
