# EN: The Mould That Changed the World
# ZH: 改变人类命运的霉菌
## 英文

In September 1928 Alexander Fleming, a bacteriologist at St Mary's Hospital in London, returned from holiday to find that a **stray** **mould** had **contaminated** a dish of staphylococci. No **bacteria** grew around it. Popular **mythology** blames an open window; the spores probably **originated** in a laboratory downstairs, where moulds were **cultivated**.

Fleming published his findings in June 1929. Yet the **crude** juice was **unstable** and **stubbornly** hard to **purify**, and he lacked the chemical **expertise** to **refine** it. He used it **chiefly** to **isolate** other **microbes**, and for about a decade penicillin lay **dormant**, a **curiosity** rather than a **cure**.

In Oxford, Howard Florey, an Australian pathologist, **revived** the work with Ernst Chain, a German-born **chemist** and Jewish **refugee**. Their colleague Norman Heatley **improvised** an **ingenious** **apparatus** to **extract** the **compound**. On 25 May 1940 eight mice received a **lethal** **dose** of bacteria; only the four **injected** with penicillin lived. The Lancet, a **prestigious** journal, printed the **outcome** that August.

In February 1941 Albert Alexander, an Oxford police **constable** **gravely** ill with an **infection**, **rallied** on penicillin. Then the **scarce** supply ran out, and in March he died. The **obstacle** was now **quantity**. From July 1941 a government laboratory in Peoria, Illinois, **boosted** yields about tenfold with corn-steep liquor, and a mouldy market **melon** **furnished** a more **prolific** strain. By D-Day, in June 1944, **industrial** **fermentation** met **Allied** needs.

In October 1945 Fleming, Florey and Chain shared the Nobel prize; Heatley was **overlooked**. **Fortune** put the mould in Fleming's dish; a **tenacious** team, and years of **toil**, **transformed** it into a **remedy**.

## 中文

1928年9月，伦敦圣玛丽医院的细菌学家亚历山大·弗莱明度假归来，发现一团**外来的**(stray)**霉菌**(mould)**污染了**(contaminated)一只培养葡萄球菌的培养皿。霉菌周围没有**细菌**(bacteria)生长。流行的**传说**(mythology)把它归咎于一扇敞开的窗户；其实孢子很可能**来自**(originated)楼下一间**培育**(cultivated)霉菌的实验室。

弗莱明于1929年6月发表了研究结果。然而，这种**粗制的**(crude)汁液性质**不稳定**(unstable)，又**始终**(stubbornly)难以**提纯**(purify)，而他也缺乏**精炼**(refine)它所需的化学**专业知识**(expertise)。他**主要**(chiefly)用它来**分离**(isolate)其他**微生物**(microbes)，此后约十年，青霉素一直**沉寂**(dormant)，只是件**稀罕物**(curiosity)，而不是**治病的药**(cure)。

在牛津，澳大利亚病理学家霍华德·弗洛里与德国出生的**化学家**(chemist)、犹太**难民**(refugee)恩斯特·钱恩一起，**重启**(revived)了这项研究。他们的同事诺曼·希特利用手边的材料**临时拼凑**(improvised)出一套**巧妙的**(ingenious)**装置**(apparatus)，用来**提取**(extract)这种**化合物**(compound)。1940年5月25日，八只小鼠接种了**致死**(lethal)**剂量**(dose)的细菌；只有**注射**(injected)了青霉素的那四只活了下来。同年8月，**享有盛誉的**(prestigious)期刊《柳叶刀》刊登了这一**结果**(outcome)。

1941年2月，牛津**警员**(constable)艾伯特·亚历山大因**感染**(infection)而病情**危重**(gravely)，用上青霉素后**有所好转**(rallied)。随后，**稀缺的**(scarce)青霉素用完了，3月，他去世了。此时的**障碍**(obstacle)已变成了**数量**(quantity)。从1941年7月起，伊利诺伊州皮奥里亚的一家政府实验室用玉米浆把产量**提高**(boosted)了约十倍，市场上一只发霉的**甜瓜**(melon)又**提供**(furnished)了一株更**高产的**(prolific)菌株。到1944年6月诺曼底登陆时，**工业**(industrial)**发酵**(fermentation)已能满足**盟军**(Allied)的需求。

1945年10月，弗莱明、弗洛里和钱恩共同获得诺贝尔奖；希特利却**遭到忽视**(overlooked)。**机缘**(Fortune)把霉菌送进了弗莱明的培养皿；一支**锲而不舍的**(tenacious)团队，加上多年的**辛劳**(toil)，把它**转化**(transformed)成了**良药**(remedy)。
