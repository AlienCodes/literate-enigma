# EN: The Woman Who Won Two Nobels

Marie Curie was born Maria Skłodowska on 7 November 1867 in **Russian-ruled** Warsaw, where women were **denied** university **admission**. **Eager** to **pursue** **formal** study, she **emigrated** to Paris in 1891, **enrolling** at the Sorbonne. On 26 July 1895 she **wed** the physicist Pierre Curie, **forging** a research **partnership**.

Becquerel found that **uranium** gives off rays; she coined **radioactivity** for the rays some **elements** give off. In July 1898 the Curies announced polonium, a **tribute** to **Poland**, then **radium** on 26 December. In a leaky shed they processed several **tonnes** of pitchblende, a uranium **ore**, **recovering** about a tenth of a **gram** of radium **chloride** by 1902.

On 10 December 1903 she **jointly** won the physics Nobel with Pierre and Becquerel, the first woman so **recognised**. The nomination **initially** left her out; Pierre's **insistence** **rectified** the **omission**. **Bereavement** followed. On 19 April 1906 Pierre was **fatally** injured in a street accident. On 5 November she gave her **inaugural** lecture as his **successor**, the Sorbonne's first woman professor.

On 10 December 1911 she won the chemistry Nobel **outright**. A press **scandal** about her private life **erupted**; **begged** to stay away, she lectured **undeterred** in **Stockholm** on 11 December. Her Nobels in two sciences remain **unmatched**. In the First World War, X-ray vehicles and **stationary** posts she **organised** examined over a million **casualties**.

She died on 4 July 1934 of a blood **disorder**, probably **attributable** to **prolonged** exposure to radiation. Her radioactive notebooks are **preserved** in **lead-lined** boxes at the National Library of France; **readers** sign a **liability** **waiver** and wear **protective** **garments**. Each page **retains** her **handwriting** and her radium.
