# EN: The Woman Who Won Two Nobels

Maria Skłodowska was born in Warsaw on 7 November 1867, under a Russian **regime** that **prohibited** women from **enrolling** at university. In 1891 she **emigrated** to Paris and the Sorbonne, **pursuing** an **ambition** **denied** at home. On 26 July 1895 she **wed** the physicist Pierre Curie; their **alliance** became a **partnership**.

Henri Becquerel found that **uranium** gives off **rays**; she coined **radioactivity** for the rays some **elements** give off. The couple **heralded** polonium, named after her **motherland**, in July 1898, and radium on 26 December. In a **leaking** shed they **laboured** through **tonnes** of **ore**; by 1902 she had **recovered** **approximately** a tenth of a gram of radium **chloride**.

On 10 December 1903 she shared the physics Nobel **alongside** her **spouse** and Becquerel, the first woman so **recognised**. The nomination **initially** left her out; Pierre's **insistence** **ensured** her **inclusion**. On 19 April 1906 a street accident **fatally** injured him. On 5 November she gave her **inaugural** lecture as his **successor**, the Sorbonne's first woman professor.

On 10 December 1911 she won the chemistry Nobel **outright**. During a press **scandal** about her private life, she was **discouraged** from **attending**. **Undeterred**, she lectured in Stockholm on 11 December. Her prizes in two scientific **domains** remain **unrivalled**. In the First World War her X-ray **vans** and **stationary** posts examined over a million **casualties**.

She died on 4 July 1934 of a blood **disorder**, probably the **consequence** of **prolonged** exposure to radiation. Her **handwritten** notebooks, still **radiating**, **reside** in lead-lined boxes in the **custody** of the National Library of France. Readers sign a **waiver** and **don** **protective** **gowns** to **handle** them.
