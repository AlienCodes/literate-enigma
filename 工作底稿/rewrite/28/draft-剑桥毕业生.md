# EN: The Woman Who Won Two Nobels

Maria Skłodowska was born in Warsaw on 7 November 1867, under Russian **dominion**, where women were **prohibited** from attending university. **Deprived** of **admission**, she **emigrated** to Paris in 1891 to **pursue** studies at the Sorbonne. On 26 July 1895 she **wed** the physicist Pierre Curie, **forging** an **intellectual** **partnership**.

**Investigating** Becquerel's **uranium** rays, she coined **radioactivity** for the rays some **elements** give off. In July 1898 the **duo** announced polonium, named in **tribute** to her homeland, and on 26 December, **radium**. In a leaky shed they processed tonnes of pitchblende, a uranium **ore**; by 1902 she had **recovered** about a tenth of a **gram** of radium **chloride**.

On 10 December 1903 she **jointly** won the Nobel Prize in Physics with Pierre and Becquerel, the first woman to **garner** one. The original **proposal** **neglected** to name her; Pierre's **insistence** **ensured** her **inclusion**. Pierre's **untimely** **demise** came on 19 April 1906; on 5 November she gave her **inaugural** lecture as his **successor**, the Sorbonne's first woman professor.

On 10 December 1911 she won the Nobel Prize in Chemistry **outright**. **Mired** in a press **scandal** about her private life, she was **counselled** to stay away but, **undeterred**, lectured in Stockholm on 11 December. Her Nobels in two sciences remain an **unrivalled** **distinction**. During the First World War she **mobilised** a **fleet** of X-ray **vans**.

She died on 4 July 1934 of a blood **disorder**, probably caused by **prolonged** exposure to radiation. Her still-radioactive notebooks are **preserved** in **lead-lined** boxes in a Paris **repository**; **patrons** must sign a **liability** **waiver** and wear **protective** **garments**. On every page, the **phenomenon** she named **persists**.
