# EN: The Day Zero That Never Came

In January 2018, Cape Town warned that Day Zero could arrive in April. The term **denoted** a **grim** **threshold**: once dam storage, the water in the city's **reservoirs**, fell to 13.5 per cent, most **households** would be cut off. **Residents** would **trek** to about 200 **designated** collection points for water.

The **prospect** was real. Three years of **relentless** drought, from 2015 to 2017, had **sapped** the **catchments** supplying a **metropolitan** area of about four million people. By mid-2017 the dams stood between a seventh and a third full, and **precipitation** remained **meagre**. The **countdown** turned a **chronic** **shortage** into an **imminent** date.

From 1 February 2018, each person was **allotted** 50 litres a day, enough for a quick shower and toilet **flush**. Officials **disclosed** dam levels and usage, making **thrift** **visible** and **lapses** **conspicuous**. **Consumption** was roughly **halved**, from about 1.2 billion litres a day in 2015 to about 500 million by early 2018.

Other forces **intervened**. In February 2018 farmers **upstream** **discharged** water from their own dam, and Day Zero was **postponed** to July. In March 2018 officials judged it **unlikely** that year. Winter **downpours** from mid-2018 **replenished** the reservoirs, about half full by July.

The **sacrifice** was **unequal**. Critics argued that **townships**, poor areas **segregated** under **apartheid**, had long used **scant** water yet **incurred** higher **tariffs**. Others blamed **alarmist** **rhetoric** for **anxiety** and tourist **cancellations**. **Restrictions** were **eased** from October 2018. The collection points stayed **unused**. A **stark** countdown had changed **habits**, but rain finished the job, and the **frugal** poor had little left to give.
