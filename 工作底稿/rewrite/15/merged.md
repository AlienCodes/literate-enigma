# EN: The Day Zero That Never Came

In January 2018, Cape Town **issued** a **stark** **alert**: Day Zero could arrive in April. The term **denoted** a **grim** **threshold**, reached when dam storage, the water in the city's **reservoirs** as a **percentage** of capacity, fell to 13.5 per cent. Most taps would then be shut, and **residents** would queue at about 200 **designated** collection points.

The **prospect** was **genuine**. Three **consecutive** **arid** years, from 2015 to 2017, had **diminished** the reservoirs serving a **metropolitan** area of about four million people. By early 2018 the dams were roughly a fifth full. The **countdown** turned a **chronic** **shortage** into an **imminent** date.

The city paired **mandatory** **restrictions** with **unprecedented** **transparency**. From 1 February 2018, each person was **allotted** 50 litres a day. Officials **disclosed** dam levels and **household** **consumption**, making **thrift** **visible**, and some reports described **watchful** neighbours noticing **lapses**. Daily use was roughly **halved**, from about 1.2 billion litres in 2015 to about 500 million by early 2018.

Other forces **intervened**. In February 2018, **upstream** farmers **voluntarily** released water from their own dam, and Day Zero was **postponed** to July. In March 2018, officials judged Day Zero **unlikely** that year. Winter rains then **replenished** the reservoirs, which were about half full by July.

The **burden**, critics argued, fell **unevenly**. **Townships**, poor areas **segregated** under **apartheid**, had long used **scant** water, yet **steeper** **tariffs** still **squeezed** them. Others blamed **alarmist** warnings for tourist **cancellations**. The 50-litre limit was **eased** in October 2018. Households, farmers and winter rains together kept the collection points **idle**, while the poorest had the least to spare.
