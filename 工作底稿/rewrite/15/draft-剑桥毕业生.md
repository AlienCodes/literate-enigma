# EN: The Day Zero That Never Came

In January 2018, Cape Town **issued** a **stark** **alert**: Day Zero could arrive in April. The term marked a **threshold**. Once storage in the city's **reservoirs** fell to 13.5 per cent, taps to most homes would be shut, and **residents** would collect a **meagre** **quota** at about 200 **designated** collection points.

The **threat** had **loomed** for years. Three **consecutive** dry years, from 2015 to 2017, had **sapped** the reservoirs serving about four million people. By early 2018 they were roughly a fifth full. **Scarcity**, once an **abstract** **statistic**, had become a **tangible** **prospect**.

The city paired **mandatory** **restrictions** with **unprecedented** **transparency**. From 1 February 2018, each person was **allotted** 50 litres a day. Officials **disclosed** dam levels and **household** **consumption**, the **countdown** made the **peril** **visible**, and **peer pressure** helped **foster** **compliance**. Daily use **shrank** from about 1.2 billion litres in 2015 to about 500 million by early 2018.

Yet **restraint** alone did not **avert** the crisis. In February 2018, **upstream** farmers released water from their own dam, and Day Zero was **postponed** to July. In March 2018, officials said it was unlikely that year. Winter **downpours** then **replenished** the dams to about half full by July 2018.

The **burden**, critics argued, fell **unevenly**. **Townships**, **impoverished** districts **segregated** under apartheid, had always used little water, yet **steeper** **tariffs** still **squeezed** them. Others called the warnings **alarmist**, blaming them for tourist **cancellations**. The 50-litre limit was **eased** in October 2018, and the collection points stayed **idle**. Did four million people **fend off** Day Zero, or did the rain **intervene**?
