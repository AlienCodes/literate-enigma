# EN: The Day Zero That Never Came

In January 2018 Cape Town set its **residents** an **ominous** date. Day Zero, when dam storage would hit a **threshold** of 13.5 per cent, could arrive in April. Taps to most homes would be shut; people would queue at about 200 collection points, **clutching** **buckets**, for a **quota**. A **dire** **countdown** began.

The **crisis** had been **brewing** for years. Three years of **meagre** rain, from 2015 to 2017, had **sapped** the **reservoirs** of a city of about four million, leaving them about a fifth full by early 2018. A **prolonged** **shortage** had become **acute** **scarcity**.

Yet **salvation** came first not from the sky but from **ordinary** **households**. From 1 February 2018 each resident was **restricted** to 50 litres a day. Officials **disclosed** dam levels and **consumption**, the countdown made danger **tangible**, and **peer** pressure **enforced** **compliance**. Daily use was **slashed** from about 1,200 million litres in 2015 to about 500 million by early 2018.

Others **intervened**. In February 2018 **upstream** farmers **surrendered** water from their own dam, and Day Zero was **postponed** to July. In March 2018 officials called it **unlikely** that year. Winter **downpours** **replenished** the dams to about half full by July 2018, and the 50-litre limit was **eased** in October 2018.

But the **burden** fell **unevenly**. **Researchers** argued **townships**, **impoverished** areas **segregated** under **apartheid**, had used little water all along, so **steeper** **tariffs** hit them **disproportionately**. Critics said **alarmist** warnings **provoked** panic and hurt **tourism**. The collection points stood **unused**, **testament** to a **disaster** **averted** by fear, rain and people with little left to give.
