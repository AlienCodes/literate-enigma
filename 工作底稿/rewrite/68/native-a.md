In June 1816 a **relentless** **downpour** held a small party **captive** at the Villa Diodati, a house Lord Byron had **rented** near Geneva. After reading a French **translation** of German ghost stories, Byron **proposed** a **contest**, in which each **guest** would write a ghost story.

The **gloom** had a distant **origin**. On 10 April 1815 Mount Tambora, on the Indonesian island of Sumbawa, **erupted** with a **ferocity** **rarely** matched in recorded history. Roughly 10,000 people **perished** **outright**, and tens of thousands more **succumbed** to the **famine** and **disease** that followed.

The **blast** **injected** vast quantities of **sulphur** dioxide into the **stratosphere**. There the gas formed **sulphate** **aerosols** that **encircled** the **globe**, **reflecting** sunlight and **lowering** temperatures worldwide. In Europe and North America 1816 became known as the Year Without a Summer.

Europe **endured** a **chilly**, **sodden** summer. **Crops** failed or **rotted** in the fields, the price of **grain** and bread **soared**, and **hunger**, sickness and **unrest** **ensued**.

Among those **trapped** near Geneva was Mary Godwin, later Mary Shelley, then 18. She had **journeyed** to the lake in May with Percy Bysshe Shelley and Claire Clairmont, while Byron had brought his **physician**, John Polidori. In her **introduction** to the 1831 **edition** she **recalled** a damp, **cheerless** summer in which **ceaseless** rain often **confined** the group indoors for days.

The contest proved **fruitful**. Polidori produced The Vampyre, and Byron **composed** the poem Darkness that summer. Mary began the tale that became Frankenstein. **Published** **anonymously** in 1818, the novel **bore** her name only in the 1831 edition. A rainy summer in a rented villa had **yielded** what is often called the first work of science **fiction**.
