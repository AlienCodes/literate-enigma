## 英文
On 10 April 1815 Mount Tambora, on the Indonesian island of Sumbawa, **erupted** with a **ferocity** **rarely** **matched** in recorded history. Roughly ten thousand people **perished** in the **blast** and its **immediate** **aftermath**. Tens of thousands more later **succumbed** to **famine** and **disease**.

The volcano **hurled** **colossal** **quantities** of **sulphur dioxide** into the **stratosphere**. There the gas formed sulphate **aerosols**, a **veil** that **encircled** the **globe** and **reflected** sunlight back into space. Temperatures worldwide **dipped**. In Europe and North America, 1816 became known as the Year Without a Summer.

Across Europe that summer was cold and **sodden**. **Crops** failed or **rotted** in the fields, the price of grain and bread **soared**, and hunger, sickness and **unrest** followed.

In May the 18-year-old Mary Godwin, later Mary Shelley, travelled to Lake Geneva with Percy Bysshe Shelley and Claire Clairmont. Lord Byron had **leased** the Villa Diodati nearby, **accompanied** by his **physician**, John Polidori. Around 16 June, **confined** **indoors** by rain, the party read a French **translation** of German ghost stories. Byron then **proposed** that each of them write a ghost story of their own.

Mary later **recalled**, in her preface to the 1831 edition, a wet and **cheerless** summer in which **relentless** **downpours** often kept the group inside for days.

From that **contest** Mary **embarked** on the tale that became Frankenstein. Polidori produced The Vampyre, and Byron **composed** a poem, Darkness. Frankenstein **appeared** **anonymously** in 1818. The 1831 edition **bore** her name, and the novel is often called the first work of science **fiction**.

A **haze** of sulphate over the **tropics** had **drifted** **halfway** round the world and **drawn** **curtains** of rain across a lake in Switzerland.
