## 英文
On 10 April 1815 Mount Tambora, on the Indonesian island of Sumbawa, tore itself apart in an **eruption** of **tremendous** **fury**, **rarely** equalled in recorded history. Roughly ten thousand people died from its direct effects. Tens of thousands more on Sumbawa, Lombok and Bali were lost to the **starvation** and sickness of its **aftermath**.

The blast sent **prodigious** volumes of **sulphur dioxide** into the stratosphere. There the gas formed **sulphate particles**, a thin **sheet** that drifted around the globe and turned sunlight back towards space, **thereby** **lowering** temperatures **world-wide**. **Consequently**, Europe and North America came to know 1816 as the **so-called** Year Without a Summer.

Europe's summer was cold and **damp**. Crops failed or lay **rotten** in the fields, the price of **cereal** and **loaves** climbed **sharply**, and famine, sickness and **turmoil** followed.

In May the 18-year-old Mary Godwin, later Mary Shelley, travelled to the **shore** of Lake Geneva with Percy Bysshe Shelley and Claire Clairmont. Lord Byron had taken a **lease** on the Villa Diodati, on the **outskirts** of Geneva, and brought his **personal physician**, John Polidori. Around 16 June, **cooped up** by rain, the friends read German **ghost stories** in French **translation**. Byron then suggested that each of them write a **frightful** tale of their own.

In her **preface** to the 1831 edition, Mary **recollected** a wet and **gloomy** summer, **amidst** **ceaseless** **downpours** that often confined the party indoors for days.

The **storytelling** contest set Mary to work on what became Frankenstein. Polidori produced The Vampyre, **whilst** Byron's summer **verse** included the poem Darkness. Frankenstein was published **anonymously** in 1818, and the 1831 edition carried her name. It is often called the first work of science fiction, a **genre** with a **grievous** **genesis**.

The rain that held the **occupants** of a Swiss house **indoors** had begun **halfway** round the world.
