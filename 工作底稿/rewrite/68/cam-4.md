# EN: The Volcano That Gave Us Frankenstein

## 英文

In June 1816 a **ceaseless** **downpour** **besieged** the Villa Diodati, Lord Byron's **leased** **abode** near Geneva, and confined his guests indoors. After reading a French **anthology** of German **ghost** stories in **translation**, Byron suggested that each of them write a **spine-chilling** tale.

The **melancholy** weather had a distant cause. On 10 April 1815 Tambora, on the **Indonesian** island of Sumbawa, reached the **culmination** of a **fierce** **eruption**, among the most **potent** in recorded history. About 10,000 people died outright, and tens of thousands more were lost to **starvation** and disease in its **aftermath**.

The blast **expelled** **prodigious** quantities of sulphur dioxide into the stratosphere. There sulphate aerosols formed a **particulate** **blanket** around the world, reflecting **sunshine** and **lowering** temperatures **globally**. In Europe and North America, 1816 became known as the Year Without a Summer.

Europe endured a **chilly**, **stormy** summer. **Cereal** crops failed or **decayed** in the fields, grain and bread prices **escalated**, and **scarcity**, sickness and **unrest** followed.

The **unmarried** Mary Godwin, later Mary Shelley, had arrived in May **alongside** Percy Bysshe Shelley and Claire Clairmont. Byron had brought his doctor, John Polidori. In her **preface** to the 1831 edition, Mary would **recollect** a wet, **miserable** summer whose **perpetual** rain often kept the group in the house for days.

The contest proved **fruitful**. Mary, then an **eighteen-year-old**, began the **prose** tale that became Frankenstein. Polidori wrote The Vampyre, **whilst** Byron's **poetic** **output** that summer included Darkness.

Frankenstein was published **anonymously** in 1818. The 1831 **reissue** carried her name, and the novel is often called the first work of science fiction. The downpour was Tambora's doing, the creature Mary's alone.
