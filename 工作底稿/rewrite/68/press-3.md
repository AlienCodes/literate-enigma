## 英文
On 10 April 1815 Mount Tambora, on the Indonesian island of Sumbawa, **tore** itself apart in an **eruption** of **tremendous** **fury**, **rarely** equalled in recorded history. Roughly ten thousand people died at once. Tens of thousands more were lost to the **starvation** and sickness of its **aftermath**.

The **climatic** **repercussions** reached far beyond the **tropics**. **Prodigious** volumes of **sulphur dioxide** rose into the stratosphere and formed **sulphate particles**. Drifting around the globe as a thin **mantle**, they turned sunlight back into **outer space**, **thereby** **lowering** temperatures **world-wide**. **Consequently**, Europe and North America called 1816 the **so-called** Year Without a Summer.

Europe's summer was **chilly** and **rain-soaked**. Crops failed or lay **rotten** in the fields. **Cereal** and bread prices **escalated** **sharply**, and famine, sickness and **unrest** followed.

In May the 18-year-old Mary Godwin, later Mary Shelley, travelled to the **shore** of Lake Geneva with Percy Bysshe Shelley and Claire Clairmont. The **poet** Lord Byron had taken a **lease** on the Villa Diodati, a **residence** on the **outskirts** of Geneva, with his **personal physician**, John Polidori. Around 16 June, **cooped up** by a **torrent** of rain, the group read German **ghost stories** in French **translation**. Byron then suggested that each write a **frightful** tale of their own.

In her 1831 **preface**, Mary **recollected** a wet, **gloomy** summer, **amidst** **ceaseless** **downpours** that often trapped them inside for days.

The **contestants** set to work. Mary began the **prose** tale that became Frankenstein, Polidori produced The Vampyre, **whilst** Byron wrote the poem Darkness. The novel appeared **anonymously** in 1818, and the 1831 edition bore her name. It is often called the first work in the **genre** of science fiction.

Far from the lake, the **storytelling** had its **genesis** **halfway** round the world.
