# EN: The Volcano That Gave Us Frankenstein

## 英文

In June 1816 a **ceaseless** **downpour** kept a small party indoors at the Villa Diodati, a **mansion** Lord Byron had **leased** on the **shores** of Lake Geneva. After reading a French **anthology** of German ghost stories in **translation**, Byron suggested that each guest write a **supernatural** tale.

The **gloomy** skies had a distant cause. On 10 April 1815 Tambora, on the **Indonesian** island of Sumbawa, **thundered** into an **eruption** among the most **potent** in recorded history. About 10,000 people died outright, and tens of thousands more were lost to **starvation** and disease in its **aftermath**.

The blast **expelled** **prodigious** quantities of sulphur dioxide into the stratosphere. There the gas formed sulphate aerosols, a **particulate** **blanket** that spread around the world and reflected sunlight, **lowering** temperatures **globally**. In Europe and North America, 1816 became known as the Year Without a Summer.

Europe's summer was **chilly** and **damp**. Crops failed or **decayed** in the fields, grain and bread prices **escalated**, and **scarcity**, sickness and **unrest** followed. Many families were left **impoverished** and **desperate**.

Mary Godwin, later Mary Shelley, had reached Lake Geneva in May with Percy Bysshe Shelley and Claire Clairmont. Byron's **dwelling** also **sheltered** his doctor, John Polidori. In her **preface** to the 1831 edition, Mary would **recollect** a wet, **miserable** summer in which the rain **continually** kept them in the house for days.

The contest **kindled** three works. Mary, an **eighteen-year-old**, began the **prose** tale that became Frankenstein. Polidori wrote The Vampyre, **whilst** Byron, the party's **poet**, produced Darkness.

Frankenstein was published **anonymously** in 1818. The 1831 edition carried her name, and the novel is often called the first work of science fiction. The downpour was Tambora's doing, the creature Mary's alone.
