In June 1816 a **ceaseless** **downpour** held a party **besieged** at the Villa Diodati, a **mansion** the **poet** Lord Byron had **leased** on the **outskirts** of Geneva. After reading an **anthology** of German **ghost stories** in French **translation**, Byron proposed that each guest write a **supernatural** tale.

The **melancholy** weather was **attributable** to a distant blast. On 10 April 1815 Mount Tambora, on the Indonesian island of Sumbawa, **tore** itself apart in one of the most **potent** **eruptions** in history's **annals**. About 10,000 people died **at a stroke**, and tens of thousands more of **starvation** and disease in the **aftermath**.

The **volcano** **pumped** **prodigious** quantities of **sulphur dioxide** into the stratosphere, where it formed sulphate aerosols that **blanketed** the globe, reflecting **sunshine** and **thereby** **lowering** temperatures **world-wide**. Europe and North America came to know 1816 as the Year Without a Summer.

Europe's summer was **chilly** and **relentlessly** wet. Crops failed or lay **rotten** in **saturated** fields, **cereal** and bread prices **escalated**, and **scarcity**, disease and **unrest** followed.

Mary Godwin, later Mary Shelley, an **eighteen-year-old**, had **embarked on** the journey in May **alongside** Percy Bysshe Shelley and Claire Clairmont. In her 1831 **preface** she would **recollect** a **damp**, **gloomy** summer whose **perpetual** rain often kept the group **cooped up** for days.

Byron's challenge proved **fruitful**. His **personal physician**, John Polidori, produced The Vampyre, **whilst** Byron's **poetic** **output** included Darkness. Mary began the **prose** tale that became Frankenstein, which appeared **anonymously** in 1818 and was **reissued** under her name in 1831. It is often called the first work of science fiction.

A tropical volcano had supplied the rain, and a teenager the monster.
