In June 1816 a **ceaseless** **downpour** kept a small party **confined** to the Villa Diodati, near Geneva. After reading a French **translation** of German ghost stories, Lord Byron **proposed** a **contest**. Each guest would write a ghost story of their own.

The **gloom** had a **remote** **origin**. On 10 April 1815 Tambora, on the Indonesian island of Sumbawa, **erupted** with a **violence** **rarely** matched in recorded history. Roughly 10,000 people **perished** **outright**, and tens of thousands more died of **famine** and **disease** in the months that followed.

The **blast** **injected** **sulphur dioxide** into the **stratosphere**. There it formed **sulphate** **aerosols**, which **encircled** the **globe** and **reflected** sunlight, **lowering** temperatures worldwide. In Europe and North America, 1816 became known as the Year Without a Summer.

European **harvests** **failed** or **rotted** in **sodden** fields. **Grain** and bread prices **soared**, and **hunger**, **sickness** and **unrest** **ensued**.

Beside Lake Geneva, the **weather** was **dismal** too. Mary Godwin, later Mary Shelley, had arrived in May with Percy Bysshe Shelley and Claire Clairmont. Byron had **rented** the villa nearby, bringing his **physician**, John Polidori. In her **preface** to the 1831 edition, Mary **recalled** a wet, **cheerless** summer in which **incessant** rain often **trapped** the group indoors for days.

The **challenge** **yielded** **unexpected** **fruit**. Mary, then 18, started the tale that **grew** into Frankenstein. Polidori produced The Vampyre, and Byron **composed** the poem Darkness.

Frankenstein **appeared** **anonymously** in 1818. The 1831 edition **bore** her name, and the novel is often called the first work of **science fiction**. A **teenager** stuck indoors by a **foreign** volcano's **sulphate** haze had **conceived** a creature **assembled** by science.
