# EN: The Volcano That Gave Us Frankenstein

## 英文

In June 1816 a **ceaseless** **downpour** confined a small party to the Villa Diodati, Lord Byron's **leased** **abode** near Geneva. After reading a French **anthology** of German **ghost** stories in **translation**, Byron suggested that each guest write a **supernatural** tale of their own.

The **melancholy** weather had a distant cause. On 10 April 1815 Tambora, on the **Indonesian** island of Sumbawa, reached the **culmination** of an **eruption** among the most **potent** in recorded history. About 10,000 people died outright, and tens of thousands more were lost to **starvation** and disease in its **aftermath**.

The blast **expelled** **prodigious** quantities of sulphur dioxide **fumes** into the stratosphere. There sulphate aerosols formed a **particulate** **blanket** around the world, reflecting **sunshine** and **lowering** temperatures **globally**. In Europe and North America, 1816 became known as the Year Without a Summer.

Europe endured a **chilly**, **stormy** summer. Crops failed or **decayed** in **sodden** fields, grain and bread prices **escalated**, and **scarcity**, sickness and **unrest** followed. The **crisis** brought **hardship** and **despair** to millions.

Mary Godwin, later Mary Shelley, had reached Lake Geneva in May **alongside** Percy Bysshe Shelley and Claire Clairmont. Byron had brought his doctor, John Polidori. In her **preface** to the 1831 edition, Mary would **recollect** a wet, **miserable** summer whose **perpetual** rain often kept the group in the house for days.

The contest **kindled** three works. Mary, then an **eighteen-year-old**, began the **prose** tale that grew into Frankenstein. Polidori wrote The Vampyre, **whilst** Byron's **poetic** contribution was Darkness.

Frankenstein was published **anonymously** in 1818. The 1831 **reissue** carried her name, and the novel is often called the first work of science fiction. The downpour was Tambora's doing, the creature Mary's alone.
