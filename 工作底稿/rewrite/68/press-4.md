## 英文
On 10 April 1815 Mount Tambora, on the Indonesian island of Sumbawa, **tore** itself apart in an **eruption** of **tremendous** **fury**, **rarely** equalled in recorded history. Roughly ten thousand people died at once. Tens of thousands more, on **famine-hit** Sumbawa, Lombok and Bali, were lost to **starvation** and sickness in its **aftermath**.

**Prodigious** volumes of **sulphur dioxide** formed **sulphate particles** in the stratosphere. Drifting round the globe as a thin **mantle**, they turned sunlight back into **outer space**, **thereby** **lowering** temperatures **world-wide**. **Consequently**, 1816 became the **so-called** Year Without a Summer in Europe and North America.

Europe's summer was **chilly** and **rain-soaked**. Crops failed or lay **rotten** in the fields. **Cereal** and bread prices **escalated** **sharply**, and famine, sickness and **unrest** followed.

In May the 18-year-old Mary Godwin, later Mary Shelley, travelled to the **shore** of Lake Geneva with Percy Bysshe Shelley and Claire Clairmont. The **poet** Lord Byron had taken a **lease** on the Villa Diodati, on the **outskirts** of Geneva, and brought his **personal physician**, John Polidori. Around 16 June, a **torrent** of rain kept the friends inside. They **pored over** German **ghost stories** in French **translation**, and Byron suggested that each write a **frightful** tale.

Mary's 1831 **preface** **recollected** a **damp**, **gloomy** summer of **ceaseless** **downpours** that often trapped the party indoors for days.

The **contestants** set to work. Mary began the **prose** tale that became Frankenstein. Polidori produced The Vampyre, **whilst** Byron wrote the poem Darkness. The novel appeared **anonymously** in 1818, and the 1831 edition bore her name. It is often called the first work in the **genre** of science fiction.

The rain that kept the friends **cooped up** had its **genesis** in the **tropics**, **halfway** round the world.
