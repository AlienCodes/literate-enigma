# EN: The Wolf and the River

On 12 January 1995, eight grey wolves from Alberta, Canada, were **hauled** into Yellowstone National Park, whose wolves had been **exterminated** by 1926; six more followed a week later. **Confined** to **enclosures** until late March, the 14 then **roamed** free.

In a **viral** 2014 video, How Wolves Change Rivers, the British writer George Monbiot **recounts** how **intimidated** elk **retreat**, **willows** **sprout**, beavers **rebound** and rivers **stabilise**. Ecologists **dub** such **dread** a landscape of fear, and the **sequence** a trophic **cascade**, **tumbling** from **predators** to **vegetation**.

Yet the **census** **contradicted** the tale. Northern-range elk peaked at about 19,000 in January 1994 and were **dwindling** before any wolf arrived, **depleted** by hunters, **drought**, bears and cougars. Arthur Middleton of the University of Wyoming reported in 2013 that **collared** Clarks Fork elk, east of the park, seldom **encountered** wolves. The elk kept **grazing** unless one **ventured** within about half a mile.

In 2022 Elaine Brice and colleagues at Utah State University **demonstrated** that **sampling** only the tallest **aspens** **overstated** their **regrowth** four- to sevenfold. From 2001, Colorado State University ecologists Tom Hobbs and David Cooper **excluded** elk from plots and **erected** **artificial** beaver **dams**. In January 2024 they **concluded** that streams, **eroded** since beavers **vanished**, left willows high and dry. Oregon State University's William Ripple and **allies** **retorted** in 2025 that a weaker cascade **persists**.

From 1987, Defenders of Wildlife, a **conservation** charity, **reimbursed** ranchers about $1.4 million for **livestock** killed by wolves. The rivers proved **messier** than the **fable**; a science willing to **revise** its **cherished** story, though, may **merit** more trust than the story ever did.
