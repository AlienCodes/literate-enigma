# EN: Shadows of Wolves Over Yellowstone
# ZH: 黄石公园狼影再现
## 英文

Yellowstone National Park's **iconic** grey wolves had been **exterminated** by 1926. On 12 January 1995 eight wolves **captured** in Alberta, Canada, were **hauled** there in **crates**; six more followed a week later. Kept in **enclosures** until late March, they **roamed** the **wilderness**.

A **viral** 2014 video narrated by George Monbiot, a British writer, **spun** a **compelling** **narrative**: **wary** of **predators**, elk **retreated** from valleys, letting **willows** **flourish** and riverbanks **stabilise**. Ecologists call this chain a trophic **cascade**: predators eat or **deter** plant-eaters, and **vegetation** **rebounds**.

Yet the **census** **contradicted** the story: northern-range elk peaked at about 19,000 in January 1994 and were **dwindling** before wolves arrived, **depleted** by **hunters**, **drought**, bears and cougars. A 2013 study by Arthur Middleton, a University of Wyoming ecologist, found that **collared** elk **rarely** **encountered** wolves and kept **grazing** unless one **strayed** within half a mile.

**Scrutiny** of the trees followed. A 2022 paper by Elaine Brice of Utah State University showed that **sampling** only the tallest **aspens** **overstated** **regrowth** four- to sevenfold. But the deeper **flaw** concerned water. Since 2001 Tom Hobbs and David Cooper of Colorado State University had fenced out elk and built **artificial** beaver **dams**. In January 2024 they reported that streams, **eroded** since beavers left, had cut too deep to **nourish** **stunted** willows.

Oregon State University's William Ripple and Robert Beschta, **advocates** of the **hypothesis**, **retorted** in 2025 that a weaker cascade **persists**; the **dispute** is **unresolved**. The wolves' shadow over the rivers proved fainter and **messier** than the **fable**. Yet a science willing to **revise** its favourite example in public may deserve more trust than any tale.

## 中文

黄石国家公园里**标志性的**(iconic)灰狼，到1926年已遭**赶尽杀绝**(exterminated)。1995年1月12日，八只在加拿大艾伯塔省**捕获**(captured)的灰狼装在**板条箱**(crates)里，**运**(hauled)到了这里；一周后又来了六只。它们在**围栏**(enclosures)里一直待到3月下旬，随后便在**荒野**(wilderness)中**四处游荡**(roamed)。

2014年，一段由英国作家乔治·蒙比奥特解说的视频在网上**疯传**(viral)，**编织**(spun)出一个**引人入胜的**(compelling)**故事**(narrative)：麋鹿**提防**(wary)着**捕食者**(predators)，**退出**(retreated)了河谷，于是**柳树**(willows)**繁茂**(flourish)起来，河岸也**稳固**(stabilise)下来。生态学家把这种连锁效应称为营养**级联**(cascade)：捕食者吃掉或**吓阻**(deter)食草动物，**植被**(vegetation)便**恢复生长**(rebounds)。

然而，**普查**(census)结果却与这套说法**相悖**(contradicted)：黄石北部草场的麋鹿在1994年1月达到约19,000头的峰值，狼还没来就已在**日渐减少**(dwindling)，数量被**猎人**(hunters)、**干旱**(drought)、熊和美洲狮不断**消耗**(depleted)。怀俄明大学生态学家阿瑟·米德尔顿2013年的一项研究发现，**戴项圈的**(collared)麋鹿**很少**(rarely)**遇到**(encountered)狼，只要没有狼**误入**(strayed)半英里以内，它们就照样**吃草**(grazing)。

随后，树木也受到了**仔细审查**(Scrutiny)。犹他州立大学的伊莱恩·布赖斯2022年发表的一篇论文表明，只**抽样测量**(sampling)最高的**山杨**(aspens)，会把它们的**再生**(regrowth)程度**夸大**(overstated)四到七倍。但更根本的**缺陷**(flaw)在于水。自2001年起，科罗拉多州立大学的汤姆·霍布斯和戴维·库珀就用围栏把麋鹿挡在外面，并修建了**人工**(artificial)海狸**坝**(dams)。2024年1月，他们报告说，海狸离开后溪流受到**侵蚀**(eroded)，河床下切太深，再也**滋养**(nourish)不了那些**矮小的**(stunted)柳树。

俄勒冈州立大学的威廉·里普尔和罗伯特·贝施塔是这一**假说**(hypothesis)的**倡导者**(advocates)，他们在2025年**反驳**(retorted)说，较弱的级联效应**依然存在**(persists)；这场**争论**(dispute)仍**悬而未决**(unresolved)。狼投在河流上的影子，原来比那则**寓言**(fable)淡得多，也**乱**(messier)得多。然而，一门愿意当众**修正**(revise)自己最得意案例的科学，或许比任何传说都更值得信赖。
