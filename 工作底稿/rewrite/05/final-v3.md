# EN: Shadows of Wolves Over Yellowstone
# ZH: 黄石公园狼影再现
## 英文

Yellowstone's **iconic** grey wolves had been **exterminated** by 1926. On 12 January 1995 eight wolves **captured** in Alberta, Canada, were **hauled** there in **crates**; six more followed. Kept in **enclosures** until late March, they were then released to **roam** the **wilderness**.

In February 2014 a **viral** video narrated by George Monbiot, a British writer, **spun** a **compelling** **narrative**: elk, thinned and **wary**, **retreated** from valleys, letting **willows** **flourish** and riverbanks **stabilise**. Ecologists call this a trophic (food-chain) **cascade**: **predators** eat or **deter** plant-eaters, and **vegetation** **rebounds**.

Yet the **census** **contradicted** the first claim: elk on the park's northern range peaked at about 19,000 in January 1994 and were **dwindling** before wolves arrived, **depleted** by **hunters**, **drought** and bears. Fear proved weak too: in June 2013 Arthur Middleton's Wyoming team found **collared** elk east of the park **grazing** on unless wolves **ventured** within half a mile.

In November 2021 Utah State's Elaine Brice showed that **sampling** only the tallest **aspens** **overstated** **regrowth** four- to sevenfold. The story's deeper **flaw** was water. Since 2001 Colorado State's Tom Hobbs and David Cooper had **excluded** elk with fences and built **artificial** beaver **dams**. In January 2024 they reported that **eroding** streams had cut so deep that **stunted** willows' roots no longer reached water.

In January 2025 Oregon State's William Ripple, an **advocate** of the **hypothesis**, reported one of the world's strongest cascades; critics **disputed** his methods. The question is **unresolved**, but on present evidence the wolves' shadow looks fainter than the **fable** told, and the science **messier**. A **discipline** willing to **revise** its **cherished** example may deserve more trust than any **fairy** tale.

## 中文

黄石公园那些**标志性的**(iconic)灰狼，到1926年已遭**赶尽杀绝**(exterminated)。1995年1月12日，八只在加拿大艾伯塔省**捕获**(captured)的狼装在**板条箱**(crates)里，一路**拖运**(hauled)到这里；随后又来了六只。它们在**围栏**(enclosures)里一直待到3月下旬，才被放出来，在**荒野**(wilderness)中**四处游荡**(roam)。

2014年2月，一段由英国作家乔治·蒙比奥特解说的视频在网上**疯传**(viral)，**编织**(spun)出一个**引人入胜的**(compelling)**故事**(narrative)。片中说，麋鹿数量变少，又处处**提防**(wary)，便**撤离**(retreated)了河谷，于是**柳树**(willows)**繁茂**(flourish)起来，河岸也**稳固**(stabilise)下来。生态学家把这称为营养**级联**(cascade)，也就是沿食物链层层传递的效应。**捕食者**(predators)吃掉或**吓阻**(deter)食草动物，**植被**(vegetation)便**恢复生长**(rebounds)。

然而，**普查**(census)结果先与第一条说法**相悖**(contradicted)。黄石北部草场的麋鹿在1994年1月达到约19,000头的峰值，狼还没来就已**日渐减少**(dwindling)；**猎人**(hunters)、**干旱**(drought)和熊不断**削减**(depleted)着鹿群。“恐惧”一说也站不住。2013年6月，阿瑟·米德尔顿领导的怀俄明大学团队发现，园区东侧**戴项圈的**(collared)麋鹿照样**吃草**(grazing)，除非狼**闯**(ventured)进半英里以内。

2021年11月，犹他州立大学的伊莱恩·布赖斯指出，只**抽样测量**(sampling)最高的**山杨**(aspens)，会把它们的**再生**(regrowth)程度**夸大**(overstated)四到七倍。这个故事更根本的**漏洞**(flaw)出在水上。自2001年起，科罗拉多州立大学的汤姆·霍布斯和戴维·库珀就用围栏把麋鹿**挡在外面**(excluded)，并修建**人工**(artificial)海狸**坝**(dams)。2024年1月，他们报告说，**不断冲蚀**(eroding)河床的溪流下切太深，那些**矮小的**(stunted)柳树根已经够不着水了。

2025年1月，这一**假说**(hypothesis)的**倡导者**(advocate)、俄勒冈州立大学的威廉·里普尔发文称，黄石的级联效应位居世界最强之列；批评者则对他的方法提出**质疑**(disputed)。问题仍**悬而未决**(unresolved)，但就目前的证据看，狼投下的影子比**寓言**(fable)里讲的淡，科学本身也**纠缠不清**(messier)得多。一门肯**修正**(revise)自己**珍视的**(cherished)案例的**学科**(discipline)，或许比任何**童话**(fairy)故事都更值得信赖。
