# 第05篇 来源摘录（2026-10-01，WebSearch 摘要；编号与 brief 一致）

W1 NPS Yellowstone《RG01 Wolf》https://www.nps.gov/yell/learn/historyculture/upload/RG01-Wolf.pdf ；Greater Yellowstone Coalition timeline https://greateryellowstone.org/yellowstone-wolf-reintroduction
- "On January 12, 1995, eight gray wolves from Alberta, Canada ... first to live there since they were extirpated in 1926 ... Seven days later, six more wolves arrived ... total to 14."
- "released from their pens ... during the last two weeks of March."
- "in January 1996, 17 new wolves ... from British Columbia."
- 1914–1926: at least 136 wolves killed in the park.

W2 NPS Yellowstone Science 24-1 "The Challenge of Understanding Northern Yellowstone Elk Dynamics after Wolf Reintroduction" https://www.nps.gov/yell/learn/ys-24-1-...htm
- record 19,045 elk counted January 1994; December 1994 count ~2,254 fewer (decline began before wolves).
- "many factors (e.g., predation, hunting, and drought) likely contributed."

W3 Middleton et al. 2013 (University of Wyoming; Ecology Letters — 期刊名单一来源，慎用) — National Parks Traveler 2013-06; Discover Magazine
- Clarks Fork herd (east of park, Wyoming), 2008–2010; GPS collars on ~90 female elk and 15 wolves.
- elk rarely encountered wolves; did not move/stop eating unless wolves within about half a mile; no link between encounter rate and pregnancy or body fat.

W4 Brice, Larsen & MacNulty, "Sampling bias exaggerates a textbook example of a trophic cascade", Ecology Letters 2022 (online November 2021; ScienceDaily 2021-11-13; PMC9298920)
- sampling only the tallest young aspen overestimated regeneration by a factor of 4–7 vs random sampling; random sampling still showed a cascade, but weaker.

W5 Hobbs, Cooper et al., Ecological Monographs, January 2024 (CSU NREL news); Cooper & Hobbs experiment since 2001 (with USGS)
- factorial experiment: fences (exclude elk) × simulated beaver dams (raise water); willows on northern-range small streams.
- streams incised from ca. 1950, 20–30 years after beavers abandoned dams; reducing browsing alone does not bring back the old state.
- conclusion quoted: restoration of apex predators "should no longer be held up as evidence of a trophic cascade in riparian plant communities of small streams on the northern range."
- 2025 Painter et al. (Ripple, Beschta camp) reply: cascade real if weaker; dispute ongoing.

W6 "How Wolves Change Rivers", Sustainable Human, 2014, narrated by George Monbiot, 4 min 33 s; >45 million views (cross-platform; 数字慎用)。月份未核（多见 2014 年 2 月），不写月份。

W7 Defenders of Wildlife Wolf Compensation Trust — Animal People 2010-07; defenders.org
- since 1987, ~$1.4 million to ranchers in six states; 1,301 cattle, 2,431 sheep, 108 other animals.
- announced 20 August 2010 it would stop most payments from 10 September 2010 (states took over).
