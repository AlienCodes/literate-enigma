# EN: The Fingerprint in the Blood

In November 1983 a 15-year-old girl was murdered in the Leicestershire village of Narborough. The **homicide** went **unsolved**, and the **investigation** **stagnated**. In the summer of 1986 a second 15-year-old was killed near **neighbouring** Enderby. Then a 17-year-old local youth **confessed** to the second **slaying**, and the **detectives'** **deadlock** was **seemingly** **resolved**.

On 10 September 1984, Alec Jeffreys, a **geneticist** at the University of Leicester, had **perceived** **stripes** on an X-ray **image**. Their **molecular** **motifs** **varied** from person to person, offering a way of **discerning** one person's DNA from another's. He called them DNA fingerprints, and **heralded** the **genetic** **milestone** in Nature in March 1985.

Police **enlisted** Jeffreys, and his **profiling** **dismantled** the **confession**. The teenager was not the **perpetrator**, and the same man had killed both girls. The young man **consequently** became the first person known to be cleared by DNA evidence. **Forensic** **biochemistry** had **sheltered** an **innocent** before it **apprehended** anyone.

In January 1987 police **initiated** the world's first mass DNA screening, asking more than 5,000 men in three villages to give blood or **saliva**. None **corresponded**. The **elusive** killer, a local baker, had **induced** a **co-worker** to give a sample in his name. The **substitution** **persisted** until August 1987, when someone **overheard** the colleague **gossiping** and **notified** police.

Colin Pitchfork was **detained** on 19 September 1987, and his DNA matched. In January 1988 he **pleaded** guilty and was sentenced to life imprisonment, the first person convicted of murder on the basis of DNA evidence. **Casual** **chatter**, not the **massive** **sieve**, had **undone** him. The test had cleared a **self-confessed** **juvenile** before it **nailed** a murderer.
