# EN: The Fingerprint in the Blood

In November 1983 a 15-year-old girl was murdered in the **tranquil** Leicestershire village of Narborough, and the **homicide** went **unsolved**. In the summer of 1986 a second 15-year-old was **slain** near **neighbouring** Enderby. A 17-year-old local youth admitted the second killing. To **weary** **detectives**, the **perplexing** **investigation** seemed **resolved**.

The **revelation** had come **quietly**. At about nine o'clock on the morning of 10 September 1984, Alec Jeffreys, a **geneticist** at the University of Leicester, **gazed** at an X-ray film. Its **molecular** patterns **diverged** **noticeably** from person to person. He called them DNA fingerprints, a **robust** way of telling one person's DNA from another's. Nature **duly** carried his **influential** paper in March 1985.

**Investigators** **enlisted** him, and his tests **overturned** a **credible** **confession**. The youth was not the **perpetrator**, and the same **attacker** had killed both girls. The teenager became the first person known to be cleared by DNA evidence. **Ironically**, the **innovation** had **defended** **innocence** before **exposing** **guilt**.

In January 1987 police began the world's first mass DNA screening, asking more than 5,000 men in three villages for blood or **saliva** samples. The **endeavour** proved **futile**. The **murderer**, a **baker**, had **induced** a colleague to **contribute** a sample in his name. The **deceit** **persisted** until August 1987, when someone **overheard** the colleague and **notified** police.

Colin Pitchfork was **detained** on 19 September 1987, and his **profile** **corresponded**. In January 1988 he **pleaded** guilty and was **jailed** for life, the first person convicted of murder on the basis of DNA evidence. The test had **unmasked** a **killer**, yet its first **beneficiary** was a **blameless** youth who had **confessed**.
