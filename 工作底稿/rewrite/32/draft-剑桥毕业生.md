# EN: The Fingerprint in the Blood

In November 1983 a 15-year-old girl was killed in the Leicestershire village of Narborough, and the **homicide** went **unsolved**. In summer 1986 a second 15-year-old was **slain** near **neighbouring** Enderby. **Dread** **gripped** the **tranquil** **locality**. Then a 17-year-old local youth **confessed** to the second killing, and the **investigation** **seemingly** ended.

Earlier, on 10 September 1984, Alec Jeffreys, a **geneticist** at the University of Leicester, had **discerned** on an X-ray film **molecular** **motifs** whose **configuration** varied **distinctly** from person to person. He called them DNA fingerprints, a way of telling one person's DNA from another's. His **groundbreaking** **revelation** appeared in the **renowned** **periodical** Nature in March 1985.

Police **enlisted** Jeffreys, whose tests showed the youth was not the **murderer**. A **lone** **attacker** had killed both girls. Despite his **confession**, the youth became the first person known to be cleared by DNA evidence. The **forensic** **innovation** had **implicated** no one, but **unequivocally** **contradicted** an **erroneous** confession.

In January 1987 police began the first **large-scale** DNA screening, asking more than 5,000 men in three villages for blood or **saliva**. None matched. The **perpetrator**, a baker, had **induced** a colleague to give a sample as his **proxy**, **thereby** **evading** **suspicion**. In August 1987 someone **overheard** the colleague discussing the **fraudulent** **substitution** and **notified** police.

Colin Pitchfork was **apprehended** on 19 September 1987, and his DNA **profile** **corresponded**. In January 1988 he **pleaded** guilty and was **jailed** for life, the first person convicted of murder on the basis of DNA evidence. An **informant**, not the screening, had **unmasked** him. The test's first **beneficiary** had been a **juvenile** whose only **accuser** was himself.
