# EN: The Trial of the Father of the Atomic Bomb

A New Yorker born on 22 April 1904, J. Robert Oppenheimer was a physicist of **considerable** **stature** at Berkeley and Caltech. In October 1942 General Leslie Groves asked him to **oversee** a **top-secret** **weapons** **facility**. It opened at Los Alamos in April 1943, **marshalling** a **workforce** of thousands. The bomb was a **collective** **achievement**.

Before **dawn** on 16 July 1945 the first nuclear test, Trinity, **generated** roughly 20 kilotons of **explosive** force in the **desolate** New Mexico desert. On 6 and 9 August **fission bombs** **flattened** Hiroshima and Nagasaki. By the end of 1945 the **death toll** was well over 100,000 and about 70,000 **respectively**.

After Oppenheimer's **resignation** in October 1945, his **misgivings** **persisted**. In October 1949 the **influential** **advisory** committee he chaired **unanimously** **counselled** against a crash programme to build the **hydrogen** bomb. In December 1953 President Eisenhower issued a **directive** cutting him off from **sensitive** material.

From 12 April to 6 May 1954 Oppenheimer **underwent** a security hearing, an **administrative** **proceeding**, not a criminal trial. On 29 June the Atomic Energy Commission voted 4 to 1 not to restore his clearance, two days before his **consulting contract** **expired**. The ruling did not find him **disloyal** or accuse him of **leaking** secrets. It **nevertheless** questioned his character and associations.

On 2 December 1963 President Johnson **bestowed** the Fermi Award on Oppenheimer, a **gesture** of **reconciliation**. He died of throat cancer on 18 February 1967, aged 62. On 16 December 2022 the US energy secretary **vacated** the 1954 **judgement**, **declaring** the process flawed and **in breach of** the commission's own rules. The **redress** reached a man fifty-five years dead.
