# EN: The Trial of the Father of the Atomic Bomb

J. Robert Oppenheimer, born in New York City on 22 April 1904, held **dual** physics posts in **academia** at Berkeley and Caltech. In October 1942 General Leslie Groves **engaged** him to head a **secretive** weapons **facility**. Los Alamos opened in April 1943, its **weaponry** the **collaborative** **creation** of a **workforce** of thousands he **supervised**.

In the **pre-dawn** **darkness** of 16 July 1945, the first nuclear test, Trinity, **illuminated** the **desolate** New Mexico **landscape**, releasing roughly 20 kilotons of **destructive** power. On 6 and 9 August, **atomic** bombs **devastated** Hiroshima and Nagasaki. In the **aftermath**, **fatalities** by the end of 1945 numbered well over 100,000 and about 70,000 **respectively**.

Resigning that October, he **presided** from 1947 over Princeton's Institute for Advanced Study. In October 1949 his **advisory** committee **unanimously** **counselled** against a crash programme for the **hydrogen** bomb. His **misgivings** **bred** **mistrust**. In December 1953 President Eisenhower **decreed** a **barrier** between him and **sensitive** **documentation**.

From 12 April to 6 May 1954, a security hearing, not a **courtroom** **prosecution**, examined his **reliability**. On 29 June the Atomic Energy Commission voted 4 to 1 against restoring his clearance, his **authorisation** for **confidential** material, two days before his consulting contract **expired**. Alleging neither **disloyalty** nor **leaks**, it **emphasised** **reservations** about his character and **associates**.

On 2 December 1963 President Johnson **bestowed** on him the Fermi Award, **sanctioned** by President Kennedy. He died of throat cancer on 18 February 1967. On 16 December 2022 the US energy secretary **vacated** the 1954 **judgement**, deeming the **proceeding** **defective** and **contrary** to the commission's rules. The **revocation** had **persisted** fifty-five years beyond his **lifetime**.
