# EN: The Trial of the Father of the Atomic Bomb

J. Robert Oppenheimer, born in New York City on 22 April 1904, was an **accomplished** physicist and **prominent** **lecturer** at Berkeley and Caltech. From April 1943 he **oversaw** a **secretive** **weapons** laboratory at Los Alamos, whose bombs were the **collaborative** **creation** of a **thousands-strong** **workforce** he **supervised**.

In the **pre-dawn** **darkness** of 16 July 1945, the first nuclear test, Trinity, **illuminated** the **desolate** New Mexico **landscape** with roughly 20 kilotons of **explosive** force. On 6 and 9 August 1945 atomic **bombings** **devastated** Hiroshima and Nagasaki, where year-end **fatalities** numbered well over 100,000 and about 70,000 **respectively**.

He resigned in October 1945 and from 1947 **presided** over Princeton's Institute for Advanced Study. In October 1949 the **advisory** committee he chaired, voicing **misgivings**, **unanimously** **counselled** against a crash programme for the **hydrogen** bomb, a **vastly** more **destructive** weapon. In December 1953 a presidential **directive** **severed** his access to **confidential** **documentation**.

From 12 April to 6 May 1954 he was **cross-examined** at a security hearing, an **administrative** **proceeding**, not a criminal **prosecution**. On 29 June 1954 the Atomic Energy Commission voted 4 to 1 against letting him **regain** his clearance, two days before his **consultancy** contract was due to **expire**. The ruling did not find him **disloyal**, but **nonetheless** questioned his character and **associates**.

On 2 December 1963 President Johnson **bestowed** on him the Fermi Award, which Kennedy had **sanctioned** before his **assassination**. He died on 18 February 1967, aged 62. On 16 December 2022 the energy secretary **officially** **vacated** the 1954 **judgement**, deeming the process **defective** and **contrary** to the commission's own rules. The **revocation** had **persisted** fifty-five years beyond his **lifetime**.
