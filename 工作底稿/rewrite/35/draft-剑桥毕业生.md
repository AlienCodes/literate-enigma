# EN: The Trial of the Father of the Atomic Bomb

Born in New York City on 22 April 1904, J. Robert Oppenheimer was an **accomplished** physicist and **prominent** **lecturer** at Berkeley and Caltech. In October 1942 he was asked to lead a **secretive** **weapons** laboratory, opened at Los Alamos in April 1943. Its **thousands-strong** **workforce** **rendered** the bomb a **collective** **achievement**, not **solely** his.

Before **dawn** on 16 July 1945 the first nuclear test **illuminated** the **arid** New Mexico desert with roughly 20 kilotons of **explosive** force. **Atomic** **bombings** **devastated** Hiroshima and Nagasaki on 6 and 9 August, with year-end **fatalities** of well over 100,000 and about 70,000 **respectively**. In October 1945 he resigned, from 1947 **presiding** over an **influential** Princeton institute.

Victory **bred** **misgivings**. In October 1949 the **advisory** committee he chaired **unanimously** **counselled** against a crash programme for the **hydrogen** bomb, a **vastly** more **destructive** weapon. In December 1953 President Eisenhower issued a **directive** **severing** his access to **confidential** material.

From 12 April to 6 May 1954 he was **cross-examined** at a security hearing, an **administrative** **proceeding**, not a criminal **prosecution**. On 29 June 1954 the Atomic Energy Commission voted 4 to 1 against letting him **regain** his clearance, two days before his **consultancy** contract would **expire**. It did not find him **disloyal**, but **nonetheless** questioned his character and **associates**.

On 2 December 1963 President Johnson **bestowed** on him the Fermi Award, which Kennedy had **sanctioned** before his **assassination**. Throat cancer killed him on 18 February 1967, aged 62. On 16 December 2022 the energy secretary **officially** **vacated** the 1954 **judgement**, citing **irregularities** that **infringed** the commission's own rules. His **rehabilitation** was **posthumous** by **fifty-five** years.
