# EN: The Letter That Startled Darwin

Darwin returned from HMS Beagle's voyage in October 1836. In September 1838, after reading Malthus, he began to **formulate** natural **selection**, the **principle** that individuals better **adapted** to their **surroundings** **survive** and **transmit** their traits. By 1844 he had **drafted** a 230-page **manuscript**, yet kept it **unpublished**, **amassing** **additional** evidence for some twenty years.

In February 1858 the **naturalist** Alfred Russel Wallace, working in the Malay Archipelago, **independently** **conceived** the same idea during a **bout** of fever. His **concise** essay reached Darwin on 18 June 1858. Its **conclusions** **paralleled** Darwin's almost **precisely**. Darwin wrote to his **mentor** Charles Lyell that he had never seen a more **striking** **coincidence**.

Lyell and Joseph Hooker **promptly** **arranged** a **compromise**. Both men's papers would be read **simultaneously** at the Linnean Society, a scientific society in London, on 1 July 1858. Neither **contributor** **attended**. Wallace remained across the **globe**, while Darwin was **mourning** his **infant** son, who had died of **scarlet** fever on 28 June.

The papers appeared on 20 August 1858 to a **muted** **reception**. In May 1859 the Society's president **remarked** in his **yearly** address that the year had brought no **revolutionary** **advances**. On 24 November 1859 Darwin's **treatise**, On the Origin of Species, was published. Booksellers had **eagerly** **pre-ordered** all 1,250 copies of the first **impression**.

Wallace raised no **objection**. In October 1858, a year before that **publication**, he **assured** Hooker of his **satisfaction** with how his paper had been **handled**. A **lengthy** **feud** over **priority** might have **ensued**. Instead, in 1889 he **entitled** his **exposition** of the theory Darwinism. Wallace put another man's name on the cover.
