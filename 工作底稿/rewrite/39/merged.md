# EN: The Octopus That Escaped

One night in early 2016, after **keepers** at New Zealand's National Aquarium in Napier had left, an octopus named Inky **crept** through an **aperture** in the **mesh** **atop** its tank. The **fugitive** **presumably** dropped to the floor, crossed the room and **compressed** its **elastic** body into a drain, whose **hollow** pipe **stretched** some 50 metres to the Pacific.

Staff **reconstructed** that route from the **slime** **residue** it **deposited**. Inky had **resided** in **captivity** since 2014, when a fisherman **retrieved** it from a crayfish **pot** off Napier. The **breakout** went **unreported** for about three months. Made public in April 2016, it travelled the **globe**.

Such **agility** is **feasible** because an octopus's **pliable** body has no **skeleton** or shell. Its only hard part is its **beak**, so any **crevice** that **accommodates** the beak will do. About two-thirds of its roughly 500 million **neurons** lie in the **limbs**, which handle **sensory** input and react with some **autonomy**.

The **wits** are as **versatile** as the **anatomy**. A giant Pacific octopus in Seattle learned to open screw-top **jars**, needing 15 minutes at the **outset**, later about two. In a 2010 study, octopuses came to **discriminate** between two strangers, a **feeder** and one who touched them with a bristly stick, **adopting** a different **hue** and **posture** for each.

In July 2012 the Cambridge Declaration on Consciousness listed octopuses among animals **possessing** the **neurological** **substrates** for **conscious** **awareness**. A November 2021 UK government review recommended treating them as **sentient**, and the Animal Welfare (Sentience) Act **encompassed** them in April 2022. None of this proves an **inner** life. Inky, somewhere beyond the pipe, lay **wholly** outside the Act's **jurisdiction**.
