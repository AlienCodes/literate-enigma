# EN: The Crowded Heavens
# ZH: 拥挤的苍穹
## 英文

On 10 February 2009, nearly 790 kilometres above Siberia, two satellites **slammed** together at about 42,000 kilometres an hour. Iridium 33 belonged to an American satellite-phone **constellation**; Kosmos 2251, a Russian military satellite, had been **derelict** since about 1995. Both **shattered** in the first **accidental** **collision** between two intact satellites.

Neither craft **swerved**. The Russian **hulk**, lacking **propulsion**, could not. A public collision-warning service had forecast a **narrow** miss of about 584 metres, yet the predicted **encounter** was not among the week's closest; such warnings were **frequent** and **imprecise**. One owner's **discarded** **relic** **demolished** another's working **asset**.

The **wreckage**, however, **endangered** everyone else. The **impact** **flung** out more than 2,000 **catalogued** fragments and a **multitude** of others too small to **pinpoint**. At that height air **drag** is **feeble**, so many would **linger** for decades. In January 2012 the International Space Station fired its **thrusters** to **dodge** an Iridium 33 **remnant**.

In June 1978 two NASA scientists **envisaged** a **vicious** **spiral**, later named the Kessler **syndrome**: each collision would **scatter** fragments, **raising the odds** of **subsequent** crashes. The 2009 crash **illustrated** that **premise** without **triggering** a **runaway** **chain reaction**. By the end of 2024 **surveillance** networks tracked about 40,000 objects, only about 11,000 of them **functional**.

Such a cascade would **unfold** over decades, though, and is not **inevitable**; it can be **mitigated**. Operators now **routinely** **manoeuvre** satellites **out of harm's way**. In September 2022 America's Federal Communications Commission, which licenses satellites, **mandated** **disposal** of low-orbit craft within five years of **retirement** instead of 25. Years after the crash, spacecraft whose owners had no part in it kept swerving around its **splinters**.

## 中文

2009年2月10日，在西伯利亚上空近790公里处，两颗卫星以约每小时42,000公里的速度**猛撞**(slammed)在一起。铱星33号隶属于美国的一个卫星电话**星座**(constellation)；宇宙2251号是一颗俄罗斯军用卫星，约自1995年起便已**废弃**(derelict)。在这起两颗完整卫星之间的首次**意外**(accidental)**相撞**(collision)中，两星双双**粉碎**(shattered)。

两颗卫星都没有**转向避让**(swerved)。那具俄罗斯**空壳**(hulk)没有**推进**(propulsion)装置，想躲也躲不了。一项公开的碰撞预警服务曾预测，两星将相距约584米**惊险**(narrow)擦过，但在当周的预测中，这次**交会**(encounter)并未排进距离最近的几次之列；这类预警既**频繁**(frequent)又**不精确**(imprecise)。一方**弃置的**(discarded)**遗物**(relic)，**撞毁了**(demolished)另一方仍在工作的**资产**(asset)。

然而，这些**残骸**(wreckage)却**危及**(endangered)了其他所有人。**撞击**(impact)**抛出**(flung)了2,000多块**已编目的**(catalogued)碎片，另有**大量**(multitude)其他碎片小到无法**精确定位**(pinpoint)。在那个高度，空气**阻力**(drag)**微弱**(feeble)，因此许多碎片会在轨道上**滞留**(linger)数十年。2012年1月，国际空间站启动**推进器**(thrusters)，**躲开**(dodge)了一块铱星33号的**残片**(remnant)。

1978年6月，两位美国国家航空航天局（NASA）的科学家**设想**(envisaged)了一种**恶性**(vicious)**螺旋**(spiral)，后来被称为凯斯勒**综合征**(syndrome)，即每次相撞都会**散布**(scatter)碎片，使**后续**(subsequent)撞击**更有可能发生**(raising the odds)。2009年的这次相撞为这一**前提**(premise)提供了**例证**(illustrated)，却并未**引发**(triggering)**失控的**(runaway)**连锁反应**(chain reaction)。截至2024年底，太空**监视**(surveillance)网络跟踪的物体约有40,000个，其中能**正常运行**(functional)的只有约11,000个。

不过，这样的连锁效应会在几十年的时间里逐步**展开**(unfold)，而且并非**不可避免**(inevitable)，也可以**加以缓解**(mitigated)。如今，运营方会**例行**(routinely)**操纵**(manoeuvre)卫星**避开危险**(out of harm's way)。2022年9月，负责为卫星发放许可的美国联邦通信委员会**强制要求**(mandated)低轨航天器在**退役**(retirement)后五年内完成**处置**(disposal)，而非原先的25年。相撞多年之后，那些主人与此事毫无瓜葛的航天器，仍不得不一再转向，绕开它留下的**碎屑**(splinters)。
