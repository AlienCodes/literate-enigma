# EN: The Man Who Gave Machines a Mind
# ZH: 为机器点燃思想的人

## 英文
Born in June 1912, Alan Turing had a **formidable** **intellect** by his early twenties. In a paper read in November 1936, he **outlined** a "universal machine": a **device** able to **execute** any **computation**, given the right instructions. That **abstract** **construct**, rooted in **mathematical** **logic**, became the **theoretical** **blueprint** of the modern computer.

On 4 September 1939 he joined the **classified** codebreaking effort at Bletchley Park, leading work on **naval** Enigma, Germany's **encryption** machine. Polish mathematicians had **deciphered** Enigma by late 1932 and shared their **insights** with Britain and France in July 1939. His team's machines **exploited** them and, **operational** from March 1940, **automated** the **laborious** search for each day's **rotor** settings.

In a paper published in Mind in October 1950, he **posed** a **provocative** question: "Can machines think?" He **proposed** a **practical** **substitute**, the imitation game, now **widely** called the Turing test. An **interrogator** exchanges typed messages with two **concealed** players, one human and one machine. If the interrogator cannot **consistently** **distinguish** them, the machine passes.

His **exceptional** **wartime** **contribution** did not protect him. On 31 March 1952 he was **convicted** of "gross indecency", the charge then used against gay men. He **forfeited** his security **clearance** and, to **avert** **imprisonment**, **submitted** to **hormone** **injections**. He died of cyanide poisoning on 7 June 1954. A **coroner**'s inquest ruled it **suicide**, though some doubt it.

**Recognition** **lagged**. On 10 September 2009 Britain's prime minister **publicly** **apologised**, and on 24 December 2013 Turing was **posthumously** **granted** a royal pardon. On 23 June 2021, his birthday, a fifty-pound note bearing his **portrait** entered **circulation**. The country that **prosecuted** him put his face on its money.

## 中文

艾伦·图灵生于1912年6月。二十出头时，他已拥有**超群的**(formidable)**才智**(intellect)。在1936年11月宣读的一篇论文中，他**勾勒**(outlined)了一种"通用机器"：只要给出恰当的指令，这种**装置**(device)就能**执行**(execute)任何**计算**(computation)。这一**抽象的**(abstract)**构想**(construct)植根于**数理**(mathematical)**逻辑**(logic)，后来成为现代计算机的**理论**(theoretical)**蓝图**(blueprint)。

1939年9月4日，他加入布莱切利园的**机密**(classified)破译工作，主持破解**海军**(naval)恩尼格玛密码机，即德国的**加密**(encryption)机器。早在1932年底，波兰数学家就已**破译**(deciphered)了恩尼格玛，并于1939年7月将他们的**破解思路**(insights)告知英法两国。他的团队研制的机器**利用**(exploited)了这些思路，从1940年3月起**投入运行**(operational)，把每天搜寻**转子**(rotor)设置这项**繁重的**(laborious)工作**自动化**(automated)了。

1950年10月，他在《心灵》（Mind）上发表论文，**提出**(posed)了一个**发人深省的**(provocative)问题——"机器能思考吗？"他**建议**(proposed)用一种**切实可行的**(practical)**替代办法**(substitute)取而代之，即"模仿游戏"，如今**普遍**(widely)称为"图灵测试"。一名**提问者**(interrogator)通过打字，与两个**隐藏起来的**(concealed)参与者交流，其中一个是人，一个是机器。如果提问者无法**始终如一地**(consistently)**分辨**(distinguish)二者，机器就算通过测试。

他在**战时**(wartime)**卓越的**(exceptional)**贡献**(contribution)并没有护住他。1952年3月31日，他被**判定**(convicted)犯有"严重猥亵罪"，这是当时用来惩治男同性恋者的罪名。他因此**失去**(forfeited)了安全**许可**(clearance)；为了**避免**(avert)**入狱**(imprisonment)，他还**接受**(submitted)了**激素**(hormone)**注射**(injections)。1954年6月7日，他死于氰化物中毒。**验尸官**(coroner)的死因调查裁定为**自杀**(suicide)，不过有人对此存疑。

**认可**(recognition)**来得迟缓**(lagged)。2009年9月10日，英国首相**公开**(publicly)**道歉**(apologised)；2013年12月24日，图灵在**身后**(posthumously)**获得**(granted)皇室赦免。2021年6月23日，也就是他的生日，印有他**肖像**(portrait)的50英镑钞票开始**流通**(circulation)。**起诉**(prosecuted)他的国家，把他的头像印上了自己的钞票。
