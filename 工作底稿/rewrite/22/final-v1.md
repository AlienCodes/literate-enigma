# EN: The Truth About One Marshmallow
# ZH: 一颗棉花糖的真相

## 英文

Picture a **nursery** school on Stanford's **campus** in the late 1960s and early 1970s. A child of about four or five sits in **solitude** with one treat and a **blunt** **bargain**: eat it now, or **resist** **temptation** for about 15 minutes and **earn** two. **Impulsive** children **surrender** **instantly**; **resolute** ones hold out.

**Follow-ups** made the test **legendary**. A 1990 study in Developmental Psychology found that longer waiters tended, as **adolescents**, to **attain** higher scores on the SAT, a US college-entrance exam. A **trivial** **snack** became an **emblem** of **virtue**, and **restraint** at four was **glorified** as a **predictor** of **destiny**.

Yet the **foundation** was **precarious**. Only a few dozen children entered the SAT analysis, **predominantly** the sons and daughters of Stanford **faculty** and **postgraduate** students. Such a **homogeneous**, **privileged** group could **scarcely** represent **ordinary** families, and **prosperous** homes may **foster** patience and grades alike.

A study published in Psychological Science in May 2018 ran a **conceptual** **replication**: the same idea, a new sample, **modified** methods. More than 900 **diverse** **youngsters** were **assessed** at four and a half and again at 15. The raw link was roughly half as strong as the original. **Adjusting** for family background and early **cognitive** ability shrank it by about two thirds, leaving it mostly **statistically** **insignificant**.

The test is not **worthless**; it may still **reflect** something **substantive**. A study published in Cognition in January 2013 adds **context**: children who had watched an adult **betray** a promise waited **markedly** less than **peers** who saw one kept. How long a child **perseveres** partly reflects how **trustworthy** the nearby adults seem.

## 中文

想象一下20世纪60年代末至70年代初斯坦福大学**校园**(campus)里的一所**幼儿园**(nursery)。一个大约四五岁的孩子**独自**(solitude)坐着，面前只有一份零食和一桩**直截了当的**(blunt)**交易**(bargain)：现在就吃，或者**抵挡**(resist)**诱惑**(temptation)大约15分钟，**挣得**(earn)两份。**冲动的**(impulsive)孩子**立刻**(instantly)**缴械投降**(surrender)；**意志坚定的**(resolute)孩子则坚持到底。

**后续研究**(follow-ups)让这项测验变得**家喻户晓**(legendary)。1990年发表于《发展心理学》（Developmental Psychology）的一项研究发现，等得更久的孩子到了**青少年**(adolescents)时期，往往在SAT（美国大学入学考试）中**取得**(attain)更高分数。一份**微不足道的**(trivial)**零食**(snack)成了**美德**(virtue)的**象征**(emblem)，四岁时的**克制**(restraint)被**美化**(glorified)为**命运**(destiny)的**预测指标**(predictor)。

然而，这一结论的**根基**(foundation)并不**牢靠**(precarious)。进入SAT分析的孩子只有几十个，**大多**(predominantly)是斯坦福**教职员**(faculty)和**研究生**(postgraduate)的子女。如此**同质**(homogeneous)而**优越的**(privileged)群体**很难**(scarcely)代表**普通**(ordinary)家庭，而且**富裕的**(prosperous)家庭可能同时**培养**(foster)出耐心与好成绩。

2018年5月发表于《心理科学》（Psychological Science）的一项研究做了一次**概念**(conceptual)**复制**(replication)：同一思路，新的样本，**调整过的**(modified)方法。900多名背景**多样的**(diverse)**孩子**(youngsters)在四岁半时接受**评估**(assessed)，15岁时再测一次。未经控制的相关强度大约只有原研究的一半。在**校正**(adjusting)家庭背景和早期**认知**(cognitive)能力之后，相关又缩小了约三分之二，在**统计上**(statistically)大多**不显著**(insignificant)。

这项测验并非**毫无价值**(worthless)；它或许仍能**反映**(reflect)某些**实质性的**(substantive)东西。2013年1月发表于《认知》（Cognition）的一项研究补充了**背景**(context)：目睹成人**背弃**(betray)承诺的孩子，比看到成人守诺的**同龄人**(peers)等待的时间**明显**(markedly)更短。一个孩子能**坚持**(perseveres)多久，部分取决于身边的成人看起来是否**值得信赖**(trustworthy)。

- 【T1/T2】20世纪60年代末至70年代初，斯坦福校园内幼儿园；约四五岁（文献口径3.5–5.5岁）；一份零食即吃，或独自等约15分钟（部分版本至20分钟）得两份。原实验零食不止棉花糖一种，正文写 one treat，不写"总是棉花糖"。属跨年时段，不标月。https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_marshmallow_experiment ；https://www.simplypsychology.org/?p=11458
- 【T3】追踪研究：1988年一篇在 Journal of Personality and Social Psychology（非 Developmental Psychology），正文只写 follow-ups，不点期刊；1990年 Developmental Psychology 26(6) 报告等待时长与 SAT 相关（正文以 tended to 对冲，写相关不写因果）。**1990年不写月份**：26(6) 期号对应 November 1990，但月份未在来源中直接核实，故只写年份。r≈0.42 不写。https://www.smith.edu/news/peake-on-marshmallow-test-studies/ ；https://scholarworks.smith.edu/psy_facpubs/79
