# Article 34 – Mona Lisa theft 1911: sources & excerpts
(All [search] = search-snippet only; nothing [fetched]. [ref] = memory, unsourced.)

S1 [search] https://www.historytoday.com/archive/months-past/mona-lisa-stolen-louvre — stolen 21 Aug 1911 by Vincenzo Peruggia, "Italian decorator who had briefly worked as a glazier at the museum"; hid in a storeroom Sunday, Monday closure, wore white workman's smock, hid painting under clothes.
S2 [search] https://www.pbs.org/wnet/secrets/mona-lisa-mystery-theft-mona-lisa-louvre/1769 — "On August 22, 1911, a local artist entered the Salon Carré planning to sketch the Mona Lisa ... found an empty space"; "The Louvre was closed for an entire week."
S3 [search] https://www.history.com/news/the-heist-that-made-the-mona-lisa-famous — trunk with a false bottom; Dec 1913 letter as "Leonard" to Florence dealer Alfredo Geri; Uffizi director Giovanni Poggi; hotel room, red silk; "on the afternoon of December 11, 1913, police arrested Peruggia at his hotel"; Apollinaire "arrested in September and questioned for a week"; Picasso next suspect; trial at Palazzo Vecchio began June 4, 1914; "one year and 15 days ... served just seven months"; record crowds viewed the empty space.
S4 [search] https://www.sortiraparis.com/en/what-to-visit-in-paris/history-heritage/articles/351380-... — Apollinaire arrested 7 Sept 1911, La Santé, released 13 Sept; link via former secretary Géry Pieret (statuettes stolen from Louvre 1907); Picasso, who had bought Pieret's Iberian statues, questioned, exonerated. (Also en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillaume_Apollinaire.)
S5 [search] https://www.historyhit.com/theft-mona-lisa/ ; https://www.sortiraparis.com/.../257946-... — returned to Louvre January 4, 1914; sentence 1 yr 15 days "later reduced to seven months" (one source says served ~8 months).
S6 [search] https://daily.jstor.org/mona-lisa-mystery ; https://www.thecollector.com/man-who-stole-mona-lisa-vincenzo-peruggia/ — Peruggia "believed that the painting had been stolen from Florence by Napoleon"; Leonardo took it to France when invited by Francis I (1516); after arrest displayed a week in the Uffizi, then shown in Italy before return Jan 1914.
S7 [search] https://www.npr.org/2011/07/30/138800110/the-theft-that-made-the-mona-lisa-a-masterpiece ; https://www.cllct.com/... ; https://www.euronews.com/2020/11/13/... — 1911 heist "transformed the Mona Lisa from one among many masterworks to the most famous painting in the world"; Noah Charney: "The theft is what really skyrocketed its appeal and made it a household name."
S8 [search] https://themillions.com/2013/09/nice-wall.html ; https://www.goethe.de/prj/geg/de/thm/kfk/25238081.html ; https://gekoski.com/lost-stolen-or-shredded/ — Kafka and Max Brod visited the Louvre (Sept 1911); Kafka diary: "Gedränge im Salon Carré, erregte Stimmung ... wie wenn die Mona Lisa gerade gestohlen worden wäre."
S9 [search] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduardo_de_Valfierno ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yves_Chaudron — Karl Decker, Saturday Evening Post, 25 June 1932, "Why and How the Mona Lisa Was Stolen"; six forged copies by Chaudron; only evidence is Decker's article; no copy ever surfaced.
S10 [ref] Accomplices Lancelotti brothers (Vincenzo & Michele) — suspected, not established; ~28 months is arithmetic (Aug 1911–Dec 1913).

## Myths
- Valfierno/Chaudron six-forgeries plot (Decker 1932) – unsupported, likely invented.
- Napoleon stole it from Italy – false; Leonardo took it to France, acquired by Francis I (Napoleon only hung it in his bedroom for a time).
- Picasso/Apollinaire stole it – false; both cleared.
- "Accomplices" Lancelotti – unproven.
