# EN: The Stolen Smile

On Monday 21 August 1911, a Louvre **closure** day, the Mona Lisa was **quietly** taken from its wall. The **disappearance** went **undetected** until the next day, when a painter, **intent** on a **sketch**, **confronted** a **conspicuous** **void**. The museum **shuttered** itself for a week.

At the **reopening**, **curious** **onlookers** **flocked** to **gaze** at the **unoccupied** space, an **unexplained** **emptiness**. On 7 September 1911 the **poet** Guillaume Apollinaire was **apprehended** and held in **custody** for about a week, while Pablo Picasso was **interrogated**. Both were cleared.

The **intruder**, Vincenzo Peruggia, was an Italian who had **formerly** been a Louvre glazier. Having **lurked** in a storeroom on the Sunday, he **exited** **unchallenged**, **clad** in a white workman's smock, the painting **tucked** **beneath** his clothes. In his Paris **lodgings**, a trunk with a false bottom kept it **undisturbed** for more than two years.

In December 1913 Peruggia **contacted** Alfredo Geri, an art **dealer** in Florence. The Uffizi's director **authenticated** the painting at Peruggia's hotel, and **detention** followed on 11 December 1913. Peruggia, a **self-proclaimed** **patriot**, wanted it back in Italy. He believed the **emperor** Napoleon had **pillaged** it. In fact Leonardo da Vinci himself had taken it to France at the **invitation** of the **monarch** Francis I.

After **viewings** in Italy, the painting was **reinstated** at the Louvre on 4 January 1914. In June 1914 a **tribunal** **imposed** a **penalty** of a year and 15 days, but Peruggia was **imprisoned** for about seven months. Many historians argue that, through the **affair**, one **admired** masterpiece among many became the world's most **renowned** painting. Its **notoriety** began at a **vacant** **stretch** of wall.
