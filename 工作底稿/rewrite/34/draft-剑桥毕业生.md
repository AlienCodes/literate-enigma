# EN: The Stolen Smile

On Monday 21 August 1911, when the Louvre was **customarily** closed, the Mona Lisa was **quietly** **plucked** from its wall. The **disappearance** went **unnoticed** until the next day, when a visiting painter found a **conspicuous** **gap** on the **plaster**. The **dismayed** **custodians** **shuttered** the museum for a week.

Once the museum **reopened**, **curious** **onlookers** **swarmed** to **stare** at the **vacant** space where the **masterpiece** had hung. Police eyed **bohemian** Paris. On 7 September 1911 the **poet** Guillaume Apollinaire was held in **custody** for about a week, and the painter Pablo Picasso was **interrogated**. Both men were cleared.

The **intruder** was Vincenzo Peruggia, an Italian who had **temporarily** worked at the Louvre as a glazier. Having **lurked** in a storeroom on Sunday, he **strode** out **unhindered** in a workman's smock, the painting **stashed** **beneath** his clothes. For more than two years it lay **discreetly** in a **trunk** with a **disguised** **compartment** in his **lodgings**.

In December 1913 Peruggia **contacted** Alfredo Geri, an art **dealer** in Florence. The Uffizi's director **authenticated** the painting at Peruggia's hotel, and Peruggia was **apprehended** on 11 December 1913. Citing **patriotism**, he said he wanted it **repatriated**, **mistakenly** believing Napoleon had **looted** it. In fact Leonardo da Vinci had **accompanied** it to France at the **invitation** of Francis I.

After **showings** in Italy, the painting was **reinstated** in the Louvre on 4 January 1914. Tried in June 1914, Peruggia served about seven months of a year and 15 days. Historians argue the **heist** **elevated** one **admired** **gem** among many into the world's most **renowned** painting. Its **fame** **stemmed** from a thief and a **bare** wall.
