# EN: The Stolen Smile

On Monday 21 August 1911, when the Louvre was **customarily** closed, the Mona Lisa was **discreetly** **plucked** from its wall. The **disappearance** went **unnoticed** until the next day, when a painter arriving to **sketch** found a **conspicuous** **gap** on the **plaster**. **Custodians** **shuttered** the museum for a week.

When the museum **reopened**, **curious** **onlookers** queued to **stare** at the **vacant** space where the **masterpiece** had hung. On 7 September 1911 the poet Guillaume Apollinaire was held in **custody** for about a week, and Pablo Picasso was **interrogated**. Both were cleared of **involvement** in the **burglary**.

The **intruder** was Vincenzo Peruggia, an Italian who had **temporarily** worked at the Louvre as a glazier. Having **lurked** in a storeroom on Sunday, he **strode** out **unchallenged** in a white workman's smock, the painting **tucked** **beneath** his clothes. For more than two years it lay **stashed** in a false-bottomed **trunk** in his Paris **lodgings**.

In December 1913 Peruggia **contacted** Alfredo Geri, an art **dealer** in Florence. The Uffizi's director **authenticated** the painting at Peruggia's hotel; Peruggia was **apprehended** on 11 December 1913. Citing **patriotism**, he wanted it **repatriated**, **mistakenly** believing Napoleon had **looted** it. In fact Leonardo da Vinci had **accompanied** it to France at the **invitation** of King Francis I.

After **showings** in Italy, the painting was **reinstated** in the Louvre on 4 January 1914. In June 1914 a **tribunal** **imposed** a **penalty** of a year and 15 days, but Peruggia was **imprisoned** for about seven months. Many historians argue the **heist** **elevated** one **esteemed** **gem** among many into the world's most **renowned** painting. If so, its **fame** began with a **bare** wall.
