# EN: The Stolen Smile

On Monday 21 August 1911, while the Louvre was **shuttered**, the Mona Lisa was **quietly** taken from its wall, and the **heist** went **altogether** **unnoticed**. A **painter** found the **gaping** **void** the next day. **Bewildered** **curators** **hastily** closed the museum for a week.

When the museum **reopened**, **curious** **onlookers** **patiently** queued to **gaze** at the **conspicuously** **bare** wall. On 7 September 1911 the **poet** Guillaume Apollinaire was arrested and held in **custody** for about a week, and Pablo Picasso was **interrogated**. Both were cleared of **involvement** in the **burglary**.

The **intruder** was Vincenzo Peruggia, an Italian who had **temporarily** worked at the Louvre as a glazier. Having **lurked** in a storeroom on the Sunday, he **strolled** out **unchallenged** in a workman's white smock, the painting **tucked** **beneath** his clothes. For more than two years it sat **stashed** in a false-bottomed **trunk** in his Paris **lodgings**.

In December 1913 Peruggia **resurfaced**, writing to Alfredo Geri, a Florence art **dealer**. The director of the Uffizi **authenticated** the painting at Peruggia's hotel, and on 11 December 1913 the culprit was **duly** arrested. He claimed a **patriotic** **motive**, **wrongly** believing Napoleon had **plundered** it. In fact Leonardo da Vinci took it to France at the **invitation** of the **monarch** Francis I.

**Paraded** in Italy, the painting was **safely** **reinstated** in the Louvre on 4 January 1914. A June 1914 **tribunal** **imposed** a **lenient** term of a year and 15 days, and Peruggia served about seven months. Many historians argue that its **disappearance** turned one **esteemed** masterpiece among many into the world's most **renowned** painting. Its **fame** began with a **vacant** wall.
