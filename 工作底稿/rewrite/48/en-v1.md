# EN: The Night Sweden Switched Sides

In a 1955 **referendum**, about 83 per cent of **voters** chose to keep driving on the left, an **overwhelmingly** clear **rejection** of change. On 10 May 1963, however, **parliament** **overruled** the **electorate**, approving right-hand traffic from 1967 in **defiance** of a **populace** whose **resistance** had made the plan **unpopular**.

The case rested on **conformity**. Norway and Finland drove on the right, and about five million vehicles crossed the border each year, so a left-hand **orientation** meant **isolation** and **inconvenient** journeys. Safety favoured **alignment** too. About 90 per cent of cars were left-hand drive, so **motorists** **overtaking** on narrow two-**lane** **highways** caused **head-on** collisions.

A state body ran the **mammoth** **preparation** under a **logo**, a large H with an arrow. Its **propaganda** included a song contest on television, and the winning **jingle**, whose **lyrics** urged people to keep right, reached fifth in the chart. Some 360,000 items of **signage** were changed, and a **massive** **fleet** of over 1,000 new buses was **procured** with right-hand doors.

The **transition** came on Sunday 3 September 1967. A **curfew** kept **non-essential** vehicles off the **asphalt** from one to six, with essential traffic **exempt**. At ten to five, all traffic stopped, **crawled** across and set off at five on **flipped** roads, before **dawn**. Stockholm and Malmö had a longer **closure**, from Saturday morning to Sunday afternoon, while crews reconfigured **junctions**.

On the following Monday, 125 accidents were reported, against 130 to 198 on earlier Mondays, a **statistical** gain put down to the **heightened** care of **hesitant** drivers. But the **nervousness** **subsided**, **prudent** **habits** **faded**, and by 1969 accident and fatality rates had **reverted** to their **baseline**.
