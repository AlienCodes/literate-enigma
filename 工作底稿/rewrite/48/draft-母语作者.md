# EN: The Night Sweden Switched Sides

In a 1955 **referendum**, the **electorate** **overwhelmingly** chose to keep driving on the left, with about 83 per cent of **voters** in favour. **Parliament** **overruled** them anyway. On 10 May 1963, **legislators** approved right-hand traffic from 1967, in open **defiance** of a **populace** whose **resistance** had made the plan **unpopular**.

Norway, Finland and the other neighbours drove on the right, with about five million vehicles crossing each year. **Isolation** was **inconvenient**, and **conformity** would **align** the roads with the region. About 90 per cent of **motorists** sat on the left, a poor **orientation** for **overtaking** on narrow two-**lane** roads, and **head-on** collisions followed.

A state commission ran the **mammoth** **preparation**, under a **logo** of a large H with an arrow. Its **propaganda** included a televised song contest, whose winning **tune**, urging Swedes to keep right, reached fifth in the chart. About 360,000 pieces of **signage** changed, and a **fleet** of over 1,000 new buses arrived with right-hand doors.

The **transition** came on Sunday 3 September 1967. A **curfew** **restricted** **non-essential** vehicles from one to six. At ten to five, all traffic stopped, **crawled** across the **asphalt** and moved off at five, before **dawn**. Stockholm and Malmö had a longer **closure**, from Saturday to Sunday, while crews rebuilt **intersections** for the **flipped** flow.

On the following Monday, 125 accidents were reported, against 130 to 198 on earlier Mondays. The **statistical** gain reflected **heightened** care from **hesitant** drivers. Iceland moved to **emulate** the **precedent** on 26 May 1968. But the **nervousness** **subsided**, **prudent** **habits** **faded**, and by 1969 accident and fatality rates had **reverted** to their **baseline**.
