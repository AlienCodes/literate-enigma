# EN: The Night Sweden Switched Sides

In a 1955 **referendum**, about 83 per cent of **voters** chose to keep driving on the left. The **electorate** had issued an **overwhelmingly** clear **rejection**. On 10 May 1963, however, **parliament** **overruled** the **populace** and approved right-hand traffic from 1967. The **legislators** passed an **unpopular** measure in **defiance** of such **resistance**.

The case rested on **conformity**. Norway and Finland drove on the right, and about five million vehicles crossed the border yearly, so a left-hand **orientation** meant **isolation** and **inconvenient** journeys. **Alignment** also bore on safety. About 90 per cent of cars were left-hand drive, so **motorists** **overtaking** on narrow two-**lane** **highways** faced **head-on** collisions.

A state body ran the **preparation** under a **logo**, a large H with an arrow. Its **propaganda** included a contest-winning **tune**, a **jingle** urging Swedes to keep right, which reached fifth in the chart. These **reminders** sought **cooperation** and **willingness**. Around 360,000 items of **signage** changed, a **massive** task, and over 1,000 buses had right-hand doors.

On Sunday 3 September 1967, a **curfew** banned **non-essential** vehicles from one to six, leaving only **exempt** traffic before **dawn**. At ten to five, vehicles stopped, **crawled** across and waited. The **transition** **flipped** the flow in ten minutes. Stockholm and Malmö banned traffic from Saturday to Sunday while crews rebuilt **junctions** and **intersections**.

On the following Monday, 125 accidents were reported, against 130 to 198 on earlier Mondays, a **statistical** gain attributed to **prudent**, **restrained** driving and **heightened** **diligence**. Iceland would **emulate** the **precedent** on 26 May 1968. The extra care proved **temporary**, for accident and fatality rates had **reverted** to their former **baseline** by 1969.
