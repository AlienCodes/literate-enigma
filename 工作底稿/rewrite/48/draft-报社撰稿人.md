# EN: The Night Sweden Switched Sides

In a 1955 **referendum**, about 83% of **voters** chose to keep driving on the left. The **rejection** was **overwhelmingly** clear, yet **parliament** **overruled** it. On 10 May 1963, **legislators** approved right-hand traffic from 1967, in **defiance** of the **electorate**. The **resistance** of the **populace** counted for less than the map.

Norway, Finland and the other land neighbours kept right, with about 5 million border crossings each year. That **isolation** was **inconvenient** for **motorists**, and **conformity** promised **alignment**. Left-hand **orientation** was dangerous. About 90% of cars on Swedish **highways** were left-hand drive, so **overtaking** on narrow two-**lane** roads caused **head-on** crashes.

The state **preparation** ran under a **logo**, a large H with an arrow. A televised **propaganda** contest crowned a **jingle** urging Swedes to keep right, and the **tune** reached fifth in the charts. In a **mammoth** act of **cooperation**, crews replaced about 360,000 pieces of **signage**. Over 1,000 buses with doors on the right became **massive** **reminders**.

On Sunday 3 September 1967, a **curfew** kept **non-essential** vehicles off the **asphalt** from 1am to 6am. Before **dawn**, at ten to five, **obedient** drivers stopped, **flipped** to the right and moved again at five. The **transition** demanded **compliance**, with essential traffic **exempt**. Stockholm and Malmö banned traffic from Saturday to Sunday afternoon while crews rebuilt **junctions**.

On the following Monday, 125 accidents were reported, against 130 to 198 on earlier Mondays. Analysts credit **heightened** **diligence** by **hesitant** drivers for the **statistical** fall. Iceland chose to **emulate** the change in May 1968. The **prudent** **habits** proved **temporary**, and by 1969 accident and fatality rates had **reverted** to their old **baseline**.
