# EN: The Last Flight of the Hindenburg

A **colossal** **aerial** **marvel** of **luxury** and **elegance**, the Hindenburg made 17 round trips across the Atlantic in 1936. Its **amenities** included a **lounge**, and a grand piano, removed for 1937, lent **glamour**. **Smokers** used an airlock with one electric lighter. An American **embargo** refused to **export** **helium**, so **combustible** hydrogen lifted the ship.

The airship left Frankfurt on 3 May 1937 with 97 people aboard. Hours behind its **timetable**, the **vessel** met **thunderstorms**, and the captain kept **hovering** for over an hour before the **descent**. At about 7:25 pm on 6 May, as it landed at Lakehurst, **flames** appeared near the **stern**. The **inferno** **dramatically** **incinerated** the ship in about half a minute.

Most of the 97 aboard **endured** the fire. Sixty-two survived, a **proportion** of almost **two-thirds**, and a **minority** died, 35 aboard and one man on the ground. A 14-year-old cabin boy was **drenched** when a water tank **burst** above him. **Soaked** and **bewildered**, he dropped through a **hatch** and **scrambled** clear.

Newsreel cameras and **photographers** recorded the disaster. The **eyewitness** **commentary** of a radio **reporter** was recorded, not live, and **listeners** heard it next day. The cause was never **definitively** established. The leading **hypothesis** holds that **static** **electricity** set **leaking** hydrogen alight. The paint theory is **discredited**.

**Ironically**, the USS Akron crash of 1933 killed more, 73 people. Flames before cameras **abruptly** wrecked **confidence** and the **image** of the airship **era**. As **perception** shifted, Graf Zeppelin was withdrawn and the **glamorous** **epoch** of passenger **aviation** ended. Its first voyage to North America had set out on 6 May, a year before that **fateful** evening.
