# EN: The Last Flight of the Hindenburg

In 1936 the **colossal** Hindenburg made 17 round trips across the Atlantic, all **luxury** and **glamour**. Its **aluminium** **frame** held a **lounge** and a grand piano, removed for 1937. **Smokers** sat behind an airlock with one electric lighter. Such **elegance** floated on **combustible** hydrogen, since a US **embargo** blocked the **export** of **helium**.

The airship set out from Frankfurt on 3 May 1937 with 97 aboard. Hours late, it kept **hovering** through **thunderstorms** over an hour. At about 7:25 pm on 6 May, in its **descent** towards the **mooring** **mast** at Lakehurst, **flames** appeared at the **stern**. The **inferno** took about half a minute, leaving it **crumpled** and **incinerated**.

Sixty-two of the 97 lived, close to **two-thirds**. A **minority** of 35 died aboard, with one man on the ground. Many **scrambled**, **leapt** or **dashed** clear. A 14-year-old cabin boy, a junior **steward**, was **drenched** when a water tank **burst** above him. **Soaked** and **bewildered**, he followed **instinct** through a hatch.

Newsreel cameras, **photographers** and **reporters** were waiting on the field. A radio reporter's **eyewitness** **commentary** was recorded, not live, and **listeners** heard it next day. The cause has not been **definitively** established. The likeliest account blames **static** **electricity** and **leaking** hydrogen. A theory about the **doped** outer **fabric** has been rejected.

The USS Akron crash had killed 73, yet the **image** weighed heavier. A **majestic** **giant** burning on camera wrecked **confidence** and **perception**, the Graf Zeppelin was grounded, and an **epoch** of **aviation** ended **abruptly**. **Ironically**, the first North American trip had set out from Frankfurt on 6 May 1936, a year before that **fateful** evening.
