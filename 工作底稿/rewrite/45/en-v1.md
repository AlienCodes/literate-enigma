# EN: The Last Flight of the Hindenburg

In 1936 the **colossal** Hindenburg made 17 Atlantic round trips, a **marvel** of **luxury** and **glamour**. Its **amenities** included a **lounge** and, that year, a grand piano. **Smokers** sat behind an airlock with one electric lighter, for the gas was **combustible**. An American **embargo** on **helium** **export** had left the ship relying on hydrogen.

The airship left Frankfurt on 3 May 1937. Hours behind its **timetable**, it met **thunderstorms** at Lakehurst and circled for over an hour before its **descent** towards the **mooring** **mast**. At about 7:25 pm on 6 May, **flames** appeared near the **stern**. The **inferno** **incinerated** the ship in about half a minute, leaving a **crumpled** **frame**.

Sixty-two of the 97 aboard survived, a **proportion** of almost **two-thirds**, while a **minority** died: 35 aboard and one man on the ground. A 14-year-old cabin boy was **drenched** by a **deluge** when a water tank **burst** above him, and the **soaking** probably saved him. He dropped through a **hatch** and **scrambled** clear, **bewildered** but alive.

Newsreel cameras and **photographers** caught the disaster. A radio **reporter**'s **eyewitness** **commentary** was not live; **listeners** heard it the next day. The cause has not been **definitively** established, but **static** **electricity** igniting **leaking** hydrogen is the leading explanation. A theory blaming the **doped** **fabric** has been rejected.

The 1933 crash of USS Akron killed 73 yet won less **notoriety**. Fire on camera left a **fateful** **image** that **abruptly** wrecked **confidence**. As **perception** soured, Graf Zeppelin was withdrawn, later **scrapped**, and a **glamorous** **epoch** of **aviation** closed. The ship's first journey to North America had begun on 6 May 1936, exactly a year before the fire.
