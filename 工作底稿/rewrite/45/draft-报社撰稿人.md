# EN: The Last Flight of the Hindenburg

Few machines carried more **glamour**. Across 1936 the **giant** airship made 17 Atlantic round trips, offering **elegance** in a **luxurious** **lounge**. A grand piano flew that year. **Smokers** sat behind an airlock with one electric lighter, for the gas was **combustible**. An American **embargo** on **helium** **export** left the **colossal** ship on hydrogen.

The airship left Frankfurt on 3 May 1937 with 97 aboard. Facing **thunderstorms**, it kept **hovering** over an hour before the **descent** to the **mooring** **mast** at Lakehurst. At about 7:25 pm on 6 May, **flames** appeared near the **stern**. The **ignition** became an **inferno**; in about half a minute the ship lay **incinerated** and **crumpled**.

Death claimed a **minority**. Sixty-two of the 97 aboard survived, a **proportion** near **two-thirds**, while 35 aboard and one man on the ground died. When a water tank **burst** above a 14-year-old cabin boy, the **soaking** left him **drenched**. Still **soaked**, he dropped through a hatch on **instinct**, **scrambled** up and **dashed** clear.

Newsreel cameras and **photographers** recorded the fire. A radio reporter's **eyewitness** **commentary** was recorded, not live, and **listeners** heard it the next day. The cause was never **definitively** established. The leading explanation is **static** **electricity** igniting **leaking** hydrogen. A theory that the **doped** **fabric** of the skin burned like **powder** has been rejected.

**Ironically**, the USS Akron killed more, 73 in 1933, yet **notoriety** followed this crash. The **fateful** **image** of fire on camera wrecked **confidence** and **perception** in **aviation**. Graf Zeppelin was **abruptly** withdrawn, and an **era** **faded**. The ship's first journey to North America also left Frankfurt on 6 May, exactly a year earlier.
