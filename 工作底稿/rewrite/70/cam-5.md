In late summer 1977, NASA **despatched** two Voyager probes. **Affixed** to each was a **gilded** copper **disc** thirty **centimetres** across, and the two were **duplicates**.

A committee chaired by Carl Sagan of Cornell **curated** this **eclectic** **compendium** **on behalf of** NASA. Greetings in 55 languages sit beside the **rumble** of **thunder**, whale song, **footsteps**, **laughter** and the **rhythm** of a **heartbeat**. Some ninety minutes of music span the **diversity** of cultures and eras, **notably** the **composer** Bach and Chuck Berry. An **array** of 115 images is **encoded** in analogue form, alongside printed messages from two **statesmen**, Jimmy Carter and Kurt Waldheim. The **sleeve** shows any **finder** how to play the disc, and a **diagram** uses pulsars as **navigational** **beacons** to locate the Sun.

Sagan had long argued for a **backward** **glance**, and his **persistence** prevailed. On 14 February 1990, Voyager 1 **accordingly** turned its camera across the **void** from some six billion kilometres. Sixty frames formed a **mosaic**, a family portrait of the solar system. Seen from **afar**, Earth was a **negligible** **glimmer** of about twelve hundredths of a pixel.

The **incoming** frames reached **receivers** **successively** between March and 1 May, with a **latency** of about five and a half hours across the **gulf**. Sagan's 1994 book Pale Blue Dot **meditated** on the image, reading in it humanity's tiny, **ephemeral** place amid the **vastness** of the cosmos.

Voyager 1 entered **interstellar** space in August 2012, the first human-made object to do so, and Voyager 2 followed in 2018. Both work on, though **depletion** of their **finite** **electricity** is expected within a few years.

Two copper discs carry those **audible** greetings **outward**, into the **emptiness** of a **realm** first entered in 2012.
