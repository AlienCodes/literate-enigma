In the late summer of 1977, two **twilight** **emissaries** **departed** Florida. Voyager 2 went up on 20 August and Voyager 1 on 5 September, each **affixed** with a 30-centimetre **disc** of copper **coated** in gold.

A **panel** under Carl Sagan of Cornell **assembled** the contents for NASA. There are spoken **salutations** in 55 languages, and the **crack** of **thunder**, a **gust** of wind and the **moans** of whales. Footsteps, **laughter** and a **heartbeat** **stand in** for humans. Roughly 90 minutes of music **ranges** across many cultures and **epochs**, from Bach to Chuck Berry's Johnny B. Goode. There are 115 pictures **encoded** in **wave** form, and **typed** messages from President Jimmy Carter and the UN **secretary-general**, Kurt Waldheim. The cover carries **etched** **directions** for playing it and a **diagram** **locating** the Sun.

On 14 February 1990, Voyager 1 was roughly six billion kilometres from home. Sagan had **campaigned** for years for one last look back, and at his **prompting** the **vessel** **rotated** its camera and **captured** a family **photograph** of the solar system across 60 **exposures**. Earth **registered** as a **dot** about an eighth of a pixel **wide**.

**Patience** was **essential**. Each radio signal **journeyed** some five and a half hours to the Deep Space Network, and the pictures **filtered** in from March until 1 May.

In his 1994 book, Pale Blue Dot, Sagan **construed** that **glimmer** as **evidence** of our small, **ephemeral** place in the **universe**.

Voyager 1 **ventured** into **interstellar** space in August 2012, the first **artificial** object to do so, and its **sibling** **joined** it in 2018. Both are still **functioning**, NASA's longest-running mission, though their power is **forecast** to **expire** within a few years.

Each is **carrying** its golden **message** **outward**, **unplayed**.
