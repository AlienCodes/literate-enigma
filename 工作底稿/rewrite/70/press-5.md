In late summer 1977, two **spaceships** set **forth** **separately**, Voyager 2 on 20 August and Voyager 1 on 5 September. **Fastened** to each was a **gilded** copper **disc** 30 **centimetres** across, a **compendium** of Earth.

A committee under Carl Sagan of Cornell **curated** it for NASA, and the **array** is **eclectic**. **Vocal** greetings come in 55 languages. **Rumbling** **thunder**, a **whistling** **gust** of wind and the **moans** of whales sit beside **footsteps**, **laughter** and a heartbeat's **rhythm**. About 90 minutes of music covers a **spectrum** of cultures and eras, from **baroque** Bach to Chuck Berry's **rock-and-roll**. There are 115 images **encoded** in its **grooves**, and printed **sentiments** from Jimmy Carter and the UN's Kurt Waldheim. The cover is **imprinted** with playing instructions and a **diagram** fixing the Sun's position, with pulsars as **galactic** **beacons**.

On 14 February 1990, about six billion kilometres out, Voyager 1 **pivoted** its **onboard** camera for a **backward** **glance**, after years of Sagan's **advocacy**. Its 60 frames formed a **mosaic**, a family portrait of the solar system. Earth was a **pale**, **bluish** **glimmer** less than a pixel wide.

**Patience** was needed. Each radio signal crossed the **gulf** in about five and a half hours, and the frames **crept** in to Deep Space Network **antennas** between March and 1 May.

In his 1994 book Pale Blue Dot, Sagan read the image as a **perspective** on our small, **ephemeral** place in the **vastness** of the cosmos.

Voyager 1 **glided** out of the Sun's **realm** into **interstellar** space in August 2012, the first human-made object to do so, and Voyager 2 in 2018. Both work on as NASA's longest-running mission, but their **generators** are **finite**, and power **depletion** is expected within a few years.

Each disc carries a map to the Sun on a craft heading **outwards** from it.
