# EN: The Planet That Was Demoted

On 18 February 1930, comparing **photographic** plates at the Lowell Observatory, the **astronomer** Clyde Tombaugh saw a **glimmer** **creeping** between **frames**. News broke on 13 March. An eleven-year-old English girl, Venetia Burney, offered the name Pluto, after the Roman **deity** of the **underworld**. For 76 years it held the ninth **slot** on the **planetary** **roster**.

In January 2005, astronomers found a **far-off** body, later named Eris, that at first seemed larger than Pluto. NASA **provisionally** **heralded** it as the tenth planet. The word had **hitherto** been used **loosely**, and with more **icy** worlds likely in the **outer** **realm**, a **definition** became a **necessity**.

On 24 August 2006, a **gathering** of the International Astronomical Union in Prague settled it by **ballot**. A planet must **revolve** around the Sun, be nearly **spherical** and have cleared the **neighbourhood** around its orbit. Pluto **stumbled** at that last **criterion**. **Reclassified** as a **dwarf** planet, it left a **lineup** of eight.

The vote was **valid**, the **turnout** thin. Only 424 **attendees** of the final day voted, out of some 10,000 members. Hundreds of **objectors** signed a **petition** of **dissent**. The **slang** verb "plutoed", meaning to **devalue**, became the American Dialect Society's word of 2006, and **lawmakers** in New Mexico and Illinois passed resolutions **declaring** Pluto a planet.

There was an **irony**. New Horizons, with a **pinch** of Tombaugh's ashes **onboard**, had been launched on 19 January 2006, seven months before the vote. On 14 July 2015 it **glided** past Pluto and **imaged** an **enormous** **heart-shaped** plain, **fittingly** named Tombaugh Regio. The **discoverer**'s ashes had come within sight of the heart that **commemorates** him.
