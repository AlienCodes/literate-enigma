# EN: The Planet That Was Demoted

On 18 February 1930, comparing **photographic** plates at the Lowell Observatory, the **astronomer** Clyde Tombaugh **perceived** a **dim** object **wandering** against a **backdrop** of stars, a find announced on 13 March 1930. An eleven-year-old English girl **nominated** the name Pluto, after the Roman **deity** of the **underworld**, and for 76 years it ranked **firmly** as the ninth planet.

That place went **uncontested** until January 2005, when astronomers found a far-off body, later named Eris, that at first seemed larger. NASA **provisionally** called the **challenger** the tenth planet. Yet the word had been used **loosely**, without a **precise** **criterion**, and the **dilemma** **spurred** the **profession**'s first formal **definition**.

On 24 August 2006, in Prague, the International Astronomical Union **decreed** that a planet must **revolve** around the Sun, be nearly **spherical** and have cleared the **neighbourhood** around its orbit. Pluto failed the third test, so the **assembly** **reclassified** it as a **dwarf** planet, leaving eight. Pluto was **physically** **unaltered**; only the **boundary** had moved.

The vote was **valid**, yet its **legitimacy** drew **dissent**. A **mere** 424 of some 10,000 members, those remaining on the final day, cast a **ballot**, and hundreds **petitioned** against it. The American Dialect Society made the **playful** "plutoed", meaning to **devalue**, its word of 2006, and **legislators** in New Mexico and Illinois **affirmed** Pluto as a planet.

**Ironically**, New Horizons had been launched on 19 January 2006, seven months before the vote, carrying some of Tombaugh's ashes. On 14 July 2015 the **unmanned** craft first flew past Pluto, **transmitting** **imagery** of an **enormous** **heart-shaped** **formation**, **fittingly** named Tombaugh Regio. The heart now **commemorates** Pluto's **discoverer**.
