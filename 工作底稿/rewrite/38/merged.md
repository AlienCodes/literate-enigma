# EN: The Planet That Was Demoted

On 18 February 1930, comparing **photographic** plates at the Lowell Observatory, the **astronomer** Clyde Tombaugh **perceived** a **dim** dot that had shifted between them. An eleven-year-old English girl **nominated** the name, after the Roman **deity** of the **underworld**. For 76 years Pluto held the ninth **slot** on the **planetary** **roster**.

In January 2005, astronomers found a **far-off** body, later named Eris, that at first seemed larger than Pluto. NASA briefly called it the tenth planet. The **contender** posed an **awkward** **dilemma**. The word had **hitherto** been used **loosely**, and with more **icy** worlds likely to be found, a **precise** **definition** became a **necessity**.

On 24 August 2006 the International Astronomical Union, **convened** in Prague, voted that a planet must **revolve** around the Sun, be nearly **spherical** and have cleared the **neighbourhood** around its orbit. Pluto failed that last **criterion** and was **reclassified** as a **dwarf** planet, leaving eight. Pluto itself was **physically** **unaltered**; only the **boundary** had moved.

The vote was **valid**, yet its **legitimacy** drew **criticism**: only 424 of some 10,000 members took part. Hundreds of **dissenting** astronomers signed a **petition**. The American Dialect Society made the **playful** **slang** verb "plutoed", to **devalue**, its word of 2006, and **lawmakers** in New Mexico and Illinois **affirmed** Pluto as a planet.

Amid the **controversy**, an **irony** was already in **transit**. New Horizons had been launched on 19 January 2006, carrying a **pinch** of Tombaugh's ashes. On 14 July 2015 the **unmanned** craft flew past Pluto, **transmitting** images of an **enormous** **heart-shaped** plain, **fittingly** named Tombaugh Regio. The **discoverer**'s ashes swept past the heart that bears his name.
