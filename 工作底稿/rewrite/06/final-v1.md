# EN: The Myth of the Unsinkable
# ZH: 永不沉没的神话
## 英文

On 10 April 1912 the **opulent** **liner** Titanic left Southampton for New York on her **maiden** voyage. A shipbuilding magazine had **dubbed** her "practically unsinkable". At about 11.40pm on 14 April she **collided** with an **iceberg**; by about 2.20am she had **vanished**, and the death **toll** would **surpass** 1,500.

**Folklore** blames **arrogance**. Yet the Titanic was **compliant**. Under 1894 rules, the Board of Trade, Britain's **maritime** **regulator**, **pegged** lifeboats to **gross** tonnage, its **scale** topping out at 16 boats above 10,000 tons. At about 46,000 tons she carried 20, **exceeding** the **requirement**. Their **capacity** was 1,178, **roughly** half the 2,200 aboard: **sufficient** in law, **inadequate** at sea.

The Californian, a steamer nearby, was **equally** **lawful**. Her **sole** **wireless** **operator**, Cyril Evans, was a Marconi **employee**, like most ships' operators. Around 11.30pm he switched off and went to bed. **Hindsight** may call that **negligence**, yet no rule **obliged** him to stay **vigilant** overnight, so the Titanic's **frantic** **distress** calls went **unheard**.

From April to July 1912 US senators and Lord Mersey, a British judge, heard **testimony** from **survivors**. In August American **legislation** made **round-the-clock** radio watch **compulsory**. On 20 January 1914 **delegates** of 13 countries signed the first Safety of Life at Sea **convention** in London, **stipulating** lifeboats for all, **continuous** radio watch and an ice **patrol**. The First World War **stalled** the **treaty**; the patrol went ahead **regardless**.

The **doomed** liner sank in under three hours. The **outdated** rules that let her sail with seats for half those aboard had stayed **afloat** for 18 years; it took some 1,500 deaths to **discredit** them.

## 中文

1912年4月10日，**豪华**(opulent)**邮轮**(liner)泰坦尼克号离开南安普敦，驶往纽约，开始**处女**(maiden)航。一家造船杂志曾把她**称作**(dubbed)“几乎永不沉没”。4月14日晚约11时40分，她与一座**冰山**(iceberg)**相撞**(collided)；到凌晨约2时20分，她已**不见踪影**(vanished)，遇难**人数**(toll)最终**突破**(surpass)1,500。

**民间传说**(Folklore)把灾难归咎于**傲慢**(arrogance)。然而泰坦尼克号是**合规**(compliant)的。按照1894年的规则，英国的**海事**(maritime)**监管机构**(regulator)贸易委员会把救生艇数量与**总**(gross)吨位**挂钩**(pegged)，其**等级表**(scale)最高一档只到“一万吨以上配16艘”。泰坦尼克号约46,000吨，带了20艘，**超出**(exceeding)了**要求**(requirement)。这些艇的**容量**(capacity)为1,178人，**大约**(roughly)只够船上2,200人的一半：按法律**足够**(sufficient)，到了海上却**不足**(inadequate)。

附近的轮船加州人号**同样**(equally)**合法**(lawful)。船上**唯一的**(sole)**无线电**(wireless)**报务员**(operator)西里尔·埃文斯是马可尼公司的**雇员**(employee)，和大多数船只的报务员一样。晚上11时30分左右，他关掉设备，上床睡觉。以**后见之明**(Hindsight)看，这或许算**疏忽**(negligence)，但没有哪条规则**责成**(obliged)他通宵保持**警觉**(vigilant)，于是泰坦尼克号**急切的**(frantic)**求救**(distress)呼叫**无人听见**(unheard)。

1912年4月至7月，美国参议员和英国法官默西勋爵听取了**幸存者**(survivors)的**证词**(testimony)。8月，美国一项**立法**(legislation)把**全天候**(round-the-clock)电台值守定为**强制**(compulsory)措施。1914年1月20日，13国**代表**(delegates)在伦敦签署了第一部《海上人命安全**公约**(convention)》，**规定**(stipulating)救生艇要够所有人乘坐，电台要**不间断**(continuous)值守，还要开展冰情**巡逻**(patrol)。第一次世界大战让这项**条约**(treaty)**搁浅**(stalled)；冰情巡逻却**照样**(regardless)开展起来。

这艘**在劫难逃的**(doomed)邮轮不到三小时就沉没了。**过时的**(outdated)规则允许她只带够一半人坐的救生艇出海，而这套规则已经**安然漂浮**(afloat)了18年；付出约1,500条人命的代价，才让它**信誉扫地**(discredit)。
