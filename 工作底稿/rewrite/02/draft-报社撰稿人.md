# EN: Four Sightings, Four Miles

On 5 December 1914 the **vessel** Endurance **departed** South Georgia, a **remote** **whaling** **outpost**, for the Weddell Sea, off Antarctica. By January 1915 she was **trapped** **in the grip of** its pack ice. On 27 October, her **hull** **buckling**, the men **abandoned** ship. She **vanished** beneath the ice on 21 November. Her captain, Frank Worsley, **logged** the spot.

For months the **stranded** men **endured** the ice. In April 1916 they **launched** three lifeboats and reached Elephant Island, **bleak**, **isolated** and far from any shipping lane. No one would come. On 24 April Shackleton and five others **ventured** out in the James Caird, a **fragile** lifeboat under seven metres, on a **perilous** **voyage** of some 800 nautical miles. **Navigation** was a **gamble**. Worsley **snatched** only four **fleeting** sightings of the sun. The rest was **dead reckoning**, **estimating** position from course, speed and time.

It **sufficed**. On 10 May they landed, **exhausted**, on South Georgia's **uninhabited** south coast. Shackleton, Worsley and Tom Crean **traversed** its **mountainous** **interior**, never before crossed, and reached the whaling station at Stromness on 20 May. Ice **repelled** three **rescue** attempts. On 30 August a Chilean navy **tug** **evacuated** the 22 men on Elephant Island. All 28 men of Endurance's party survived their **ordeal**.

On 5 March 2022 an **expedition** **located** the **wreck** 3,008 metres down, **upright** and **intact**. She lay about four miles south of where Worsley had **plotted** her 106 years earlier, a **discrepancy** **underscoring** his **accuracy**.

**Legendary** accounts **ascribe** survival to Shackleton's **charisma**, **justly**. Yet the **narrative** hangs on **meticulous** **calculation**. **Leadership** kept 28 men alive. **Precision** brought them **ashore**, and their ship back to light.
