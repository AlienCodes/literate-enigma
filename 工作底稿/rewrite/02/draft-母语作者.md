# EN: Four Sightings, Four Miles

On 5 December 1914 Endurance **departed** South Georgia, a **remote** **whaling** **outpost**, **bound** for the Weddell Sea, off Antarctica. By January 1915 pack ice had **trapped** the **vessel**. On 27 October, the ice **crushing** her **hull**, Shackleton ordered the men to **abandon** ship. Her captain, Frank Worsley, **logged** where she sank on 21 November.

The **stranded** men **endured** over five months on the ice. When it split on 9 April 1916, lifeboats **conveyed** them to **bleak**, **uninhabited** Elephant Island by 15 April. No one would search so **isolated** a place; help had to be **summoned**.

On 24 April Shackleton and five others **embarked** in the James Caird, a **fragile** lifeboat, on a **perilous** **voyage** of some 800 nautical miles. **Navigating**, Worsley **snatched** only four **sightings** of the sun, **estimating** the rest by **dead reckoning** from course, speed and time **elapsed**. On 10 May they came **ashore** on South Georgia's **deserted** south coast.

Shackleton, Worsley and Tom Crean **traversed** its **mountainous** **interior** in about 36 **exhausting** hours, reaching Stromness whaling station on 20 May. Ice **repelled** three **rescue** attempts before the Yelcho, a Chilean navy **tug**, **evacuated** the 22 **marooned** on Elephant Island on 30 August. All 28 of Endurance's party survived.

On 5 March 2022 the Endurance22 **expedition** **located** the **wreck** 3,008 metres down, **upright** and **remarkably** **intact**, about four miles south of the position Worsley recorded 106 years earlier.

The **saga** is told as a **tribute** to Shackleton's **legendary** **charisma**, and **justly**. Yet both ends **hinge on** Worsley's **meticulous**, long-**overshadowed** **arithmetic**. **Leadership** kept 28 men alive, but **precision** found them land in May 1916 and their ship in March 2022.
