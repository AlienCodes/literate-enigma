# EN: Four Sightings, Four Miles

On 5 December 1914 Endurance **departed** from South Georgia, a **remote** **whaling** outpost, **bound** for the Weddell Sea, off Antarctica. By January 1915 the **vessel** was **trapped** in pack ice. On 27 October, her **hull** **buckling**, Shackleton ordered his men to **abandon** ship. She **foundered** on 21 November; her captain, Frank Worsley, **logged** the spot.

The **stranded** crew **endured** months of **grim** **hardship** on the ice. In April 1916 they reached Elephant Island, so **desolate** and **isolated** that no one would come looking. On 24 April Shackleton and five others set out in the James Caird, a **fragile** lifeboat, on a **perilous** **voyage** of some 800 nautical miles. **Battered** by **ferocious** **gales**, Worsley **snatched** only four **sightings** of the sun; the rest was dead reckoning, **estimating** position from course, speed and time.

On 10 May they landed on South Georgia's **rugged**, **uninhabited** south coast. Shackleton, Worsley and Tom Crean **traversed** its **mountainous** interior and **staggered** into Stromness whaling station on 20 May. Ice **repelled** three **rescue** attempts before the Yelcho, a Chilean navy **tug**, **evacuated** the 22 **marooned** men on 30 August. All 28 of Endurance's party survived; three of the expedition's Ross Sea party did not.

On 5 March 2022 searchers **scouring** the Weddell Sea **located** the **wreck** 3,008 metres down, **upright** and **remarkably** **intact**. She lay about four miles south of Worsley's **reckoning**, a **slight** **discrepancy** for a **calculation** made on **shifting** ice.

Legend rightly **salutes** Shackleton's **charisma** and **resolve**. Yet both ends of the story **hinge** on one man's **meticulous** **arithmetic**. **Leadership** kept 28 men alive; **precision** **steered** them **ashore**, and their ship back into view.
