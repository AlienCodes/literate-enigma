# EN: The Language the Children Invented
## 英文
On **rattling** school buses in Managua, deaf pupils **chattered** in a **brisk**, **animated** **flutter** of **palms** their teachers could not **comprehend**. Until the late 1970s, Nicaragua had no deaf community. Most deaf people lived **secluded** lives, sharing **homemade** signs with **siblings** and parents.

In 1977 a special-education centre opened in San Judas with 50 deaf children. By 1983 it and a **vocational** school in Villa Libertad held over 400 pupils. The **curriculum** **emphasised** spoken Spanish and **lipreading**, and teachers signed **solely** through the **alphabet**. The **drill** brought **negligible** results, and most could not **digest** even the idea of a Spanish word.

At **recess**, in the street and the bus **aisle**, pupils **mingled** gestures with home signs. A **rough**, **haphazard** system appeared **rapidly**, then **blossomed** into a **coherent**, **rule-bound** language. Teachers found it **opaque**, so in June 1986 the Ministry of Education brought in Judy Kegl, an American Sign Language linguist from MIT.

A 2001 study traced the **momentum** to the young. Pupils who arrived younger, and later **intakes**, used **grammatical** devices more often and with greater **regularity** than older, earlier **signers**. Each class of small **entrants** added **richness**.

A 2004 Science paper showed how. Describing an object rolling down an **incline**, gesturing Spanish speakers, like the first signers, **bundled** manner and path into one **gliding** movement. Later signers **dismantled** it into **discrete** **constituents** in **linear** order, a **segmentation** absent from surrounding gestures.

Linguists call the episode an **unparalleled**, **real-time** view of a language in its **infancy**. It is **eloquent** evidence of a **vigorous** **predisposition** to **generate** grammar. The **machinery** of Nicaraguan Sign Language was **wrought** **unaided** by its young signers.
