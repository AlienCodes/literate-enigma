# EN: Life in the Forbidden Zone

Early on 26 April 1986, a **reactor** exploded at the Chernobyl plant in **Soviet** Ukraine, **dispersing** **radioactive** **fallout** across Europe. On 27 April, an **urgent** **evacuation** emptied Pripyat of about 45,000 **inhabitants** within hours. A sealed exclusion zone, roughly 30 kilometres in **radius**, was **imposed** around the plant, and Belarus fenced off **adjoining** **terrain**.

In October 2015, a study in the journal Current Biology **merged** **aerial** surveys with **footprint** tallies. On the Belarusian side, **elk**, roe deer, red deer and **boar** proved similar in **density** to four **contamination-free** reserves, with wolves about seven times as **numerous**. Earlier flights showed these **herds** **expanding** in the decade after the **meltdown**.

More **sightings** followed. In April 2016, a camera-trap survey **snapped** wolves, foxes, boar and raccoon dogs even beside the most **polluted** spots, with no sign of **suppressed** numbers. Przewalski's horses, a **threatened** Mongolian species, were **provisionally** **reintroduced** in 1998. The likely **explanation** seems **mundane**: no **agriculture**, **forestry** or hunting.

Other studies are **sobering**. In March 2007, **nesting** birds were fewer in species and number where **exposure** was higher. In March 2009, **pollinators** were less **plentiful** as radiation rose. In March 2014, **withered** **foliage** **decomposed** about 40 per cent less at the most **hazardous** sites, **impeding** the soil's **mineral** supply.

Counting heads cannot show whether **individual** animals are healthy, and how much damage low but constant doses **inflict** is still debated, since **rival** studies **clash** over methods. Human **departure** may **outweigh** the hazard. Although the wolves make that **dividend** **conspicuous**, the slow-rotting leaves keep an **unseen** **ledger** of the radiation's toll.
