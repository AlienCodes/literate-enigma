# EN: Life in the Forbidden Zone

Chernobyl, **synonymous** with **disaster**, holds a **startling** amount of **wildlife**. Before dawn on 26 April 1986, a **reactor** exploded in Soviet Ukraine and **radioactive** **fallout** **dispersed** across Europe. On 27 April, about 45,000 **inhabitants** of Pripyat were gone within hours. An **off-limits** **radius** of about 30 kilometres followed, and **neighbouring** Belarus fenced off more.

In October 2015, a study in the journal Current Biology combined **aerial** surveys with **footprints** on the Belarusian side. **Elk**, deer and **boar** proved **comparable** in **density** to four reserves free of **contamination**, with wolves about seven times as **numerous**. Elk and boar had **surged** in the accident's first decade.

Cameras **confirm** the **revival**. In April 2016, a **camera-trap** survey **snapped** wolves, foxes, boar and raccoon dogs at the most **toxic** spots, with no sign of **suppressed** numbers. **Reintroduced** in 1998, Przewalski's horses **wander** there too. Animals have **seemingly** **reclaimed** the land after human **withdrawal**: no **ploughs**, saws or **rifles**.

Some studies are **sobering**. In March 2007, one found fewer **nesting** birds, and fewer species, where radiation was **intense**. In March 2009, **pollinators**, dragonflies and spiders grew less **plentiful** as **exposure** rose. In March 2014, leaves at the most **polluted** sites showed about 40 per cent less **decomposition**, so **vital** nutrients **seep** back slowly.

A census counts heads. It cannot **assess** whether **individual** animals are healthy. How much damage low but **steady** doses do to **fauna** remains **contentious**, as studies **clash** over methods. However **conspicuously** the wolves **advertise** an empty **landscape**, the **sluggish** **rot** of leaves keeps a quieter **ledger** of what radiation costs.
