# EN: The Toad That Conquered a Continent
# ZH: 征服一片大陆的蟾蜍
## 英文

Greyback cane **beetles**, whose **larvae** **devour** cane roots, were **plaguing** Queensland's **lucrative** sugar **plantations**. In June 1935 a scientist **imported** from Hawaii 102 cane toads, **bulky** **amphibians** native to the Americas. That August about 2,400 of their young were **released**. **Biological** control, setting one **species** against a **pest**, had an **ambitious** new **recruit**.

The **hasty** plan met **opposition**. In November 1935 an insect expert, Walter Froggatt, **persuaded** federal officials to **halt** releases; after **lobbying** by Queensland, releases **resumed** in September 1936. The **scheme** **flopped**: the toads barely **dented** the beetles, perhaps because the insects kept high on cane **stalks**.

The toads **thrived** instead. With few **foes** and **fertile** females **spawning** thousands of eggs, they **multiplied** **unchecked**. Their **invasion** **frontier**, creeping about ten kilometres a year at first, **accelerated** to about 50 kilometres in the **tropical** north. In February 2009 the first known toad **breached** Western Australia's border.

**Toxin** **glands** in their skin make them a deadly meal. Goannas, large **lizards**, have **slumped** in places, as have freshwater **crocodiles** and northern quolls, cat-sized hunters; some local quoll populations were **wiped out**. The **havoc** is **uneven**; some species have **prospered**. In June 2010 researchers reported that young quolls that were fed toad meat **laced** with a **nausea**-**inducing** chemical learned to avoid toads.

Biological control can **triumph**. From 1926 a **moth**, Cactoblastis, cleared most prickly pear, an **invasive** **cactus**, from Queensland and New South Wales within about a decade. In October 1984 Australia passed a Biological Control Act; recruits now face **stringent** **scrutiny** before release. The **blunder** of 1935 was **haste**: once loose, an **untested** ally may never be **recalled**.

## 中文

灰背蔗**甲虫**(beetles)的**幼虫**(larvae)**啃食**(devour)甘蔗根，正**困扰**(plaguing)着昆士兰州**利润丰厚的**(lucrative)甘蔗**种植园**(plantations)。1935年6月，一位科学家从夏威夷**引进**(imported)了102只甘蔗蟾蜍，这种原产美洲的**两栖动物**(amphibians)**体型粗壮**(bulky)。同年8月，它们的约2,400只后代被**放归**(released)野外。**生物**(Biological)防治，即用一个**物种**(species)去对付一种**害虫**(pest)，就此迎来了一名**雄心勃勃的**(ambitious)**新兵**(recruit)。

这个**草率的**(hasty)计划遭到了**反对**(opposition)。1935年11月，昆虫专家沃尔特·弗罗格特**说服**(persuaded)联邦官员**叫停**(halt)放归；经昆士兰州**游说**(lobbying)，放归于1936年9月**恢复**(resumed)。这套**方案**(scheme)**以失败告终**(flopped)：蟾蜍几乎没有**撼动**(dented)甲虫，或许是因为这些昆虫一直待在甘蔗**茎秆**(stalks)的高处。

蟾蜍自己反倒**兴旺**(thrived)起来。**天敌**(foes)寥寥，**能生育的**(fertile)雌蟾每次**产下**(spawning)数千枚卵，它们**不受遏制地**(unchecked)**大量繁殖**(multiplied)。它们**入侵**(invasion)的**前锋**(frontier)起初每年约缓缓推进十公里，到了**热带**(tropical)北部，**加快**(accelerated)到每年约50公里。2009年2月，已知的第一只蟾蜍**突破**(breached)了西澳大利亚州的边界。

它们皮肤里有分泌**毒素**(Toxin)的**腺体**(glands)，吃下去便是致命的一餐。巨蜥这种大型**蜥蜴**(lizards)在一些地方数量**锐减**(slumped)，淡水**鳄**(crocodiles)和猫一般大小的猎手北袋鼬也是如此；部分地方的袋鼬种群被**彻底消灭**(wiped out)。这场**浩劫**(havoc)的轻重**因地而异**(uneven)；有些物种反而**繁盛**(prospered)起来。2010年6月，研究人员报告，幼年北袋鼬吃了**掺有**(laced)能**引起**(inducing)**恶心**(nausea)的药剂的蟾蜍肉后，学会了避开蟾蜍。

生物防治也能**大获成功**(triumph)。从1926年起，一种名叫仙人掌螟的**蛾子**(moth)在约十年内清除了昆士兰州和新南威尔士州的大部分刺梨，这是一种**外来入侵的**(invasive)**仙人掌**(cactus)。1984年10月，澳大利亚通过了《生物防治法》；如今，新兵要先经过**严格的**(stringent)**审查**(scrutiny)才能放归。1935年的**大错**(blunder)在于**仓促**(haste)：**未经检验的**(untested)盟友一旦放出，就可能再也**召不回来**(recalled)。
