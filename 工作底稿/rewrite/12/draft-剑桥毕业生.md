# EN: The Toad That Conquered a Continent

A **stubborn** **pest** **plagued** Queensland's sugar **crop**: the greyback cane **beetle**, whose grubs **devour** cane roots. In June 1935 a sugar-industry scientist **imported** 102 cane toads from Hawaii; that August about 2,400 of their young, **hatched** in **captivity**, were **released**. The **scheme** was **biological** control: **enlisting** one **species** against another.

In November 1935 Walter Froggatt, an insect expert, **persuaded** federal officials to **halt** releases; Queensland **lobbied**, and releases **resumed** in September 1936. Yet the toads barely **dented** beetle numbers. One common **explanation** is that the beetles stayed high on cane **stalks** while toads hunted below.

**Unchecked**, the toads **thrived**, with few **foes** and females laying thousands of eggs at once. As they spread west from Queensland, their **frontier** **accelerated** from about ten kilometres a year to about 50 in the **tropical** north.

Toads carry a **potent** **toxin** in skin **glands**, often killing native predators that **consume** them. Goannas (large **lizards**), freshwater **crocodiles** and northern quolls (cat-sized meat-eaters) proved **vulnerable**, crashing in places; local quoll populations were **wiped** out. **Impacts** vary; some **prey** species have **prospered**. A June 2010 study offered a partial **safeguard**: after eating toad meat **laced** with a **nausea**-**inducing** chemical, **juvenile** quolls learned to avoid toads.

Biological control is no **folly**. From 1926 the Cactoblastis **moth** cleared most **invasive** prickly pear **cactus** from Queensland and New South Wales within about a decade, a widely **acclaimed** **triumph**. In October 1984 Australia passed a Biological Control Act; proposed agents now face years of **stringent** testing. The fault lay not in the method but in the **sequence**: release first, **scrutiny** a distant second.
