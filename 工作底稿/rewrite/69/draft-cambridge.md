# EN: The Stink That Built London's Sewers
# ZH: 大恶臭逼出了下水道

## 英文

In the **oppressive** summer of 1858, **statesmen** in the new Houses of Parliament sat behind curtains soaked in chloride of **lime**. The **bleaching** powder was an **expedient** to make the **pungent** Thames **tolerable**.

The river was the **outlet** for the **untreated** **sewage** of some three million Londoners, **discharged** through **antiquated** sewers straight into it. In the **baking** heat, the **festering** **excrement** **generated** an **overpowering**, **sickening** smell, remembered as the Great Stink.

Members **contemplated** an **exodus**. On 15 July Benjamin Disraeli, the **Chancellor** of the Exchequer, **characterised** the river as a **hellish** pool **brimming** with **unbearable** horrors. With sudden **urgency**, a bill funding new sewers became law in about eighteen days.

The engineer Joseph Bazalgette **masterminded** some 82 miles of **mighty** intercepting sewers, which **diverted** the **filthy** **slurry** before it reached the river and **funnelled** it east, **downstream** of the **populous** city. **Underneath** the **roadways** ran about 1,100 miles of smaller **tributaries**, the whole requiring some 318 million **bricks**. He worked out the **breadth** each **sewer** needed, then doubled it. The main **collectors** lie **buried** under the new Victoria, Albert and Chelsea Embankments. The system was **substantially** complete by 1875, and much of it is in use today.

Most doctors held the **misguided** **belief**, known as miasma, that disease spread through **tainted** air. The sewers were designed chiefly to **purge** the **stinking** **fumes**. Their gain for **hygiene** came almost **accidentally**, by keeping sewage out of the water Londoners drank.

**Verification** came **starkly** in 1866. Cholera killed about 5,600 people in the East End, the only **locality** not yet joined to the system. No major cholera epidemic followed in the **metropolis**.

## 中文

1858年那个**闷热难耐**(oppressive)的夏天，新落成的议会大厦里，**政治家们**(statesmen)坐在浸过氯化**石灰**(lime)溶液的窗帘后面。这种**漂白**(bleaching)粉是一种**权宜之计**(expedient)，为的是让**刺鼻**(pungent)的泰晤士河变得**尚可忍受**(tolerable)。

这条河是约三百万伦敦人**未经处理**(untreated)的**污水**(sewage)的**出口**(outlet)，污水经由**老旧过时**(antiquated)的下水道直接**排放**(discharged)进河里。在**烤人**(baking)的酷热中，**溃烂发酵**(festering)的**粪便**(excrement)由此**产生**(generated)出一股**压倒一切**(overpowering)、**令人作呕**(sickening)的气味，这段往事被称为"大恶臭"。

议员们**考虑过**(contemplated)集体**出走**(exodus)。7月15日，**财政大臣**(Chancellor)本杰明·迪斯雷利把这条河**描述**(characterised)为一池**地狱般**(hellish)的死水，其中**满是**(brimming)令人**难以忍受**(unbearable)的恐怖。政府以**紧迫**(urgency)之势推动，一项为新下水道拨款的法案约18天就成了法律。

工程师约瑟夫·巴泽尔杰特**主持设计**(masterminded)了约82英里**宏大**(mighty)的截流下水道，在**污秽**(filthy)的**泥浆**(slurry)流入河中之前将其**截走**(diverted)，**汇集**(funnelled)起来送往东边、这座**人口稠密**(populous)的城市的**下游**(downstream)。**马路**(roadways)的**下方**(Underneath)，约1,100英里较小的**支线**(tributaries)纵横延伸，整个工程共用约3.18亿块**砖头**(bricks)。他算出每条**下水道**(sewer)所需的**宽度**(breadth)，然后再加一倍。主干**汇流管**(collectors)被**掩埋**(buried)在新修的维多利亚、阿尔伯特和切尔西河堤之下。整个系统到1875年**基本**(substantially)完工，其中大部分至今仍在使用。

当时多数医生持有一种**错误的**(misguided)、名为瘴气说的**观念**(belief)，认为疾病通过**污浊**(tainted)的空气传播。下水道主要是为了**清除**(purge)那股**发臭**(stinking)的**浊气**(fumes)而建。它们对**公共卫生**(hygiene)的贡献几乎是**意外**(accidentally)得来的，靠的是污水不再进入伦敦人喝的水。

1866年，**验证**(Verification)以**冷酷**(starkly)的方式到来。霍乱在伦敦东区夺去约5,600人的生命，东区正是唯一尚未接入该系统的**地区**(locality)。此后，这座**大都市**(metropolis)再没有发生大规模霍乱。

## 史实与来源
- 【T1】到1858年，约三百万伦敦人的污水经老旧下水道直接排入泰晤士河（英文 outlet、untreated sewage、discharged through antiquated sewers straight into it）；1858年夏天炎热（oppressive、baking heat），腐烂的污水散发出难以忍受的恶臭（festering excrement、overpowering, sickening smell），史称"大恶臭"。S1
- 【T2】新议会大厦的窗帘浸了氯化石灰（漂白粉，英文 bleaching powder）来压住臭味（expedient to make … tolerable）；议员们考虑过搬离（contemplated an exodus）。1858年7月15日，财政大臣迪斯雷利把这条河形容为冥河般、充满难以忍受之恐怖的臭池；英文以 characterised … hellish pool brimming with unbearable horrors 转述，无直接引语。拨款修建新下水道的法案约18天成为法律（With sudden urgency 对应来源 rushed through）。S2
- 【T3】巴泽尔杰特修建约82英里大型截流下水道（mighty 对应 large；masterminded 对应其主持设计），在污水入河前截住、送往城东下游（diverted、funnelled it east, downstream）；另有约1,100英里街道下水道（smaller tributaries，比喻支线）；全系统用砖约3.18亿块（the whole requiring）；他算出所需管径（breadth）后再加倍；主干管（main collectors）埋在新修的维多利亚、阿尔伯特和切尔西河堤之下；1875年基本完工（substantially complete），大部分至今仍在使用。S3
- 【T4】当时主流为"瘴气说"，认为疾病经污浊空气传播；英文写 Most doctors held，与第01篇 Most doctors attributed epidemics to miasma 一致；misguided 对应本文反转"瘴气说是错的"。下水道主要为除臭而建（chiefly to purge the stinking fumes）；奏效是因为污水不再进入伦敦人的饮用水；came almost accidentally 概括"为除臭而建、却因护水奏效"这一反转。S4
- 【T5】1866年霍乱在伦敦东区夺去约5,600人生命，东区是唯一尚未接入截流系统的地区（the only locality not yet joined to the system）；此后伦敦再无大规模霍乱（No major cholera epidemic followed in the metropolis）。Verification 指这次霍乱印证了上段"护水"的道理。克罗斯内斯泵站（1865年4月启用）未写入正文。S5
- 【说明】正文无直接引语；中文不添英文以外的信息。结尾不含 stink / built / london / sewers，不以禁用词开头。
