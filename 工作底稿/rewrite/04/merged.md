# EN: A Harvest of Many Hands

In July 1964, **inspecting** a **paddy** at Anjiang Agricultural School in Hunan province, the **instructor** Yuan Longping **glimpsed** a **peculiar** plant. It was male-**sterile**, **shedding** no working **pollen**. Rice pollinates itself, so breeding **hybrid** seed in **bulk** depends on such plants. In February 1966 he **detailed** the **anomaly** in the **journal** Chinese Science Bulletin.

The **specimen** **launched** a **hunt** it never finished: for years his **colleagues** could not **breed** a sterile line whose **offspring** **reliably** **inherited** the **trait**. The **breakthrough** came on 23 November 1970, in a **marsh** near Nanhong Farm in Sanya, on the island of Hainan. Yuan's assistant, Li Bihu, and Feng Keshan, a farm **technician**, found wild rice with **withered** pollen.

Yuan's **decisive** move came next: rather than **withhold** the **strain**, he **distributed** it to **collaborators** **nationwide**. Hybrids need three **distinct** lines: one sterile, one to **maintain** it, one to **restore** **fertility** in the offspring. In the south, Yan Longan's team in Jiangxi bred sterile lines and Zhang Xiancheng in Guangxi first **identified** a restorer line. In October 1973, at a meeting in Suzhou, near Shanghai, Yuan declared the system complete.

Large-scale **cultivation** began in 1976, on about 140,000 **hectares**. The World Food Prize **estimates** hybrids of this **staple** **yield** up to a fifth more **grain** than **conventional** rice. By the early 2000s they **occupied** almost half of China's rice **acreage**.

Yuan, who shared the 2004 prize with Sierra Leone's Monty Jones, died in Changsha, Hunan, on 22 May 2021, aged 91. **Myth** **portrays** him as a **solitary** **genius**. His eye began the hunt; his **generosity** let many hands bring in the **harvest**.
