# EN: A Harvest of Many Hands

In July 1964, **inspecting** a **paddy** at Anjiang Agricultural School in Hunan province, the **instructor** Yuan Longping **encountered** a **peculiar** plant that **shed** no **viable** **pollen**. Rice pollinates itself, so **crossbreeding** on a field **scale** needs such a plant. In February 1966 his paper on the **anomaly** appeared in the **journal** Chinese Science Bulletin.

The **specimen** began a **quest** it could not finish: for years his **colleagues** tried in **vain** to **breed** a line that **inherited** the **trait**. The **breakthrough** came on 23 November 1970, in a **swamp** near Nanhong Farm, on the southern island of Hainan. Yuan's assistant Li Bihu and Feng Keshan, a farm **technician**, **waded** in and found wild rice whose pollen had **withered**: Ye Bai, or "wild **abortive**".

Yuan's **decisive** move came next. Rather than **withhold** the **strain**, he **distributed** it to **collaborators** **nationwide**. In the south, Yan Longan's group in Jiangxi bred **sterile** lines, and Zhang Xiancheng in Guangxi first found a line able to **restore** **fertility** in the **offspring**. In October 1973, at a national meeting in Suzhou, near Shanghai, Yuan declared the system complete: a sterile line, one to **maintain** it, and Zhang's restorer.

By 1976 large-scale **cultivation** had begun. The World Food Prize **estimates** **hybrid** rice can **yield** up to a fifth more **grain**; by the early 2000s it **occupied** almost half of China's rice **acreage**.

Yuan died in Changsha, Hunan's capital, on 22 May 2021, aged 91. **Tributes** **portray** him as a **lone** **genius**, a **myth** **overlooking** his **generosity**. One man's eye began the **saga**; the **harvest** came because he **entrusted** that wild rice to many hands.
