# EN: A Harvest of Many Hands

In July 1964, **inspecting** a **paddy** at Anjiang Agricultural School in Hunan province, a teacher named Yuan Longping noticed a **peculiar** **specimen**. It was male-**sterile**, bearing no working **pollen**. Rice fertilises itself, so large-**scale** **crossbreeding** **hinges** on such a plant. In February 1966 he **detailed** the find in the **journal** Chinese Science Bulletin.

The **anomaly** proved hard to **exploit**. Years of **fruitless** work produced no sterile **strain** whose **offspring** **reliably** **inherited** the **trait**. The **breakthrough** came on 23 November 1970, near Nanhong Farm in Sanya, on the southern island of Hainan. Yuan's assistant, Li Bihu, and Feng Keshan, a farm **technician**, found wild rice in a **marsh**, its pollen **withered**. It was **dubbed** Ye Bai, "wild abortive".

Yuan's next move was **equally** **decisive**. Rather than **withhold** Ye Bai, he **distributed** it to **collaborators**, **transforming** research into a **nationwide** **relay**. In Jiangxi, a southern province, Yan Longan's team **bred** sterile lines; in Guangxi, Zhang Xiancheng **isolated** the first restorer line, which **restores** **fertility** to the **hybrid**. In October 1973, at a rice conference in Suzhou, near Shanghai, Yuan declared the three-line system complete.

Large-scale planting began in 1976, on about 140,000 **hectares**, then **expanded** **steadily**. The World Food Prize says hybrid rice can **boost** **yields** of this **staple** by up to a fifth. By the early 2000s it covered almost half of China's rice **acreage**.

Yuan shared that prize in 2004 with Sierra Leone's Monty Jones. He died in Changsha, Hunan's capital, on 22 May 2021, aged 91. **Myth** **portrays** him as a **solitary** **visionary**; the **harvest** came because he **entrusted** his most **precious** **asset** to many hands.
