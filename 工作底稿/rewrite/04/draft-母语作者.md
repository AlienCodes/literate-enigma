# EN: A Harvest of Many Hands

In July 1964 Yuan Longping, then teaching at Anjiang Agricultural School in Hunan province, **glimpsed** a **peculiar** plant while **inspecting** a **paddy**. It was male-**sterile**: its flowers **shed** no **functional** **pollen**. Because rice pollinates itself, producing **hybrid** **seed** in **bulk** **hinges** on such plants. In February 1966 he **detailed** the **anomaly** in the **journal** Chinese Science Bulletin.

The **hunt** then **stalled**: for years his team could not **breed** a sterile **strain** that **reliably** **transmitted** the **trait** to its **offspring**. The **breakthrough** came on 23 November 1970, in a **swamp** near Nanhong Farm in Sanya, on the southern island of Hainan. There Yuan's assistant Li Bihu and Feng Keshan, a farm **technician**, found wild rice with **withered** pollen.

Yuan's **pivotal** move was to **distribute** the find to **collaborators** **nationwide**. Hybrid rice needs three lines: one sterile, one to **maintain** it, one to **restore** **fertility** in the offspring. In the southern provinces of Jiangxi and Guangxi respectively, Yan Longan's team bred sterile lines and Zhang Xiancheng first **identified** a restorer line. In October 1973, at a national rice meeting **convened** in Suzhou, near Shanghai, Yuan **declared** the system complete.

**Cultivation** **ensued**: by 1976 farmers were **sowing** about 140,000 **hectares**. The World Food Prize credits hybrid rice with **grain** **yields** up to a fifth higher than **conventional** varieties; by the early 2000s it covered almost half of China's rice **acreage**.

Yuan, who shared the 2004 prize, died in Changsha, Hunan, on 22 May 2021, aged 91. The **myth** of a **solitary** **genius** is **tempting** but **flawed**. His eye began the **harvest**; his **generosity** let many hands bring it in.
