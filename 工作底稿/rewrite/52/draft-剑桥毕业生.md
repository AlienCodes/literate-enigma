# EN: The Great Emu War

Campion, Western Australia, was farmed mainly by First World War **veterans**. These **settlers** held **marginal** land under a government plan, and most were **inexperienced** **growers**. Their **plight** was real, as a quarter had left their **parcels** by 1932. That year, about 20,000 emus came from the **arid** **inland**, and the **flocks** **trampled** wheat **crops**.

In **desperation**, a **delegation** of former soldiers voiced their **grievances** and requested **machine-guns**. The Defence Minister agreed. Instead of a **battalion**, the army sent a **detachment** under Major Meredith: two **gunners**, two Lewis guns and 10,000 rounds of **ammunition**. The **offensive**, a formal **assault**, began on 2 November 1932.

At a dam **ambush**, a dozen of a thousand birds fell before a gun **jammed**. Small, **agile** groups then defeated each **tactical** plan, and a truck-mounted gun failed on rough ground. By day six, 2,500 rounds had yielded 50 to 200 kills, a **futile**, **wasteful** effort. **Parliamentary** questions and press **ridicule** forced a **withdrawal** on 8 November, an **embarrassment**, though shooting soon restarted.

Meredith returned on 10 December. He reported 986 kills for 9,860 rounds, a **ratio** of ten **bullets** per bird. He compared the **toughness** of the emus to tanks, as if their **feathers** were **armour**. The **persistence** of such **resilient** targets made **warfare** look **ineffective**, with no clear **victor**.

Later requests for troops, in 1934, 1943 and 1948, were refused. A **bounty** **subsidy** instead **reimbursed** farmers per bird, a cash **incentive** met by **taxpayers**. In six months of 1934, over 57,000 **payments** were **redeemed**. Against 986 machine-gun kills, farmers' **rifles** and small **compensation** earned nearly sixty times the result of military **extermination**.
