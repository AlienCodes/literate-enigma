S1 Wikipedia "Emu War"; Britannica "Emu War": nuisance wildlife management operation by the Australian Army in late 1932 to cull emus damaging farmland in the Campion district, Wheatbelt of Western Australia; 2 Nov – 10 Dec 1932; commanded by Major G. P. W. Meredith, Royal Australian Artillery; planned with Sir George Pearce (Minister for Defence).
S2 Wikipedia: Campion farming dominated by First World War veterans settled on marginal land under a government scheme; inexperienced; one quarter had already abandoned their land by the time of the Emu War. A large drought-driven migration of roughly 20,000 emus caused heavy damage to wheat crops in 1932. A deputation of ex-soldiers asked for machine guns.
S3 National Geographic / History Hit: detachment = Meredith, two soldiers, two Lewis light machine guns, 10,000 rounds. Ambush at a dam where about a thousand emus approached; after about a dozen kills the Lewis gun jammed. A gun mounted on a truck proved useless: the truck was too slow and the ride too rough to fire.
S4 Wikipedia / History Hit: emus split into small, alert, highly mobile groups; operation uneconomic. By the sixth day about 2,500 rounds fired, kills estimated 50–200. Troops withdrawn on 8 November after the matter was raised in Parliament (House of Representatives) and press ridicule; operations resumed 12/13 November (sources differ — write "within days").
S5 Wikipedia: Meredith recalled 10 December; his report claimed 986 kills with 9,860 rounds (about ten rounds per bird) and that about 2,500 wounded birds later died. Meredith compared the birds' ability to absorb bullets to tanks (paraphrase only, no quote).
S6 Wikipedia / Retrospect Journal: later requests for military help (1934, 1943, 1948) refused; the government reverted to a bounty system, and over 57,000 bounties were claimed in six months of 1934.
