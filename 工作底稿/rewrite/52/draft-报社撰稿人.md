# EN: The Great Emu War

In 1932 the wheat **growers** of Campion, in Western Australia, met an unusual enemy. Most were First World War **veterans**, **settlers** handed **parcels** of **marginal** **farmland** under a government scheme. **Inexperienced**, a quarter had already quit. After a dry spell, some 20,000 emus moved **inland** in **flocks** and **trampled** the **crops**.

Their **plight** turned military. A **delegation** of ex-soldiers took their **grievances** to the Defence Minister, George Pearce, and asked for **machine-guns**. He agreed. Rather than a **battalion**, the army sent a **detachment**: Major Meredith, two **gunners** and two Lewis guns, and 10,000 rounds of **ammunition**. Firing began on 2 November.

At a dam, a thousand birds walked into an **ambush**, yet only a dozen fell before a gun **jammed**. The emus broke **formation** into small, **agile** groups. A truck-mounted gun proved **futile**, the truck too slow and bumpy. By the sixth day, **wasteful** firing had spent 2,500 rounds on perhaps 50 to 200 birds, a **ludicrous** return. After **parliamentary** questions and press **ridicule**, a **withdrawal** came on 8 November. Shooting restarted within days.

Meredith was recalled on 10 December. His **memo** gave an **inventory** of 986 kills from 9,860 rounds, a **ratio** of ten **bullets** per bird, plus 2,500 wounded that died later. He ranked the **hardy** birds' **toughness** and **endurance** beside that of tanks, as though **resilient** **feathers** made them **bullet-proof**.

Later **pleas** for military **intervention**, in 1934, 1943 and 1948, were refused. Officials turned to a **bounty**, a **premium** per bird serving as **compensation** and **incentive** for **extermination**. **Payments** mounted, and farmers with **rifles** **redeemed** over 57,000 in six months of 1934, against Meredith's 986.
