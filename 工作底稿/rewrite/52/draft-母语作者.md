# EN: The Great Emu War

Around Campion, Western Australia, the farmers were First World War **veterans**, settled by a government scheme on **marginal** **parcels** of **inland** **farmland**. They were **inexperienced** **growers**, and a quarter had already quit. In 1932, after drought, roughly 20,000 emus **swarmed** in from **arid** country in **flocks** and **trampled** the wheat **crops**.

The **desperate** ex-soldiers took their **grievances** to the government and asked for **machine-guns**. The Defence Minister agreed. What arrived was no **battalion** but a **detachment** of **gunners**: Major Meredith of the Royal Australian **Artillery**, two soldiers, two Lewis guns and 10,000 rounds of **ammunition**. The **bullets** began flying on 2 November, at **taxpayers**' expense.

At a dam, an **ambush** drew about a thousand birds. A dozen fell before a gun **jammed**. The emus broke **formation** into small, **agile** groups, a **tactical** puzzle. A truck-mounted gun proved **futile**. Six days and 2,500 rounds yielded 50 to 200 kills. After press **ridicule** and **parliamentary** questions, the troops made an **embarrassing** **withdrawal** on 8 November, returning within days.

Meredith was recalled on 10 December. He reported 986 kills for 9,860 rounds, a **ratio** of ten to one, plus some 2,500 wounded birds that died later. He compared their **toughness** to that of tanks, as if **feathers** were **armour** and the birds **bullet-proof**. Against such **resilient** **persistence**, this **absurd** **warfare** produced no **victor**.

Later **pleas** for **reinforcements** were refused, and military **intervention** gave way to a **bounty**. The **incentive** was a **token** sum per bird, and farmers **quietly** **redeemed** the **payments**. They claimed over 57,000 bounties in six months of 1934, a **frugal** **extermination** set against Meredith's 986.
