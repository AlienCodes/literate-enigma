## 英文

In 1984 Barry Marshall, a **thirty-two-year-old** physician at Fremantle Hospital, scraped two Petri dishes of **cultured** microbes into beef **broth** and **gulped** it down. He was **deliberately** **swallowing** the organism he blamed for **peptic** ulcers.

**Orthodox** medicine held that ulcers were caused by stress, **spicy** food and **excess** acid. **Conventional** **wisdom** also said no bacterium could **survive** in so **corrosive** a **stomach**.

The **heresy** began at Royal Perth Hospital, where the **pathologist** Robin Warren had seen **curved**, **spiral** bacteria in **biopsies** from patients with **gastritis**. Marshall, a young **clinician**, joined him. Attempts to grow the **bug** failed **repeatedly**. Then, over Easter 1982, **plates** sat in the **incubator** for about five days rather than the usual two, and **colonies** **sprouted**. The organism was later named Helicobacter pylori.

**Sceptics** were **unmoved**. In 1983 the pair's paper to the Gastroenterological Society of Australia was **rejected**, **rated** in the bottom ten per cent of that year's **submissions**. Efforts to **infect** **piglets** **flopped** too, so Marshall turned **guinea pig** himself.

Within days he was **vomiting**, **plagued** by bad **breath** and **exhausted**. An **endoscopy** about ten days later showed gastritis, an **inflammation** of the stomach **lining** and a **precursor** to ulcers, not an ulcer itself. The **culprit** was cultured from his stomach. At his wife's **urging** he took **antibiotics**, which **cleared** the **infection**. Ulcers came to be treated with antibiotics.

In 2005 Marshall and Warren shared the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for **discovering** the bacterium and its role in gastritis and peptic ulcer **disease**.

The **inflamed** stomach he **inflicted** on himself became part of the **evidence** for a **cure**.
