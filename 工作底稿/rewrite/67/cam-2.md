## 英文

In 1984 Barry Marshall, a **thirty-two-year-old** **physician** at Fremantle Hospital, **scraped** the **bacterial** growth from two Petri dishes into beef **broth** and drank the **murky** **mixture**. He was **willingly** **ingesting** the **germ** he blamed for ulcers.

The **prevailing** **doctrine** held that ulcers were caused by stress, **spicy** food and **surplus** **acid**. No bacterium, it was assumed, could **withstand** the **caustic** juices of the stomach.

The **dissent** began at Royal Perth Hospital, where Robin Warren, a **specialist** in **pathology**, had seen **curly**, **corkscrew** bacteria in tissue samples from patients with gastritis. Marshall, a young doctor, joined him. Attempts to grow the organism were **invariably** **unsuccessful**. Then, over Easter 1982, dishes sat in the **incubator** for about five days instead of the usual two, and **colonies** appeared. The microbe was later named Helicobacter pylori.

**Skeptical** **reviewers** were **unconvinced**. In 1983 the pair's paper to the Gastroenterological Society of Australia was **ranked** in the bottom ten per cent of that year's **entries**. Attempts to **infect** young pigs also **foundered**, so Marshall turned to his own body.

Within days he began to **vomit**, **suffered** from foul breath and was **fatigued**. An **endoscopic** **examination** about ten days later **revealed** gastritis, an **inflammation** of the stomach lining and an **incipient** stage of ulcer **formation**, not an ulcer. The **culprit** was **recovered** from his stomach. At his **spouse**'s **insistence** he took an **antibiotic**, which **purged** the infection. Ulcers came to be treated with antibiotics.

In 2005 Marshall and Warren shared the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for **identifying** the bacterium and its role in gastritis and **peptic** ulcer disease.

The **inflamed** **lining** of one doctor's stomach had become **testimony** in his own **defence**.
