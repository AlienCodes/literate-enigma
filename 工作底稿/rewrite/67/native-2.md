## 英文
In 1984 Barry Marshall, a **32-year-old** doctor at Fremantle Hospital, **scraped** the growth from two Petri dishes into beef **broth**. Then, quite **intentionally**, he drank the **mixture**.

The **prevalent** **doctrine** blamed ulcers on stress, **spicy** food and the excessive **secretion** of **acid**. No **bacterium**, the **profession** **firmly** held, could **withstand** so **caustic** a **habitat**.

Robin Warren, a pathologist at Royal Perth Hospital, had seen curved, **coiled** bacteria in stomach **biopsies** from patients with gastritis. Marshall, then a young doctor, joined him in a **collaboration**. Attempts to culture the **germ** proved **unsuccessful** until Easter 1982, when plates sat in the incubator for about five days. They were **ordinarily** **binned** after two. This time colonies appeared, and the organism was later named Helicobacter pylori.

In 1983 the pair submitted their **thesis** to the Gastroenterological Society of Australia. **Unimpressed**, its **assessors** placed the paper **squarely** in the bottom tenth of that year's **submissions**. Efforts to infect young **swine** **likewise** failed, so Marshall made himself the **guinea pig**.

The **onset** was swift. Within days he **vomited**, his breath had an unpleasant **odour** and **weariness** set in. About ten days later he **underwent** an endoscopy. It revealed gastritis, an **inflammation** of the stomach's **mucous** **membrane** and a **precursor** to ulcers. **Strictly**, he had not developed an ulcer. The organism was then cultured **directly** from his stomach.

At the **insistent** urging of his **spouse**, he took **antibiotics**, which cleared the infection. Ulcers, **similarly**, came to be treated with antibiotics.

In 2005 Marshall and Warren shared the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine. The citation named their discovery of Helicobacter pylori and its role in gastritis and peptic ulcer disease. The illness he had **willingly** given himself in 1984 is named in that citation.
