# EN: The Brain That Learned London

Name two addresses and a London cabbie will **rattle off** the quickest route **instantly**. That **prowess** is **hard-won**. To earn a **licence**, **aspiring** drivers must **conquer** "the Knowledge", an **arduous** **examination** on about 25,000 streets within six miles of Charing Cross. **Hopefuls** **typically** spend three to four years **committing** this **labyrinth** to memory, and only about half **qualify**.

A study published in PNAS in April 2000 **scanned** 16 male cabbies and 50 other men. The hippocampus is a seahorse-shaped structure **vital** to memory and **navigation**. In the cabbies, its rear, or **posterior**, **portion** was larger than in the other men, and its front, or **anterior**, portion smaller. Longer-serving cabbies had larger rear portions still.

Yet the link left the cause **uncertain**. Perhaps an **innate** **flair** for **spatial** tasks **lured** people to the **occupation**, or perhaps **stressful** driving was the **culprit**. A December 2006 study **dispelled** the second **notion**: cabbies had more rear grey matter, the tissue **dense** with **nerve** cells, than bus drivers of **equivalent** experience and stress **confined** to **predetermined** routes.

A December 2011 **longitudinal** study **tackled** the first, scanning 79 **trainees** and 31 non-trainees at **enrolment** and three to four years later. All began alike. Only the 39 who qualified **exhibited** an **expansion** of rear grey matter, so the **sequence** suggests learning **preceded** the **enlargement**.

The gain appeared to **entail** a **trade-off**: qualified drivers **trailed** the non-trainees on one test of recalling an **intricate** figure. The **cohorts** were small and **exclusively** male, yet the studies **imply** the healthy adult brain can **remodel** itself through **intensive** learning. Every street learned, it seems, **occupied** room once **claimed** by something else.
