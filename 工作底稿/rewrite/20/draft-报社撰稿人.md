# EN: The Brain That Learned London

Name two addresses and a cabbie will **sketch** the route **instantly**. That **prowess** is **hard-won**: **aspiring** drivers must learn about 25,000 streets within six miles of Charing Cross, every **landmark** and **junction** of a **dense** **labyrinth**. The **arduous** **apprenticeship**, "the Knowledge", typically takes three to four years; only about half **qualify**.

The **endurance** leaves an **imprint**. A study published in PNAS in April 2000 **scanned** 16 male cabbies and 50 other men. The cabbies' rear, or **posterior**, hippocampus, a seahorse-shaped structure **vital** to memory and **navigation**, had more **volume**, and a front, or **anterior**, region less. Longer **tenure** **accompanied** a larger rear region.

Perhaps, though, men **blessed** with **innate** **spatial** **aptitude** were **attracted** to the **vocation**, or the **disparity** could simply **reflect** **stressful** driving. A study published in Hippocampus in December 2006 dismissed the second **rival** **explanation**: cabbies had more grey matter there than bus drivers on **predetermined** routes with **equivalent** experience.

**Novices** **sharpened** the picture. A **longitudinal** study published in Current Biology in December 2011 scanned 79 **trainees** and 31 **peers** at **enrolment** and three to four years on. Only the 39 who qualified showed an **expansion** of grey matter, the tissue packed with nerve cells, in the rear hippocampus. That strongly **implies** learning was **responsible**.

The **reward** came, it appears, with a **penalty**. Graduates **lagged** on one visual memory test, recalling an **intricate** drawing after a **delay**. The **cohorts** were small and **exclusively** male. Still, the adult brain looks **malleable** enough to **remodel** itself. Each junction mastered seemed to **occupy** room once **claimed** by something else.
