# EN: Dolly, the Copied Sheep

Dolly was born at Roslin, Scotland, on 5 July 1996. Her **genome** came from a Finn Dorset ewe's mammary cell, **fused** with a Blackface egg stripped of its **nucleus**. A Blackface **surrogate** carried the **embryo** through **pregnancy**, yet her white face was **proof** of a **genetically** different **lineage**. She alone survived from 277 attempts, an **extraordinary** failure rate.

Dolly is **popularly** called the first clone, a **misconception**. Her **predecessors** included frogs from **transplanted** nuclei in 1962 and Roslin's embryo-derived **duo**, Megan and Morag, in 1995. Dolly was **genuinely** the first **mammal** cloned from an adult cell. Her **announcement** on 22 February 1997 **coincided** with the paper, and her name, a mammary **pun**, honoured Dolly Parton.

Her birth **provoked** **uproar** and **apprehension** about the **ethics** of human cloning. In March 1997 President Clinton **prohibited** federal funding for such experiments. In 1999 her telomeres, or **chromosome** tips, were reported about 20% **shortened** for her age. That fed **anxiety**, and some would **speculate** that she was ageing **prematurely**.

Dolly was put down at six on 14 February 2003, with **arthritis** and a **progressive** **lung** disease caused by a sheep **virus**. Indoor housing probably **contributed**. In July 2016, 13 clones, four from her cell line, were ageing **normally**, without **hypertension** or **diabetes**. In November 2017, X-rays of her **skeletal** remains showed ordinary arthritis, proving such fears **unfounded**.

She bore six lambs by natural **mating**, **descendants** that began with Bonnie in April 1998. Since 2003 her **stuffed** body has been **encased** in the National Museum of Scotland, a **famously** debated **icon** and **memorial**. Behind the glass, her white face **plainly** **testifies** to her Finn Dorset **ancestry**.
