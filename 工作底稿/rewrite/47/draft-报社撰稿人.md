# EN: Dolly, the Copied Sheep

On 5 July 1996 an **extraordinary** lamb was born at the Roslin Institute. A six-year-old Finn Dorset ewe's mammary cell was **fused** with a Blackface egg, the **embryo** going to a Blackface **surrogate**. Her white face and **woolly** **fleece** were **proof** of a **genetically** Finn Dorset **nucleus**. Only one live **duplicate** came from 277 tries.

Dolly was not the first clone. Frogs came from **transplanted** nuclei in 1962, and Roslin twins from embryonic cells in June 1995. Unlike those **predecessors**, Dolly was **genuinely** a **mammal** **reproduction**, from an adult cell past **specialisation**. The **accomplishment**'s **announcement**, on 22 February 1997, would **coincide** with the paper. A mammary **pun** named her after Dolly Parton.

**Anxiety** and **ethics** collided **rapidly**. In March 1997, amid the **uproar**, President Clinton **prohibited** federal human-cloning funds. Kept indoors for safety, Dolly faced a fresh **threat**. In 1999 her telomeres, the tips of her **chromosomes**, were reported **shortened** by about 20% against her peers', feeding a **nightmare** that she was ageing **prematurely**.

Dolly was put down on 14 February 2003, aged six, with **arthritis** and a **progressive** **lung** disease, a common viral illness. Indoor **confinement** probably contributed. By 2016, 13 clones, including four **siblings** from her cell line, were ageing **normally**, free of **hypertension** and **diabetes**. X-rays in 2017 showed ordinary joint wear, so the **dread** proved **unfounded**.

Dolly's six **vigorous** **descendants** arrived naturally, the first, Bonnie, in April 1998. Since 2003 the **icon** has stood **stuffed** in the National Museum of Scotland, a **famously** **tranquil** **memorial** for **pilgrims**. Behind the glass, her white face **calmly** pays **homage** to the Finn Dorset ewe behind the cell.
