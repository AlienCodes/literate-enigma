# EN: Dolly, the Copied Sheep

Dolly was born on 5 July 1996 at Scotland's Roslin Institute. Her **genome**, from a six-year-old Finn Dorset ewe's mammary cell, was **fused** with a Scottish Blackface egg, and a Blackface **surrogate** carried the **pregnancy**. Her **pale** face was **evident** **proof** of **lineage**. The only live lamb from 277 attempts, she was **genetically** **extraordinary**.

Dolly is **popularly** called the first clone, a **misconception**. Her **predecessors** included 1962 **nucleus**-**transplant** frogs and Roslin's embryonic-cell twins Megan and Morag, of June 1995. Dolly was the first **mammal** cloned from an adult cell. Her **announcement** on 22 February 1997 was **pointedly** timed to **coincide** with the paper. Her name **echoed** the mammary cell via Dolly Parton.

Dolly's birth **provoked** **ethical** **anxieties** over human cloning's **threat**; in March 1997 President Clinton **prohibited** federal funds for such experiments. Dolly was **sheltered** indoors for security. In 1999 her telomeres, or **chromosome** tips, were reported about 20% **shortened** against sheep her age, feeding **dread** that she was born **prematurely** old.

Dolly was put down on 14 February 2003, aged six, with **arthritis** and a **progressive**, **virus**-caused **lung** disease fairly common in sheep. Indoor **confinement** probably **contributed**. In July 2016, 13 clones, four of them her **siblings**, were ageing **normally**, free of **hypertension** and **diabetes**. Skeletal X-rays in November 2017 showed ordinary arthritis, making cloning fears **unfounded**.

Dolly's **reproductive** life was **plainly** **uneventful**: through **mating** she bore six lambs, the first, Bonnie, in April 1998. Since 2003 she has stood, **stuffed** and **encased**, in Edinburgh's National Museum of Scotland as an **icon**. Her white face, the breed **hallmark**, **faithfully** **testifies** to the single Finn Dorset cell.
