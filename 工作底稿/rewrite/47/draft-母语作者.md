# EN: Dolly, the Copied Sheep

Dolly was born on 5 July 1996 at Roslin, near Edinburgh. A six-year-old Finn Dorset ewe's mammary cell supplied her **genome**, **fused** with a Blackface egg, and a Blackface **surrogate** carried the **embryo** through **pregnancy**. Her white face, a **genetically** plain **signature**, was **proof** of **parentage**. Of 277 attempts, she alone lived, against **extraordinary** odds.

Contrary to **belief**, she had **predecessors**, frogs cloned from **transplanted** nuclei in 1962 and Roslin's embryonic-cell **duo** Megan and Morag, born in June 1995. **Strictly**, she was the first adult-cell **mammal** clone, **genuinely** new. Her **announcement** on 22 February 1997 **coincided** with the paper. Her name, a **pun** on the gland, honoured Dolly Parton.

Her birth stirred **apprehension** about **ethics**. In March 1997 President Clinton **prohibited** federal funds for human-cloning experiments. Security kept her in **indoor** **confinement**. In 1999 her telomeres, or **chromosome** tips, were reported about 20% **shortened** against sheep her age. That fed the **anxiety**, even the **nightmare**, of a sheep born old and ageing **prematurely**.

She was euthanised on 14 February 2003, aged six, with **arthritis** and a **progressive** **lung** disease, common among sheep, caused by a **virus**. Confinement probably **contributed**. In July 2016, 13 clones, including four **siblings** from her line, were ageing **normally**, without **hypertension** or **diabetes**. In November 2017 her **skeletal** X-rays showed ordinary arthritis, the fears **unfounded**.

She bore six lambs naturally, her **descendants** led by Bonnie, her first **heir**, in April 1998. Since 2003, **stuffed** and **woolly**, she has **famously** stood in Edinburgh's National Museum of Scotland, **icon**, **memorial** and **testament**. Behind the glass, her white face **plainly** traces her **lineage** to that adult cell.
