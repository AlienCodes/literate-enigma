# EN: Lost in the Glare

On a **misty** **cloud-forest** night at Monteverde, Costa Rica, a moth **skims** over an ultraviolet lamp and, in **mid-flight**, flips its **belly** to the sky. **Stereo** cameras catch the **acrobatics**. In 2024 such footage helped scientists at Imperial College London **unravel** an **age-old** puzzle, the **helpless** **swirl** of insects round **porch** lights.

Folk **wisdom** blames **attraction**, like a moth to a flame. In fact, insects keep their backs to the brightest thing in view, a **built-in** reflex that holds them level beneath an open sky. A lamp **hijacks** it, posing as a **counterfeit** sky. **Muddled** insects **wheel** round it or, passing above, flip over. In 477 field **clips**, they almost never flew straight at the light.

Fireflies **flirt** by **lantern**. At **dusk**, **suitors** **wink** in code and females **blink** back, a **duet** that artificial light **smothers**. In Tufts University experiments under such light, males flashed about half as often. Females replied half as often under red or blue, scarcely under white, and not once under **amber**. No flashing, no mating.

The losses spread. At 55 **roadside** sites in the Thames Valley, street lights cut moth **caterpillars** by 47% in **hedges**, and **harsh** white LEDs did worse than yellow **sodium** lamps. In 2017, on Swiss **meadows** **rigged** with lamps, **nocturnal** pollinator visits fell by 62%. The thistles set fewer fruit, and **daytime** pollinators failed to make up the **deficit**.

**Humanity** is losing its sky too. A **veil** of lamplight hides the Milky Way's **luminous** **ribbon** from more than a third of us, including 60% of Europeans and nearly 80% of North Americans. **Citizen** scientists counting stars from **backyards** and **balconies** found the night sky brightening by about 9.6% a year. A child born where 250 stars **twinkle** would, at that rate, see about 100 by their eighteenth birthday.
