# EN: Lost in the Glare

On a **misty** **cloud-forest** night at Monteverde, Costa Rica, a moth **skims** over an ultraviolet lamp and, in **mid-flight**, flips its **belly** to the sky. **Stereo** cameras catch the **acrobatics**. In 2024 the footage helped Imperial College London scientists **unravel** an **age-old** puzzle, the **helpless** **swirl** of **nocturnal** insects round **porch** lights.

Folk **wisdom** blames **attraction**, like a moth to a flame. In fact, insects keep their backs to the brightest thing in view. Under open sky, that reflex works like a **built-in** **spirit level**. A lamp **hijacks** it, posing as a **counterfeit** sky. **Muddled** insects **eddy** round it or, passing above, flip.

Fireflies **flirt** by **lantern**. At **dusk**, males **wink** in code and females **blink** back, a **glimmering** **duet** that artificial light **smothers**. In Tufts University tests under such light, **ardent** **suitors** roughly halved their flashing. Females answered about half as often under red or blue, scarcely under white, and never under **amber**. No flashing, no mating.

**Lamp-lit** **roadside** **hedges** in the Thames Valley held 47% fewer moth **caterpillars** than dark ones, and **harsh** white LEDs did more harm than yellow **sodium** lights. On Swiss **meadows** **rigged** with street lamps, night-time pollinator visits fell by 62%. The **blooms** set fewer fruit, and **daytime** pollinators failed to make up the **deficit**.

Over 80% of **humanity** lives under **washed-out** skies, and a **luminous** **veil** hides the **pale** **ribbon** of the Milky Way from more than a third. **Citizen** scientists counting stars with the **naked** eye found the night sky brightening by about 9.6% a year. A child born where 250 stars **twinkle** would, at that rate, see about 100 by their eighteenth birthday.
