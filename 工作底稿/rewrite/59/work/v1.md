# EN: Lost in the Glare

In the **misty** **cloud-forest** of Monteverde, Costa Rica, a moth **skims** over an ultraviolet lamp and flips **upside-down**, **belly** to the sky. A team from Imperial College London recorded such **acrobatics** there in **stereo**, and in a London laboratory by motion capture.

Their study, published in 2024, overturns an **age-old** **belief**. Insects are not **attracted** to light. By **instinct**, they keep their backs to the brightest thing in view, which under an **open-air** sky keeps them level. A lamp **hijacks** the habit, holding them **hostage** in circles or flipping them over. In 477 field **clips**, almost none flew **directly** at the light.

Fireflies **flirt** in light, so bright nights **smother** the **banter**. In a 2020 survey, experts ranked light pollution second only to habitat loss among threats to them, ahead of **pesticides**. At Tufts University, males under artificial light **emitted** about five flash patterns a minute instead of ten. Females answered half as often under red or blue light, scarcely at all under white, and not once under **amber**.

The losses **spill** further. At 55 **roadside** sites in the Thames Valley, street lighting cut moth **caterpillar** numbers by 47% in **hedges** and 33% in grassy **verges**, with white LEDs **harsher** than yellow **sodium** lamps. In seven of 14 Swiss **meadows** given street lamps, night-time pollinator visits fell by 62%. The plants set fewer fruit, and **daytime** insects did not make up the loss.

People are **losing** the night too. The Milky Way is **hidden** from more than a third of **humanity**, including 60% of Europeans and nearly 80% of North Americans. **Citizen** scientists counting stars from 2011 to 2022 found the sky growing about 9.6% brighter each year. A child born under 250 **naked-eye** stars would, at that **pace**, see about 100 by their eighteenth birthday.
