# EN: From a Garage to the Top of the World

In May 1985, after a **bruising** **confrontation**, Apple's board **ultimately** backed chief **executive** John Sculley. Steve Jobs, then 30, was **stripped** of his **managerial** duties at the company he had **co-founded**. That September, **sidelined** and **alienated**, he **resigned**.

**Rewind** three decades. Born in San Francisco on 24 February 1955, Jobs was adopted as a **newborn**. On 1 April 1976 he founded Apple with Steve Wozniak, the **electronics** engineer behind its first **prototypes**. Apple's garage **origins** are largely **symbolic**; Wozniak calls their **significance** **inflated**. Apple **floated** on 12 December 1980; the **flagship** Macintosh **debuted** on 24 January 1984.

**Exile** proved **productive**. Later in 1985 Jobs founded NeXT, a computer **start-up**. In February 1986 he **acquired** Lucasfilm's computer-**graphics** **outfit**, which became Pixar. It began as a **manufacturer** of **specialised** hardware, not chiefly an **animation** studio. Toy Story, released on 22 November 1995, was the first **feature-length** **computer-animated** film.

The **reversal** came on 20 December 1996: Apple agreed to buy NeXT, **regaining** its **estranged** co-founder and the **core** software of the **eventual** Mac OS X. In August 1997 Microsoft **invested** $150 million in its **distressed** **competitor**; that September Jobs took the **reins** as **interim** chief. Apple, he later said, had been weeks from **ruin**. The iMac, iPod and iPhone followed, the last **showcased** on 9 January 2007.

In October 2003 Jobs received a **diagnosis**: an **uncommon**, **cancerous** **pancreatic** **tumour**. In August 2011 Apple **briefly** **overtook** Exxon Mobil as the world's most valuable company. His **tenure** ended on 24 August 2011; he died on 5 October 2011, aged 56. He had spent nearly fourteen years at the **helm** of the company that had **ousted** him.
