# EN: From a Garage to the Top of the World

In May 1985, after a **bruising** **showdown**, Apple's board **aligned** with chief **executive** John Sculley against Steve Jobs, its **co-founder**. Jobs, then 30, was **stripped** of every **managerial** **prerogative**. In September 1985 he **resigned**, **effectively** **ousted** from a firm he had helped found.

**Rewind** three decades. Born in San Francisco on 24 February 1955, Jobs was adopted in **infancy**. On 1 April 1976 he and Steve Wozniak founded Apple. Its **famed** garage is **principally** **symbolic**; Wozniak, the engineer, has **characterised** its role as **inflated**. The Apple II **debuted** in April 1977, Apple **floated** on 12 December 1980 and the **flagship** Macintosh followed on 24 January 1984.

**Exile** did not mean **inactivity**. In 1985 Jobs founded NeXT, a **startup**. In February 1986 he **acquired** Lucasfilm's computer-**graphics** division, which became Pixar. It began as a **manufacturer** of **specialised** computers, not an **animation** house. On 22 November 1995 it released Toy Story, the first **feature-length** **computer-animated** film.

The **reversal** came on 20 December 1996, when Apple announced a **takeover** of NeXT, **reclaiming** its **estranged** co-founder. In August 1997 Microsoft **invested** $150 million in its **distressed** **competitor**; in September Jobs took the **reins** as **interim** chief. He later said Apple had been weeks from going **bankrupt**. The iMac, iPod and iPhone followed in **succession**.

In October 2003 Jobs received a **diagnosis** of an **uncommon** **variant** of **pancreatic** cancer. In August 2011 Apple **briefly** **overtook** Exxon Mobil in market **valuation**, ranking first **globally**. He **vacated** the chief executive's post on 24 August 2011 and died on 5 October 2011, aged 56. He had **presided** over the **revival** of the company that **sidelined** him.
