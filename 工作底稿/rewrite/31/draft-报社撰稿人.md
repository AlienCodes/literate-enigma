# EN: From a Garage to the Top of the World

In May 1985, after a **turbulent** **confrontation**, Apple's directors **ultimately** backed its chief **executive**, John Sculley. Steve Jobs, then 30, was **stripped** of his **managerial** role at the company he had **co-founded**. The **humiliation** of being **sidelined** ended when he **resigned** in September 1985.

Born in San Francisco on 24 February 1955, Jobs was **reared** by **adoptive** parents from **infancy**. On 1 April 1976 he **officially** founded Apple with Steve Wozniak, the **hobbyist** **responsible** for the engineering, who calls the garage of legend **overblown**. **Milestones** followed: the Apple II in April 1977, **stock** sold to **investors** on 12 December 1980, the Macintosh's **debut** on 24 January 1984.

**Exile** proved **productive**. In 1985 Jobs founded NeXT, a computer **startup**. In February 1986 he **acquired** Lucasfilm's computer-**graphics** **outfit**, which became Pixar, **originally** a **manufacturer** of **specialised** hardware. Its Toy Story, released on 22 November 1995, was the first **feature-length** **computer-animated** film.

Apple's **acquisition** of NeXT, announced on 20 December 1996, brought its **co-founder** back. In August 1997 Microsoft, a **long-standing** **competitor**, **invested** $150 million. By September 1997 Jobs was **interim** chief executive. Apple, he later said, was weeks from going **bankrupt**. A **resurgence** followed, **notably** the iMac in August 1998, the **pocket-sized** iPod in October 2001 and the iPhone, **showcased** on 9 January 2007.

In October 2003 came a **diagnosis**: an **uncommon**, **malignant** **pancreatic** **tumour**. In August 2011 Apple **briefly** **overtook** Exxon Mobil as the company with the highest **valuation**. His **tenure** ended on 24 August 2011; he died on 5 October 2011, aged 56. He had spent nearly fourteen years at the **helm** of the company that **ousted** him.
