# EN: From a Garage to the Top of the World

In May 1985, after a **bruising** **confrontation**, Apple's board sided with its chief **executive**, John Sculley. Steve Jobs, then 30, was **stripped** of his **managerial** duties at the company he had **co-founded**. In September the **outcast** **resigned**. The **exit** was **humiliating**.

Jobs had been born in San Francisco on 24 February 1955 and placed for **adoption** as a **newborn**. On 1 April 1976 he founded Apple with Steve Wozniak, an **amateur** electronics **enthusiast** who built its first **prototypes**. The garage became a **symbolic** **birthplace**, though Wozniak has said its role is **inflated**. Apple **floated** its **stock** in 1980; the Macintosh **debuted** in 1984.

**Exile** proved **productive**. Later in 1985 Jobs founded NeXT, a computer **start-up**. In February 1986 he **acquired** Lucasfilm's computer **graphics** division, which became Pixar. It was **originally** a **hardware-maker**. Its Toy Story, released on 22 November 1995, was the first **feature-length** **computer-animated** film, a **milestone** in **animation**.

Then came the **reversal**. On 20 December 1996 Apple announced the **acquisition** of NeXT, bringing Jobs back. In August 1997 Microsoft, a **longtime** **competitor**, **invested** $150 million, and in September Jobs became **interim** chief. He later said Apple had been weeks from going **bust**. In **succession** came the **colourful** iMac in 1998, the **pocket-sized** iPod in 2001 and the iPhone in 2007.

In October 2003 Jobs received a **diagnosis** of an **uncommon** **pancreatic** **tumour**. In August 2011 Apple's **valuation** **briefly** **overtook** the **petroleum** **giant** Exxon Mobil's as the world's largest. His **resignation** on 24 August 2011 ended his second **tenure**. He died on 5 October 2011, aged 56. He had left Apple twice, the second time **willingly**.
