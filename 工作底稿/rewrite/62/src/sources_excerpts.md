# 第62篇（斯瓦尔巴种子库）来源摘录（WebSearch，2026-10-03）
S1 Britannica; Crop Trust: The Svalbard Global Seed Vault opened in 2008 (26 February) on Spitsbergen in Norway's Svalbard archipelago, near Longyearbyen, dug into a mountain; seeds kept at about −18 °C, protected by permafrost; capacity about 4.5 million seed samples; designed to protect crop seeds (beans, rice, wheat…) against catastrophes such as nuclear war or disease. It is a backup: depositors keep ownership and only they can withdraw their own seeds ("like a safe-deposit box").
S2 Al Jazeera / Reuters, 23 Sept 2015; Crop Trust: The Syrian civil war prompted the first withdrawal. ICARDA (International Center for Agricultural Research in the Dry Areas), whose gene bank had been near Aleppo, asked for about 116,000 samples — 130 of the 325 boxes it had deposited before the war — including drought- and heat-tolerant strains of wheat, barley and other crops. ICARDA had moved its headquarters to Beirut because of the war.
S3 Same: ICARDA grew the seeds in Lebanon and Morocco to rebuild its collection; once it had multiplied enough, it returned fresh seeds to Svalbard (redeposits from 2017).
S4 Guardian/Engadget, May 2017: after record warm temperatures, meltwater ran into the entrance tunnel (about 100 m long) and froze; it did not reach the seeds, which stayed at −18 °C; Norway then waterproofed the tunnel and dug drainage trenches.
