# EN: The Cave Found by a Dog

Marcel Ravidat, 18, was taking a **rambling** **stroll** near Montignac with his dog Robot. In the **woodland**, Robot **sniffed** at a **burrow** left by an uprooted tree. On 12 September 1940 Ravidat returned with three other boys. The young **explorers** made the **excursion** an **adventure**, climbing down the **shaft** into **darkness**.

In later **retellings**, Ravidat **embroidered** the **anecdote**. He told **admirers** that the dog had fallen into the cave, a version that earned Robot **affection** and **devotion**. **Ironically**, the truth is less dramatic. Robot merely investigated the hole beneath a **toppled** tree on the **hillside**, and the **adventurous** teenagers entered days later.

Inside lay about 680 painted figures and about 1,500 **engravings**. These **murals** are usually dated to 17,000 to 22,000 years ago, an **antiquity** that makes their **creators** our distant **ancestors**. Horses, **bulls** and stags are among the animals **depicted**, some **galloping** in **pigments** across the walls. Their **fragility** soon became clear.

Lascaux opened to the public on 14 July 1948, and **tourism** brought a **throng** of about 1,200 visitors a day. The **influx** **exhaled** carbon dioxide and added heat and **humidity**, among other **contaminants**. This **congestion** visibly caused **deterioration** of the **vulnerable** paintings. In 1963 access was **restricted**, the paintings were restored and daily monitoring began.

UNESCO listed Lascaux as World Heritage in 1979. Lascaux II, an accurate partial **facsimile**, opened nearby in 1983, and Lascaux IV in December 2016. In 2001 a white **fungal** **outbreak** **invaded** the walls. After chemical treatment, black **stains** spread, an **infestation** threatening **preservation**. Most people now admire the bulls and horses through a **faithfully** **recreated** **duplicate**.
