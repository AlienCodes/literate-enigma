# EN: The Cave Found by a Dog

On a **stroll** through **woodland** near Montignac, France, Marcel Ravidat, 18, was with his dog, Robot. On the **hillside** Robot was **sniffing** at a hole left by an uprooted tree, a **burrow** above a **shaft**. On 12 September 1940 he returned with three other boys, and the **excursion** became an **adventure** as the **explorers** climbed into the **darkness**.

The popular **anecdote** adds **drama**. In later **retellings**, Ravidat **embroidered** the **storyline** with a **canine** **mishap**, saying Robot had fallen into the cave. **Ironically**, many **admirers** repeat that **fabricated** fall. The **credible** sequence is plainer, a dog sniffing and boys returning later.

Underground lay about 680 painted figures and about 1,500 **engravings**, usually dated to 17,000–22,000 years ago. Horses, **bulls** and stags are among the **fauna** **depicted**. **Ancestors** in **antiquity** traced the **contours** and **motifs** in **pigments**, and the **murals** had lasted millennia.

Lascaux opened to the public on 14 July 1948, and **tourism** brought a daily **throng** as people **flocked** there. About 1,200 visitors a day **exhaled** carbon dioxide and added heat and **humidity**, while **contaminants** followed the **influx**. The **deterioration** of such **vulnerable** paintings was plain. In 1963 access was **restricted**, the paintings were restored and daily checks began.

UNESCO listed Lascaux as World Heritage in 1979. Lascaux II, a partial **facsimile** **meticulously** **recreated** nearby, opened in 1983, and Lascaux IV in December 2016. **Preservation** met an **outbreak** in 2001, when a white **mildew**, a **fungus**, **invaded** the walls. After chemical treatment, black **stains** spread. Most admirers of those horses and bulls now look at a **reproduction**, because the original has been shut to visitors since 1963.
