# EN: The Cave Found by a Dog

Marcel Ravidat, 18, was on a **woodland** **stroll** near Montignac with his dog Robot when the **canine** began **sniffing** at a **hollow** left by a **toppled** tree. On 12 September 1940 he came back with three other boys for an **excursion** that became an **adventure**, and the four **explorers** climbed down into the **darkness**.

In later **retellings**, Ravidat **embroidered** his **anecdote** with a **dramatic** twist. Robot, he said, had fallen into the cave. That **fanciful** **mishap** is a **fabrication**. The animal only nosed about the hole, and the **pivotal** **initiative** belonged to the boys. **Ironically**, the invented fall handed the dog the credit for their decision.

Inside, some 680 painted figures and 1,500 **engravings** cover the rock. The **artwork** is usually dated to between 17,000 and 22,000 years ago, a remote **antiquity**; its **creators** were our distant **ancestors**. Horses, **bulls** and stags are among the animals **depicted** in **pigments** on the **murals**. Their **fragility** would soon become clear.

Lascaux opened to the public on 14 July 1948, and **tourism** brought an **influx** of about 1,200 visitors a day. The **throng** **exhaled** carbon **dioxide** and added heat, **humidity** and other **contaminants**, and the **deterioration** of the paintings became plain to see. In 1963 the site closed to the public, followed by **restoration** and daily **inspection**.

Lascaux joined UNESCO's World Heritage List in 1979. Lascaux II, a **meticulously** made **partial** **facsimile**, opened in 1983, and Lascaux IV in December 2016. A white **fungus** **invaded** the walls in a 2001 **outbreak**, and black **stains** spread after chemical treatment. **Admirers** now meet the bulls only in a **recreation**, for the originals are **inaccessible** to them.
