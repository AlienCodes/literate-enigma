## Recommended edits

### 1. P6, closing sentence: fix what "they" refers to and sharpen the contrast (+1 word)

- **Original:** A map could **chart** where people died, but only the living could say what they drank.
- **Proposed:** A map could **chart** where people died, but only the living could say what the dead drank.
- **Why:** "They" follows straight after "the living", so the grammatical reading is "what the living drank". That is the opposite of what we mean. "The dead" fixes the reference. It also turns the line into a clean living/dead contrast that echoes "the register of deaths, then the living" and "all the dead" in P2. It still echoes the title (died … drank) without copying it.

### 2. P5: fix "back home in", which attaches to the wrong verb (0 words)

- **Original:** She **perished**; so did a visiting **niece** who drank it, back home in "a high and healthy part of Islington".
- **Proposed:** She **perished**; so did a visiting **niece** who drank it and returned to "a high and healthy part of Islington".
- **Why:** Where it sits, "back home in" reads most naturally as belonging to "drank". That says she drank the water in Islington, which is wrong (G5: she drank it in Hampstead, went home, then fell ill and died). "Returned to" follows Snow's own sequence ("she returned to her residence, in a high and healthy part of Islington") and costs nothing. "Visiting … returned" already tells the reader that Islington was her home.

### 3. P6, first sentence: remove a garden path (0 words)

- **Original:** …**contending** that nobody had **demonstrated** its drinkers suffered **disproportionately**.
- **Proposed:** …**contending** nobody had **demonstrated** that its drinkers suffered **disproportionately**.
- **Why:** Without "that", "demonstrated its drinkers" first reads as a noun object, and the reader has to back up. "Contend" without "that" is normal journalistic English. "Demonstrate" without "that" is the one that trips the reader, so the "that" moves to where it is needed.

### 4. P3: cut "later" to pay for edit 1 (−1 word)

- **Original:** Edmund Parkes, a professor of **clinical** medicine, later **interpreted** its **cluster**…
- **Proposed:** Edmund Parkes, a professor of **clinical** medicine, **interpreted** its **cluster**…
- **Why:** The sentence before already says the map "was not unveiled until December". So "its cluster" can only be read after that, and "later" adds nothing. The linguist named this as the safest cut.

## The three queries

**"Cholera was swallowed, not inhaled"** is fine; keep it. It is standard metonymy, and historians routinely write that Snow held cholera was "ingested, not inhaled". The swallowed/inhaled pair sets up the air-versus-water axis in five words. Snow's own term "the cholera poison was swallowed" would be more literal and would echo Parkes's airborne "poison" in P3. It costs two words, though, and fixes nothing a reader would actually trip on.

**"The crucial cases isolated water from air"** reads naturally; keep it. "Isolate X from Y" meaning "separate one factor from another" is ordinary educated English (compare "isolate genetic from environmental factors"). Coming straight after two readings, one by air and one by water, it cannot be taken literally. It is also the paragraph's topic sentence.

**"Back home in"** is not placed cleanly. See edit 2.
