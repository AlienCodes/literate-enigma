## Proposals

### 1. Last line: fix the pronoun (must fix)
- **Original:** A map could **chart** where people died, but only the living could say what they drank.
- **Proposed:** A map could **chart** where people died, but only the living knew what the dead drank.
- **Why:** "They" attaches grammatically to the nearest plural, "the living". So the article's most important line can be read as "the survivors could say what they themselves drank". Naming "the dead" removes that reading. It also adds a living/dead antithesis inside the where/what, map/people frame. The line still echoes the title's "died" and "drank" and still picks up P2's "then the living". "Knew" is one word shorter than "could say", which pays for "the dead".
- **Word count:** 0 (could say → knew is −1; they → the dead is +1).
- **Alternative:** "…but only the living could say what the dead drank." This keeps the could chart / could say parallel and stresses testimony, but costs +1. If you prefer it, skip proposal 2 to stay at 271.
- **On "only":** the "only" is literal: dead people cannot testify. It is not a general claim, so I think §6.1(3) is met, as the linguist argued.

### 2. P2: give the handle myth-bust its own sentence
- **Original:** **At his urging**, parish officials removed the handle on 8 September, when **fatal** cases had **plunged** from 143 a day to 12.
- **Proposed:** **At his urging**, parish officials removed the handle on 8 September. By then **fatal** cases had **plunged** from 143 a day to 12.
- **Why:** Correcting the handle myth is a red line (§5 #4), but right now it sits in a "when" clause and relies on the past perfect. Many 考研 readers will miss that. "By then" makes the order unmistakable. P2 then ends on the number, and it sets up two debunkings back to back: P3 opens "Snow's renowned map was not unveiled until December."
- **Word count:** +1 (when → By then).

### 3. P3: cut "later" (pays for proposal 2)
- **Original:** Edmund Parkes, a professor of **clinical** medicine, later **interpreted** its **cluster** as poison **drifting** through the air.
- **Proposed:** Edmund Parkes, a professor of **clinical** medicine, **interpreted** its **cluster** as poison **drifting** through the air.
- **Why:** The preceding sentence ends "…not unveiled until December", and "its cluster" refers to that map. So Parkes already reads as coming after it, and "later" does no work. The linguist also names this as the safest word to cut.
- **Word count:** −1.

### 4. P6: move "that" to remove a garden path
- **Original:** …**contending** that nobody had **demonstrated** its drinkers suffered **disproportionately**.
- **Proposed:** …**contending** nobody had **demonstrated** that its drinkers suffered **disproportionately**.
- **Why:** Without "that", "demonstrated its drinkers" first reads as verb plus object, and "suffered" forces the reader to start the phrase again. The bump comes in the article's densest sentence, just before the final line. Formal prose keeps "that" after demonstrate, and "contend" drops it without trouble.
- **Word count:** 0.
