# EN: The Bread Made from Air

In July 1909, visitors from BASF gathered round a **squat** tabletop machine in Fritz Haber's **laboratory** in Karlsruhe. Behind its steel **valves**, nitrogen and hydrogen were **squeezed** together at crushing **pressure** and **furnace** heat. Then a **pungent** liquid began to **drip** out, one **bead** at a time. It was **ammonia**, the stuff that crops **hunger** for, **wrested** from the gas that fills the sky.

Carl Bosch, a BASF **engineer**, **harnessed** the process on an industrial scale, and in autumn 1913 a new plant at Oppau began to **mass-produce** it. Haber won the Nobel Prize in Chemistry for 1918 and was **acclaimed** for making bread from air. The praise was **deserved**. By 2008, researchers **estimated**, fertiliser from Haber-Bosch plants was feeding about 48 per cent of **humanity**.

The same **chemistry** served the war. When a British **blockade** cut Germany off from Chilean saltpetre, ammonia **supplied** the **nitrates** for its **explosives**. Haber, given the **rank** of **captain**, organised German chemical **warfare** with **zeal**. On 22 April 1915 at Ypres, in Belgium, soldiers opened nearly 6,000 cylinders and let about 168 tonnes of **toxic** chlorine **drift** towards the enemy **trenches**. It was the first **large-scale** gas attack.

His wife, Clara Immerwahr, had been the first woman in Germany to earn a **doctorate** in chemistry, and she **opposed** his work. On 2 May 1915, after a party **hosted** in his **honour**, she shot herself in the garden with his service **pistol**. The next day he left for the Eastern Front.

**Fame** offered no **shelter**. Haber was of Jewish **origin**, and after Hitler took power he **fled** Germany in 1933. He died in Basel the following January. **Pesticide** research at his **institute** had produced Zyklon. A later **variant**, Zyklon B, was used in Nazi death camps, and among the **victims** were **relatives** of his.

Roughly half the nitrogen in the **proteins** and DNA of the human body has passed through a Haber-Bosch plant. The man who organised the gas at Ypres is built into our **veins** and our **marrow**.
