# EN: The Bread Made from Air

In July 1909, **envoys** from BASF watched **intently** as a **squat** tabletop **rig** ran in Fritz Haber's Karlsruhe laboratory. Behind its **bolted** **valves**, nitrogen and hydrogen met at **crushing** pressure and **furnace** heat. **Slowly**, a **pungent**, **transparent** liquid began to **drip** out in **beads**. **Ammonia** had been **pried** from the air.

Carl Bosch of BASF **enlarged** the process, and from autumn 1913 a **gigantic** plant at Oppau began to **mass-produce** it. The **synthesis** brought Haber a Nobel Prize and **applause** for making bread from air. **Ploughed** into the **soil**, its fertiliser became a **cornerstone** of human **sustenance**, feeding about 48 per cent of **mankind** by 2008.

Ammonia also **stocked** the **arsenal**, supplying nitrates for **ordnance** despite a British **blockade**. Haber, made a captain, **masterminded** chemical warfare with **zeal**. On 22 April 1915 at Ypres, in Belgium, soldiers **vented** about 168 tonnes of chlorine from nearly 6,000 cylinders. A **greenish**, **poisonous** **fog** went **creeping** towards the **trenches**, the first large-scale gas **assault** on any **battlefield**.

His wife, Clara Immerwahr, the first woman in Germany to earn a doctorate in chemistry, **openly** opposed his work. On 2 May 1915, after **festivities** in his honour, she shot herself in the garden with his service **pistol**. The next day he left for the Eastern Front.

**Prestige** offered no **shelter**. Under Hitler, **persecution** drove Haber, a Jew by origin, out of Germany in 1933, and he died in Basel the following January. **Pesticide** research at his institute had produced Zyklon. A later version, Zyklon B, served the Nazi death camps, where relatives of his were among the victims of **genocide**.

About half the nitrogen in human proteins and DNA has passed through a Haber-Bosch plant. The organiser of Ypres is **literally** **incorporated** into our **bones** and **veins**.
