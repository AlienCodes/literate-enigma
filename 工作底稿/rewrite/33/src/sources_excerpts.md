# Article 33 — Randy Gardner sources (all [search] snippets; npr.org, wikipedia, guinnessworldrecords.com fetches BLOCKED)

S1 [search] NPR 2024-01-28 "Lessons from sleeplessness: 60th anniversary" https://www.npr.org/2024/01/28/1227217274/sleep-deprivation-record
 - "17-year-old Randy Gardner was a self-proclaimed 'science nerd' determined to make a splash in the 10th Annual Greater San Diego Science Fair."
 - "Gardner heard about a radio deejay in Honolulu ... who avoided sleep for 260 hours ... Gardner and his two friends, Bruce McAllister and Joe Marciano, set out to beat this record."
 - "Gardner stayed up for 264.4 hours: about 11 days and 25 minutes."
 - "Dement was so intrigued that he drove to San Diego to meet Gardner, and along with a U.S. Navy medic named Lt. Cmdr. John J. Ross, Dement helped monitor Gardner's health"
 - "After two days ... problems repeating simple tongue-twisters ... by the fifth day, was hallucinating with trouble forming short-term memories." Basketball improved. "won first place at the San Diego science fair." "Gardner's record was broken within the same year".
 - Syndicated (same text): wamc.org, kpcw.org, michiganpublic.org, gpb.org, vpm.org
 - "woke up on December 28, 1963 and didn't fall back asleep until January 8, 1964 ... without any stimulants except Coca-Cola, loud music and cold showers." (NPR/GPB snippet)

## Myths to avoid
- "No ill effects at all" — recovered quickly at the time, but he later reported insomnia; single case.
- "Record still standing / official world record" — broken claims exist, Guinness no longer monitors (since 1997).
- "11 days 24 minutes" — NPR says 25 min.
- "16 years old / 1965" — wrong (he was 17; 1963–64).
- Street-sign-as-person anecdote and press-conference detail and 14 h 40 min — unsourced here; omit or hedge.
- Implying Dement supervised from day 1 — he joined after press coverage.
- "Truly zero sleep" — microsleeps likely; can't be ruled out.
