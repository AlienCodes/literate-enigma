# 第58篇 来源摘录（两轮 WebSearch，2026-10-02）
S1 UC San Diego Health / ScienceDaily (25 Apr 2017); NIH Record (2019): Tom Patterson, a 69-year-old UC San Diego psychiatry professor, fell ill on a Thanksgiving holiday in Egypt (late 2015) with his wife, epidemiologist Steffanie Strathdee. He was flown to Frankfurt on 3 December 2015, then to UC San Diego on 12 December. He was infected with a multidrug-resistant strain of Acinetobacter baumannii that became resistant to all available antibiotics. A drain slipped, spilling bacteria into his abdomen and bloodstream; he went into septic shock and lay in a coma.
S2 Institut Pasteur; historyofinformation.com: Félix d'Hérelle, who joined the Institut Pasteur in 1911, discovered in 1917, using samples from a dysentery patient, a virus that infects bacteria. He named it "bacteriophage", literally "bacteria-eater", and suggested it could treat bacterial infections. His note was presented to the French Academy of Sciences in 1917. Frederick Twort had independently reported a similar agent in 1915.
S3 PMC reviews (PMC6203130 etc.); Wikipedia "George Eliava Institute": after the Second World War, with penicillin and other antibiotics, phage therapy was largely abandoned in the West (early preparations were less reliable). It persisted in the Soviet Union, especially Georgia: the Eliava Institute in Tbilisi was founded in 1923 by George Eliava, who had met d'Hérelle at the Pasteur Institute; d'Hérelle worked in Tbilisi in 1933–35. Eliava was executed in 1937. Phage preparations became widely available there, even over the counter.
S4 Texas A&M CPT / BuzzFeed; STAT (2018); UCSD: Strathdee searched for researchers with phages against his strain; teams at Texas A&M and the US Navy (which had collected phages from sources including sewage) found matching phages. In March 2016 Patterson became the first person in the US treated with intravenous phages for a systemic multidrug-resistant infection; he woke from the coma about three days later. He was discharged home on 12 August 2016 and returned to work.
S5 (context) WHO and many reviews: antibiotic resistance is a growing global threat, which has revived interest in phage therapy (write only in general terms).
