## 英文
In a Thai **rainforest**, a carpenter ant breaks from its **colony**'s **column** high in the **canopy**. It **totters** down into the **undergrowth**, as **disoriented** as an **intoxicated** **reveller**. Around **midday** it **fastens** its **jaws** on the **lower** face of a leaf, biting into a major **vein** about 25 **centimetres** above the ground.

The ant has been taken over by Ophiocordyceps unilateralis, a **fungal** **invader**. By **dusk** the insect is **lifeless**, yet the bite holds. The fungus has **shredded** the jaw muscles, so they cannot **loosen**. Researchers call it the death grip.

Two or three days later a **shaft** grows from the dead ant's **skull**. This fruiting body **showers** **spores** on to the ground below, where other ants **forage** and can pick them up.

The **site** is **deliberate**. Most such leaves lie on the **northwest** side of plants, where the **humid** air and temperature are **optimal** for the fungus. When researchers **transplanted** infected ants higher, the fungus grew **misshapen**. On the ground, the bodies **disappeared**, eaten or washed away.

In 2017 a team including David Hughes at Penn State put infected ants under a **microscope** that built **3D** images. Fungal cells had formed a **lattice** around muscle fibres in the legs and jaw. They had never **infiltrated** the brain.

The **scenario** is **archaic**. A 48-million-year-old **fossil** leaf from Messel, in Germany, bears the same **characteristic** bite **marks**.

In 2013 a BBC Planet Earth **sequence**, **narrated** by David Attenborough, **spurred** the video game The Last of Us. Neil Druckmann, its **creator**, **envisioned** the **parasite** jumping to humans.

The **fictional** **plague** **terrifies** **gamers**. The real one waits on a leaf vein, a hand's breadth above the **topsoil**.
