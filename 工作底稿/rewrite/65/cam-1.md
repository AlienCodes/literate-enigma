## 英文
In a Thai **rainforest**, a carpenter ant **deserts** its **colony**'s trail high in the **canopy**. It **staggers** **erratically** over low **foliage**, like a **tipsy** **wanderer**. Around **noon** it **clamps** its **jaws** onto the **underside** of a leaf, **gripping** a major **vein** about 25 centimetres above the ground.

The ant is **infected** with Ophiocordyceps unilateralis, a **parasitic** fungus. By **sunset** the insect is dead, yet the bite holds, because the fungus has **wrecked** the **jaw** muscles and they cannot **slacken**. Researchers call it the death grip.

Two or three days later a **stalk** **sprouts** from the **corpse**'s head. This **fruiting** body **sheds** **spores** onto the ground below, where **foraging** ants can pick them up.

The **perch** is no **accident**. The chosen leaves sit mostly on the north-west side of plants, where **humidity** and temperature suit the fungus. When researchers **relocated** infected ants higher, the fungus grew **abnormally**. On the ground, the bodies **vanished**.

In 2017 a team led by David Hughes at Penn State used **three-dimensional** **microscopy** to look inside infected ants. Fungal cells had formed an **interconnected** **mesh** around muscle **fibres** in the legs and jaw. They had not **penetrated** the brain at all.

The **habit** is **ancient**. A 48-million-year-old **fossilised** leaf from Messel, in Germany, bears the same **telltale** bite **scars**.

The BBC's Planet Earth, **narrated** by David Attenborough, filmed one such ant. That **footage** **inspired** The Last of Us, a 2013 video game by Naughty Dog. Its **co-creator**, Neil Druckmann, **pictured** the **parasite** leaping to humans.

On screen the leap became a **pandemic**. In the forest, the **victims** are carpenter ants, gripping leaves a hand's span above the soil.
