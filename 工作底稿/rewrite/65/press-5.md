## 英文

In a Thai rainforest, a carpenter ant quits its **colony**'s **procession** through the **canopy** and heads **downward**. It starts to **wander** **randomly** over **leafy** **undergrowth**, **swaying** as if **intoxicated**. Around **midday** it **clenches** its **jaws** **tightly** on a main **vein** **underneath** a leaf, about 25 **centimetres** above the ground.

By **dusk** the ant is dead. Its jaw muscles have been **thoroughly** **shredded**, and death does not **unlock** the grip on the **lifeless** body. Two or three days later a stalk pushes from its head and **sprinkles** **infectious** spores onto the ground below, where **unsuspecting** ants searching for food can pick them up.

The **fungal** **invader**, Ophiocordyceps unilateralis, is **picky** about **location**. Most bites land on the **north-west** side of plants, where the air is **humid** and the temperature suits the fungus. When researchers moved infected ants to **loftier** spots, the fungus grew **abnormally**. On the ground, the bodies **disappeared** **altogether**.

A 2017 PNAS study peered inside with **three-dimensional** **microscope** imaging. Fungal cells had formed a linked **cellular** **lattice**, **entangled** with the **muscle** fibres of the legs and jaw. The picture **vividly** **evokes** a **puppet** on strings. Yet the fungus did not **infiltrate** the brain.

The **habit** has deep **geological** roots. A 48-million-year-old **fossil** leaf from Messel, in Germany, bears the same bite **scars**.

The fungus helped **inspire** The Last of Us, a 2013 video game. Its **makers** at Naughty Dog drew on a Planet Earth episode, with David Attenborough as **narrator**. Neil Druckmann **envisioned** a **scenario** in which it jumped to humans.

Marks **imprinted** on a leaf at Messel show the bite is far older than the camera that filmed it.
