# EN: The 5,000-Year-Old Murder in the Ice

On 19 September 1991 two German **hikers** on a **trek** in the Ötztal Alps found a **corpse** by a **glacier**. It lay on a **ridge** at about 3,210 metres, on the border between Austria and Italy. The **assumption** was that a modern-**era** **climber** had died on an **alpine** **slope**, since glaciers sometimes yield frozen remains.

Treating it as a recent death, rescuers used a jackhammer that **tore** **flesh** from his left **hip**. His **possessions** told another story: **leather** and grass clothing, a **copper** **axe**, a bow and **arrows**, all **prehistoric** **artefacts**. Known as Ötzi, he was a natural **mummy** who lived about 5,300 years ago, between 3350 and 3105 BC.

In 2001, X-rays revealed a small **flint** arrowhead **lodged** in his left shoulder, **unseen** by **examiners** a decade earlier. A **scanner** showed that the **puncture** had **ruptured** an artery, leaving a large **clot**, a **mortal** injury that causes heavy **bleeding**. Researchers believe he died within minutes, aged about 46.

Researchers believe another person killed him. From the wound's depth they **inferred** that an **archer** shot him from the **rear**, at about 30 metres. The **shooter** probably caught him **unaware**. A 2018 study found that he had **dined** on dried ibex meat and fat, red **deer** and einkorn wheat, a **hearty**, **nourishing** meal reportedly **consumed** within two hours of his death.

**Whoever** fired the arrow is an **enigma**, and any **motive** is **conjecture**. Ötzi now lies with **curators** in Bolzano, his 61 **tattoos** on joints and **spine** perhaps an early form of **acupuncture**. He has a name, a last meal and a cause of death, while his **anonymous** **killer** has none.
