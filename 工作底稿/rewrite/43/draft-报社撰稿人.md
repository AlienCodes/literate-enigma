# EN: The 5,000-Year-Old Murder in the Ice

On 19 September 1991 two German **hikers** **stumbled** upon a **corpse** on a **rugged** **alpine** **ridge** in the Ötztal Alps, 3,210 metres up on the Austrian–Italian border. **Glaciers** sometimes give up frozen remains, so the **assumption** was a **climber** of the modern **era** lost in an accident. Reasonable, but wrong.

Assuming a recent death, rescuers used a jackhammer that tore **flesh** from his left **hip**. **Inspection** of his **belongings** told another story. **Leather** and grass clothing, a **copper** **axe**, a bow and **arrows** were no modern **kit**. He was a **prehistoric** natural **mummy**, later called Ötzi, who lived about 5,300 years ago, between 3350 and 3105 BC.

The **hint** lay **undetected** for a decade. In 2001, X-rays showed a **flint** arrowhead **lodged** in his left shoulder. **Scanner** images revealed a **torn** artery and a **clot**. Such a **puncture** causes heavy **bleeding**, and researchers believe he died within minutes, aged about 46. The **footnote** had become the story.

Researchers **inferred** that the arrow came from behind, from about 30 metres. The **archer** probably caught him **unawares** in an **ambush**. A 2018 study found a **hearty** last meal of dried ibex meat and fat, red **deer** and einkorn wheat, reportedly eaten within two hours of death. He apparently **dined** **calmly**, **unsuspecting**, his **digestion** barely begun.

Ötzi now lies in a Bolzano museum. His 61 **tattoos**, **inscribed** on joints and **spine**, were perhaps early **acupuncture**. Few strangers leave so **intimate** a **biography**. His **killer**, by **contrast**, is **nameless** and **faceless**. Ötzi has a name, a last meal and a cause of death, while whoever fired the arrow has none of these.
