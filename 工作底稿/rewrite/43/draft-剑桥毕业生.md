# EN: The 5,000-Year-Old Murder in the Ice

On 19 September 1991, two German **hikers** **trekking** on a **ridge** of the Ötztal Alps found a **corpse** by a **glacier**, 3,210 metres up on the Austrian–Italian border. **Alpine** glaciers sometimes yield frozen remains, so, **unaware** of his age, all made the **assumption** that he was a **climber** of the modern **era**.

Assuming a recent death, rescuers used a jackhammer, which **tore** **flesh** from his left **hip**. But his **belongings**, from **leather** and grass clothing to a **copper** **axe**, bow and **arrows**, were **prehistoric** **artefacts**. He was Ötzi, a natural **mummy** who lived about 5,300 years ago, between 3350 and 3105 BC.

In 2001, X-rays revealed a small **flint** arrowhead **lodged** in his left shoulder, missed by **examiners** in the first **inspection**. A **scanner** showed it had **punctured** an artery, leaving a 13-millimetre tear and a large **clot**. Such a **ruptured** vessel brings heavy **bleeding** and **cardiac** arrest, and researchers believe he died within minutes, aged about 46.

Researchers believe a **murderer** shot him. From the wound's depth, they **inferred** that the **nameless** **archer** stood about 30 metres to his **rear**. It was probably an **ambush**. A 2018 study found dried ibex meat and fat, red **deer** and einkorn wheat in his stomach, reportedly eaten half an hour to two hours before death. Seemingly **unsuspecting**, he had **dined** at a **leisurely** pace.

His **killer** is **unidentified**, and any **motive** is **conjecture**. Ötzi is known in **intimate** detail, down to 61 **tattoos**, **markings** on his joints and **spine**, perhaps early **acupuncture**. He has a name, a last meal and a cause of death, while whoever shot him has only **anonymity**.
