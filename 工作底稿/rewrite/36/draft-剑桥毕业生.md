# EN: The Book Twelve Publishers Turned Down

In 1990, on a **delayed** train from Manchester to London's King's Cross, the **inspiration** for Harry Potter, a **youthful** **wizard**, came **unexpectedly** to Joanne Rowling. Her mother's **untimely** **demise** on 30 December 1990, aged 45, brought **profound** **grief**. Rowling **relocated** to Porto to give English **tuition**.

Rowling married in Porto in October 1992; her daughter's **arrival** followed in July 1993. At the end of 1993, a **lone** mother **reliant** on state **welfare**, she **resettled** in Edinburgh with her daughter. She **habitually** wrote in **cafés**, developing the **unfinished** **storyline** with **unwavering** **dedication**.

A **literary** agent, Christopher Little, added Rowling to his **clientele**, taking on the manuscript with **evident** **confidence**. It was reportedly **rejected** by twelve publishers. Each **refusal** reflected an **editorial** **appraisal** by industry **insiders** and **veterans**, **individually** and **collectively** **unconvinced**.

At Bloomsbury, the **publisher's** chairman, Nigel Newton, gave the first chapter to his **eight-year-old** daughter, Alice, who read it and asked for the **continuation**. Bloomsbury's advance was a **sum** of 1,500 pounds. **Fearful** that boys might **disregard** a book by a woman, the publishers wanted initials to **mask** her **gender**. With no middle name, Rowling took the K from her **grandmother** Kathleen.

Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone appeared on 26 June 1997 in a **print-run** of 500 **hardcovers**, 300 **allocated** to libraries. The American publisher Scholastic paid $105,000 for US rights, **renaming** it Sorcerer's Stone for release there on 1 September 1998. In **contrast** to those 500, sales of the series and its **spin-off** **volumes** have **surpassed** 500 million copies in 80 languages. Among the earliest **readers** was a girl of eight with an **appetite** for more.
