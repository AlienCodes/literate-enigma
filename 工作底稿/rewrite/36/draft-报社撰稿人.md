# EN: The Book Twelve Publishers Turned Down

In 1990, on a **delayed** train from Manchester to London's King's Cross, the **inspiration** for Harry Potter came **unexpectedly** to Joanne Rowling. **Grief** soon **overshadowed** that **spark**: her mother died **prematurely** on 30 December 1990, aged 45. She then **relocated** to Porto, in Portugal, finding **employment** giving English **tuition**.

Rowling married in Porto in October 1992, and **motherhood** followed in July 1993. At the end of 1993 she **resettled** in Edinburgh, a **lone** mother **reliant** on state benefits. Her **unfinished** novel remained her **ambition**, pursued with **dedication**. She **habitually** wrote in **cafés**, working **patiently** in **obscurity**.

A **literary** agent, Christopher Little, took on the manuscript, an **endorsement** of a **first-time** **novelist**. He **pitched** it **repeatedly**, and the response was **decidedly** **chilly**. It was reportedly **rejected** by twelve publishers, a **succession** of **refusals** that tested his **persistence**.

At Bloomsbury, the chairman Nigel Newton gave the first chapter to his eight-year-old daughter, Alice, who read it and asked for the next **instalment**. The firm's **acquisition** followed her **enthusiastic** **reaction**, with an advance of 1,500 pounds. Bloomsbury **anticipated** **prejudice** among young male **readers** and **accordingly** **shortened** Joanne to an initial. She has no middle name, and the K came from her **grandmother** Kathleen.

Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone appeared on 26 June 1997 in a **cautious** print run of 500 hardback **volumes**, 300 of them **allocated** to libraries. Scholastic, a US publisher, paid a **considerable** **sum**, $105,000, for the American rights, and the **renamed** Sorcerer's Stone reached **bookshops** on 1 September 1998. **Globally**, sales have **surpassed** 500 million copies, in 80 languages. Alice's **appetite** was the first **indication**.
