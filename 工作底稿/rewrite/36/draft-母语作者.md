# EN: The Book Twelve Publishers Turned Down

In 1990, on a **delayed** train from Manchester to London's King's Cross, the **inspiration** for Harry Potter **dawned** **unexpectedly** on Joanne Rowling, a **would-be** **novelist**. **Grief** then **overshadowed** the **excitement**. Her mother died on 30 December 1990, aged 45, and Rowling, still **mourning**, **relocated** to Porto, in Portugal, to teach English.

She married there in October 1992, and **motherhood** followed with a daughter's **arrival** in July 1993. At the end of 1993 the two returned to Edinburgh, where Rowling, a **lone** mother **reliant** on state benefits, **habitually** wrote in **cafés**, **patiently** **polishing** a **sprawling**, **unfinished** **adventure**.

A **literary** agent, Christopher Little, took on the manuscript and **hawked** it round **prospective** publishers. It was reportedly rejected by twelve of them in **succession**, a **relentless** run of **refusals** offering the **newcomer** little **encouragement**. Yet the agent's **perseverance** held **steadfast** from the **outset**.

At Bloomsbury, the chairman Nigel Newton gave the first chapter to his eight-year-old daughter, Alice, who **relished** it and **eagerly** asked for the next **instalment**. The publisher **staked** £1,500 on it as an advance. **Apprehension** about boys' **reluctance** to read a woman **dictated** a **pragmatic**, **gender-neutral** **disguise**: J.K. Rowling. With no middle name, she took the K from her **grandmother** Kathleen.

Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone was published on 26 June 1997, in a **cautious** first print run of 500 hardbacks, 300 **allocated** to libraries. Scholastic's **acquisition** of the American rights cost $105,000, and the **renamed** Sorcerer's Stone appeared there on 1 September 1998. The **series** and its **spin-off** **volumes** have sold more than 500 million copies **overall**, in 80 languages. Alice's **appetite** was **evidently** widely shared.
