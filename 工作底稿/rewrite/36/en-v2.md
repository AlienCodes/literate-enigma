# EN: The Book Twelve Publishers Turned Down

In 1990, on a **delayed** train from Manchester to London's King's Cross, the **inspiration** for Harry Potter, an **orphaned** young **wizard**, came **unexpectedly** to Joanne Rowling, a **would-be** **novelist**. **Grief** soon **overshadowed** that **spark**: her mother died on 30 December 1990, aged 45. Rowling then **relocated** to Porto to teach English.

Rowling married in Porto in October 1992, and a daughter's **arrival** in July 1993 **ushered** in **motherhood**. At the end of 1993 she **resettled** in Edinburgh, a **lone** mother **reliant** on state **welfare**. She **habitually** wrote in cafés, **patiently** **polishing** her **unfinished** novel.

A **literary** agent, Christopher Little, took on the manuscript and **pitched** it **repeatedly** to **prospective** buyers. It was reportedly **rejected** by twelve publishers in **succession**, a **relentless** run of **refusals** that offered the **newcomer** little **encouragement**.

At Bloomsbury, the chairman Nigel Newton gave the first chapter to his eight-year-old daughter, Alice, who **relished** it and **eagerly** asked for the next **instalment**. Bloomsbury paid a **sum** of 1,500 pounds as an advance. **Fearful** that boys might **disregard** a book by a woman, the publishers **opted** for initials to **mask** her **gender**. For her **pseudonym**, Rowling, who has no middle name, took the K from her grandmother Kathleen.

Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone was published on 26 June 1997, in a **cautious** first print run of 500 hardbacks, 300 **allocated** to libraries. The American publisher Scholastic paid $105,000 for the US rights, and the **renamed** Sorcerer's Stone appeared there on 1 September 1998. The **series** and its **spin-off** **volumes** have **collectively** **surpassed** 500 million copies sold, in 80 languages. Alice's **appetite** was **evidently** shared by millions.
