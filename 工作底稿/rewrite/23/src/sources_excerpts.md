# Sources — The Steel Box That Remade the World (pass 2)
Method note: WebFetch was blocked by the egress proxy for wikipedia.org, sciencedirect.com and econpapers.repec.org. Excerpts below are therefore taken from search-result snippets of the named pages, not from full-page reads. Treat their wording as near-verbatim and re-check against the page before quoting it in print.

S1 SS Ideal X — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SS_Ideal_X ; Port Houston https://porthouston.com/ideal-x/ ; transportgeography.org https://transportgeography.org/contents/chapter1/the-setting-of-global-transportation-systems/idealx-first-containeriship-1956/
 - "On April 26, 1956 ... carrying 58 35-foot Trailer Vans." Sources disagree: some say "58 33-foot containers". The ship was a converted WWII T2 tanker.
S2 Make Tech Easier (secondary source citing Levinson) — https://maketecheasier.com/shipping-container-cut-loading-costs/
 - "Loading loose cargo on a medium-size cargo ship cost $5.83 per ton in 1956, while McLean's experts pegged the cost of loading the Ideal-X at 15.8 cents per ton." (This wording matches Levinson, The Box, ch. 3, as widely quoted. Google Books was not reachable.)
S3 Bernhofen, El-Sahli & Kneller, J. Int'l Econ. 98 (2016) 36–50, doi:10.1016/j.jinteco.2015.09.001 — https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:inecon:v:98:y:2016:i:c:p:36-50 ; accepted manuscript https://research.brighton.ac.uk/files/376845/JIE%20accepted%20manuscript%20online%20version%20(1).pdf
 - "Many historical accounts have asserted that containerization triggered complementary technological and organizational changes that revolutionized global freight transport. Our empirical strategy exploits time and cross-sectional variation in countries' first adoption of container facilities and combines it with product-level variation in containerizability and container usage."
 - Lund portal summary: "cumulative average treatment effects of containerization over a 20 year time period amount to about 700%" (North–North trade); panel 1962–1990.
 - Issue month: volume 98 is the January 2016 issue (from memory; the publisher page was blocked). Status: 存疑-minor.
S4 PANYNJ port history — https://www.panynj.gov/port/en/our-port/history.html
 - "On August 15, 1962, the Port Authority ... opened the world's first container port, Elizabeth Marine Terminal."
S5 Corner fittings / ISO (secondary snippets) — https://www.npr.org/2011/09/07/140256967/shipping-container-inventor-transformed-world-trade , https://hz-containers.com/en/news/the-ultimate-guide-to-buying-corner-castings-for-shipping-containers/
 - "In 1961, ISO established technical committee 104 (TC 104)."
 - "On January 29, 1963, Sea-Land granted free rights to the International Organization for Standardization (ISO)."
 - Corner casting and twistlock by Keith Tantlinger. "ISO 1161, first published in 1967."
 - ISO 668 first published in 1968: not confirmed by these sources. Status: 存疑.
S6 Levinson, The Box (via reviews) — https://performativebafflement.substack.com/p/longshoremen-strikes-shipping-containers , https://eh.net/book_reviews/the-box-how-the-shipping-container-made-the-world-smaller-and-the-world-economy-bigger/
 - "Between 1963 and 1976, longshore work in New York City fell by seventy-five percent."
 - "crews of 21 or 22 longshoremen."
 - "The Port of New York needed 1.9 man-hours to handle a ton of cargo in 1950, but 2.5 by 1956."
S7 Ships' time in port — https://www.morethanshipping.com/history-shipping-containers/ ; https://porteconomicsmanagement.org/pemp/contents/part8/break-bulk
 - "Ships spent half their time in port ... accumulated between 60 and 70% of their costs while sitting still."
 - "port times sometimes exceeding the time at sea."
 - These are secondary sources. The figure is commonly attributed to Levinson, but I did not see his page.
S8 London docks — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_India_Docks ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Docks ; https://canalrivertrust.org.uk/canals-and-rivers/london-docklands/history-of-london-docklands ; LSE https://blogs.lse.ac.uk/businessreview/2017/11/10/dockworkers-and-the-introduction-of-containers-in-uk-shipping-in-the-late-1960s/
 - "East India Docks ... closed 1967."
 - "The West India and Millwall Docks finally stopped general cargo operations in 1980."
 - Royal Docks: one source says closed 1981, another says 1984. Status: 存疑.
 - LSE: "many older ports (such as the Port of London) ... declining, while new ports expanded in more suitable locations such as Felixstowe."
S9 Container share of trade — https://porteconomicsmanagement.org/pemp/contents/part1/maritime-shipping-and-international-trade/value-containerized-trade/
 - "about two-thirds of the value of international trade carried by maritime transportation is carried by containers."
 - The widely repeated "~90% of non-bulk cargo" figure was not found in UNCTAD or WSC text. Status: 存疑.
S10 Ever Given — https://gcaptain.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/Final-Investigation-Report-Ever-Given-23-March-2021.pdf ; https://www.cnn.com/2021/03/29/africa/suez-canal-refloating-intl-hnk
 - "On 23 March 2021, the 20,000 TEU containership M/V EVER GIVEN grounded" ; "refloated at 4:30 a.m. local time ... 29th of March 2021" (six days).
S11 Smithsonian — https://www.smithsonianmag.com/innovation/shipping-container-idea-before-time-180963730/ — containers were used before McLean.
