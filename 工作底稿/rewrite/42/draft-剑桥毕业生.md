# EN: The Telescope That Needed Glasses

On 24 April 1990 the space shuttle Discovery **lofted** the Hubble Space Telescope into **orbital** flight. The **observatory** cost about $1.5 billion, a **lavish** **investment** carrying **considerable** scientific **aspiration**. It was **greeted** with **anticipation** and **optimism**, as the **payload** promised **celestial** **images** of high **fidelity** and **clarity**.

Its pictures soon came back **blurred**, and in June 1990 NASA announced a flaw in the primary mirror. Its **periphery** had been **flattened** by about 2.2 micrometres, roughly a fiftieth of the **width** of a human hair. That **negligible** **deviation** in the **optics** stopped the mirror bringing light to a **sharp** focus, **hampering** the telescope's **eyesight**.

The trouble lay not in the **grinding** but in the device checking the mirror's shape. One **lens** within it was **misplaced** by about 1.3 millimetres, so Perkin-Elmer **faithfully** met **incorrectly** set **tolerances**. A second, independent test gave a **divergent** result, but engineers and NASA managers judged it less **reliable** and left the **contradictory** reading **unexplored**.

No **adjustment** to the mirror was **feasible** in orbit, so engineers worked **downstream**. COSTAR, a refrigerator-sized **assembly**, carried small mirrors on movable arms that **intercepted** the light before reaching the instruments and **offset** its **distortion**. Like glasses, this **corrective** unit **compensated** for an **imperfect** eye. A new main camera was prepared too.

On 2 December 1993 Endeavour **ascended** with seven astronauts. After a **rendezvous**, each **duo** worked in **tandem** across five spacewalks, about 35 and a half hours in **aggregate**, **installing** COSTAR and the new camera. Its sight **renewed**, the telescope sent **crisp** images. The mirror stays **untouched**, its flaw **permanently** ground in and corrected only after light leaves it.
