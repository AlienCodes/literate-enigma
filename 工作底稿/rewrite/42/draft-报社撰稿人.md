# EN: The Telescope That Needed Glasses

On 24 April 1990 the space shuttle Discovery lifted an **exceptionally** **lavish** **investment** into orbit. **Cradled** in its **payload** bay was the Hubble Space Telescope, an **observatory** costing about $1.5 billion, built to fix its **gaze** on the **cosmos** and its **galaxies**. Money, it soon emerged, could not buy **clarity**.

Its first **snapshots** came back **blurred**. In June 1990 NASA broke the **tidings**, to widespread **dismay**. The main mirror was **imperfect**, its outer **rim** **flattened** by about 2.2 micrometres, roughly a fiftieth of the **width** of a hair. That slip **marred** the **optics**, leaving light without a **sharp** focus.

The trouble began with **calibration**. A **lens** in the instrument that checked the mirror had been **misplaced** by about 1.3 millimetres, so the mirror was ground with **faithful** **accuracy** to the wrong **curve**. A second, independent test by the contractor, Perkin-Elmer, gave a **divergent** result. Engineers and NASA managers judged it less **reliable** and **ignored** the **contradictory** reading.

The mirror could not be repaired in orbit, so engineers **prescribed** glasses. COSTAR, a **corrective** **cabinet** the size of a refrigerator, carried small mirrors on **adjustable** arms to **intercept** the light and **compensate** for the **distortion** before it reached the instruments. The **defective** mirror itself would stay **untouched**. A new main camera was also **commissioned**.

On 2 December 1993 Endeavour lifted off with seven astronauts. Working in **tandem**, they made five spacewalks totalling about 35 and a half hours of **exertion**, **installing** COSTAR and the new camera. The **images** turned **crisp** and Hubble's **vision** returned. The 2.2-micrometre error still **dwells** **unseen** in the mirror's **periphery**, its effect **quietly** **undone** **downstream**.
