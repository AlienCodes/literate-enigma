# EN: The Telescope That Needed Glasses

On 24 April 1990 the space shuttle Discovery **ascended** with a **gleaming** **payload**. The Hubble Space Telescope, a **hefty** **investment** of about $1.5 billion, was set loose in orbit as a **celestial** **observatory**. **Anticipation** ran high among astronomers, and its first **images** were **awaited** with **optimism**.

The **disappointment** came within two months. Images returned **blurred** and **fuzzy**, and in June 1990 NASA announced a flaw in the primary mirror: its **outer** **rim** had been ground too flat by about 2.2 micrometres. That **deviation**, roughly a fiftieth of the **width** of a hair, **sufficed**. Without a **sharp** focus, the **optics** saw the **cosmos** through **fog**.

Trouble lay in the **assembly** that checked the mirror's **contour**. One lens was **misplaced** by about 1.3 millimetres, so the contractor, Perkin-Elmer, **meticulously** ground the wrong **curve**. A second, independent ground test **contradicted** the first, but engineers and managers, **unconvinced**, **overruled** it as less accurate and **shrugged** off the conflict. Their **certainty** had **misled** them.

The mirror could not be fixed in orbit, so engineers thought like an **optometrist**. They built COSTAR, a **fridge-sized** **corrective** **attachment** whose small mirrors, on **adjustable** arms, **intercepted** the light and **refocused** it before it reached the instruments. The mirror was left **untouched**; the **correction**, like glasses, **compensated**. A new main camera was prepared too.

On 2 December 1993 Endeavour carried seven astronauts to a **rendezvous** with Hubble. In **tandem**, they made five spacewalks totalling about 35 and a half hours, **installing** COSTAR and the new camera. The **smear** was **tamed**; **vision** returned **crisp**. The mirror stayed **scarred**, but the light it **gathered** was **straightened** before anyone saw it.
