# EN: The Telescope That Needed Glasses

On 24 April 1990 the space shuttle Discovery **lofted** the Hubble Space Telescope into orbit. **Cradled** in the **payload** bay, the **observatory** had cost about $1.5 billion, a **lavish** **investment** meant to fix its **gaze** on the **cosmos** and its **galaxies**. Money, it soon emerged, could not buy **clarity**.

Its first **snapshots** came back **blurred** and **fuzzy**. In June 1990 NASA announced a flaw in the main mirror: its outer **rim** had been **flattened** by about 2.2 micrometres, roughly a fiftieth of the **width** of a hair. That **deviation** **marred** the **optics**, leaving light without a **sharp** focus.

The trouble began with **calibration**. A **lens** in the **assembly** that checked the mirror's shape had been **misplaced** by about 1.3 **millimetres**, so the mirror was ground to the wrong **curve**. A second, independent test by the contractor, Perkin-Elmer, gave a **divergent** result, but engineers and NASA managers judged it less **reliable** and left the **contradictory** reading **unexplored**.

The mirror could not be repaired in orbit, so engineers **prescribed** **tailored** glasses. COSTAR, a **corrective** **cabinet** the size of a refrigerator, carried small mirrors on **adjustable** arms that **intercepted** the light and **compensated** for the **imperfect** mirror's **distortion** before it reached the instruments. The mirror itself would stay **untouched**. A new main camera was prepared too.

On 2 December 1993 Endeavour **ascended** with seven astronauts. After a **rendezvous**, they worked in **tandem** across five spacewalks, about 35 and a half hours of **exertion**, **installing** COSTAR and the new camera. The **images** turned **crisp**, Hubble's **vision** **renewed**. The mirror still carries that 2.2-micrometre flaw, and the light it **gathers** is corrected only after leaving it.
