# 第55篇 来源摘录（选题核查员 + 本轮 WebSearch，2026-10-02）
S1 Wikipedia "20 Fenchurch Street"; vinoly.com; Dezeen: 20 Fenchurch Street ('the Walkie-Talkie'), City of London, designed by Rafael Viñoly; about 160 m tall, 37 storeys. Its south face curves inward (concave) and the upper floors are larger than the lower ones, adding floor area to the more valuable upper storeys. Completed 2014.
S2 Dezeen (2 Sep 2013); BBC via ODT/Peninsula; NPR (2015): the story broke on 2 September 2013 while the building was still under construction. Martin Lindsay had left his Jaguar XJ on Eastcheap, opposite the building, and returned to find the wing mirror, panels and Jaguar badge melted. The developers (Land Securities and Canary Wharf Group) apologised and paid for repairs (Wikipedia: £946; other reports: almost £1,000). Parking bays were suspended.
S3 National Geographic (4 Sep 2013); City A.M.: the reflected beam was up to six times brighter than direct sunlight and was measured at about 110°C. A City A.M. reporter fried an egg in a pan using only the reflected light. Nicknames: 'Walkie-Scorchie', 'Fryscraper'. The developers said the beam hit the street for about two hours a day for two to three weeks a year, because of the sun's elevation at that time of year.
S4 Guardian interview via Dezeen (Sep 2013): Viñoly said 'We made a lot of mistakes with this building' (paraphrase only); he knew the facade would focus sunlight but did not realise it would be so hot. The original design reportedly had horizontal sun louvres that would have prevented the glare, but they were removed to cut costs (write "reportedly").
S5 Architectural Record; Wikipedia "Vdara": Viñoly's Vdara hotel in Las Vegas, also with a concave face, drew complaints in September 2010 of a 'death ray' at the pool that burned a guest's scalp and melted plastic.
S6 Construction Enquirer (12 Feb 2014); Global News: a temporary screen went up in September 2013; on 12 February 2014 a permanent fix was announced — horizontal aluminium fins on the south facade to diffuse the light, installed in 2014, cost reported as "single-digit millions" of pounds.
S7 NPR, ITV, Architect Magazine (2 Sep 2015): Building Design magazine's critics unanimously awarded 20 Fenchurch Street the 2015 Carbuncle Cup for the UK's worst new building.
