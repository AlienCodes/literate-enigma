# EN: The Building That Melted a Car

On 2 September 2013, the story broke. A **skyscraper** under construction at 20 Fenchurch Street had damaged the **exterior** of a Jaguar **saloon** parked by the **pavement** on Eastcheap, opposite its **frontage**. Martin Lindsay found the wing mirror **distorted**, panels **deformed** and **badge** **warped**, as if **molten**.

The south **facade** curves inward, enlarging the more valuable upper **storeys** for **premium** space. This **glazed** **arc** acted as a **magnifying** glass, focusing **sunlight** into a **beam** up to six times brighter than direct sunlight. Measuring about 110 degrees Celsius, it let a **reporter** fry an egg in a **pan** with the **reflection**.

The **developers** **tendered** an **apology** and **reimbursed** almost 1,000 pounds for repairs. The beam struck the street about two hours a day, two to three weeks a year, owing to the sun's **angle** of **elevation**. To limit the **severity**, parking bays were suspended and a **provisional** **barrier** was **erected**.

The **architect**, Rafael Viñoly, was **candid**, admitting many mistakes. He **anticipated** the focusing but had not **grasped** the heat at the **focal** point. **Horizontal** louvres in the original design were reportedly removed for **cost-cutting**. Nor was this his first **architectural** **miscalculation**. In 2010 his concave Vdara hotel in Las Vegas burned a guest's **scalp** at the pool.

A permanent **fixture** followed on 12 February 2014. **Aluminium** **fins** on the south facade would **diffuse** the light and **soften** its **intensity**, reportedly costing several million pounds. In 2015 Building Design critics gave the **notorious** tower a **unanimous** Carbuncle Cup, the **trophy** for the UK's worst new building. The louvres reportedly dropped for **savings** had given way to a bill of millions.
