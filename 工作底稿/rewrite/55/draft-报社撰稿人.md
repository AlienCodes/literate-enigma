# EN: The Building That Melted a Car

On 2 September 2013, Martin Lindsay found his Jaguar on Eastcheap **disfigured**. The wing mirror had **warped**, body panels were **distorted** and the **badge** had **deformed**. Opposite rose an unfinished City of London **skyscraper**, 20 Fenchurch Street, whose **glazed** **facade** had thrown **sunlight** across the **pavement**.

Rafael Viñoly's 37 **storeys** **taper** downwards, the south face curving inward to enlarge upper floors, where **tenants** pay a **premium**. Like a **magnifying** glass, the **arc** made a **beam** up to six times brighter than direct sun, reaching about 110 degrees Celsius. A **reporter** fried an egg in a **pan** using it. The sun's **elevation** confined it to about two hours a day, two to three weeks a year.

The **developers** made an **apology** and **reimbursed** Lindsay almost a thousand pounds for repairs. Parking bays were suspended and a **provisional** screen was **erected** as a **barrier**. **Wags** called the tower the Walkie-Scorchie, turning the **fiasco** into an **embarrassment** on the London **skyline**.

The **architect** was **candid**, admitting many mistakes. He knew the **reflective** facade would focus sunlight but had not **anticipated** its **intensity**. Original **horizontal** louvres were reportedly **axed** to cut costs. The **miscalculation** **echoed** 2010, when his concave Vdara hotel in Las Vegas produced a pool "death ray" that burned a guest's **scalp**.

On 12 February 2014 a permanent fix was announced: **aluminium** **fins** to **diffuse** the **brightness**, at a reported cost of millions. In 2015 a **unanimous** **ballot** of Building Design's critics handed the **notorious** tower an **unflattering** **trophy**, the Carbuncle Cup for the UK's worst new building. Louvres reportedly dropped for **savings** had given way to fins costing millions.
