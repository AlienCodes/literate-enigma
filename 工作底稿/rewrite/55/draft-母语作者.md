# EN: The Building That Melted a Car

On 2 September 2013, a London **skyscraper** under construction was accused of **vandalism**. Martin Lindsay had left his Jaguar on Eastcheap, opposite the site. He returned to find its wing mirror **warped**, its panels **deformed** and its **badge** **distorted**. The **sabotage** had come from the **glossy** **sheen** above the **pavement**.

Rafael Viñoly gave 20 Fenchurch Street a concave south **facade**, an **arc** wider at the top, so the **premium** upper **storeys** offered **tenants** more space. That **geometry** made a **magnifying** surface, focusing **sunlight** into a **beam** up to six times brighter than direct sun and about 110 degrees Celsius. A **reporter** fried an egg in a **pan** with it.

The **developers** issued an **apology** and **reimbursed** almost 1,000 pounds for repairs. Parking bays were closed and a **barrier** went up. The beam, they explained, struck for only two hours a day, two to three weeks **annually**, owing to the sun's **elevation**. The **reassurance** made the **inconvenience** sound **tolerable** and the **embarrassment** no smaller.

Viñoly was **candid** about the **miscalculation**. He had **foreseen** the focusing, he said, but not its **intensity**. The original louvres were reportedly removed as a **cost-cutting** measure. London was a **replay** for the **architect**. In 2010 his Vdara hotel in Las Vegas, similarly curved and **reflective**, produced an **infamous** pool death ray that burned a guest's **scalp**.

In February 2014 came the fix: **horizontal** **aluminium** **fins** across the south face to **diffuse** the **sunshine**, reportedly costing millions. In September 2015, Building Design's critics gave the **notorious** tower the Carbuncle Cup, a **trophy** of **dishonour** for the UK's worst new building. The **ballot** was **unanimous**.
