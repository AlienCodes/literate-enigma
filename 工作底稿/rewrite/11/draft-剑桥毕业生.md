# EN: Microplastics: Everywhere, but How Harmful?

In May 2004 Richard Thompson, a **marine** **biologist** at the University of Plymouth, and colleagues reported in *Science* that **microscopic** plastic **fragments** and **fibres** had grown more **abundant** in the sea. **Plankton** samples **preserved** since the 1960s supplied the evidence; the paper **labelled** the **debris** microplastics.

The **particles** have since proved **ubiquitous**, turning up on **shorelines**, in deep-sea **sediment**, in **rainfall** and in Arctic ice: **durable** plastic breaks up rather than **degrading**. In December 2015 America **prohibited** microbeads, tiny plastic **beads** in **rinse**-off **cosmetics** such as face **scrubs**.

In March 2022 a small, **landmark** Dutch study found plastic in the blood of about three-quarters of 22 healthy **donors**. In March 2024 a study in the *New England Journal of Medicine* linked plastic in the **fatty** **plaque** of neck **arteries** to a higher risk of heart attack, **stroke** or death. Its authors **cautioned** that **correlation** is not **causation**.

The most **sensational** claims are the least **robust**. In June 2019 a report for WWF, a **conservation** group, **reckoned** we swallow a credit card's weight of plastic a week; critics call this **grossly** **inflated**. Two months later the World Health Organization reached a **sober** **verdict**: on limited evidence, microplastics in drinking water posed no **apparent** risk at current levels. In February 2025 a *Nature Medicine* study found plastic in brain **tissue**, soon **hyped** as a **spoonful**'s worth, though other scientists **queried** its **methodology**.

Scientists can now **pinpoint** microplastics almost anywhere; their effects on health remain **uncertain**. Harm is **plausible** but **unproven**. Until understanding catches up with **detection**, careful **measurement** may serve us better than **panic**.
