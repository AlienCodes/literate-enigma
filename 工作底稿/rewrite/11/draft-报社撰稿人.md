# EN: Microplastics: Everywhere, but How Harmful?

In May 2004 Richard Thompson, a **marine** **biologist** at the University of Plymouth, and colleagues reported in the journal Science on an **invisible** **pollutant**. **Sifting** **plankton** samples kept since the 1960s, they found **microscopic** plastic **fragments** and **synthetic** **fibres** had grown **steadily** more **abundant**. They **labelled** these **durable** **particles** microplastics.

They proved **ubiquitous**: on **shorelines**, in deep-sea **sediment**, in **rainfall**, in Arctic ice. Plastic seldom **decomposes**; it **disintegrates** and **persists**. In December 2015 America **prohibited** microbeads, tiny plastic **beads** in face **scrubs** and other **cosmetics** **rinsed** off.

The search turned **inward**. In March 2022 a small Dutch study found plastic in the blood of about three-quarters of 22 healthy **donors**. In March 2024 a New England Journal of Medicine study linked plastic in **fatty** **plaque** inside neck **arteries** to higher **odds** of heart attack, **stroke** or death within about three years. Its authors saw a **correlation**, not proof of **causation**.

The most **sensational** figures have not survived **scrutiny**. In June 2019 a report **commissioned** by WWF, a **conservation** group, suggested people swallow five **grams** a week, a credit card's weight; **critics** call it an **overestimate**. Two months later the World Health Organization was **cautious**: on limited evidence, microplastics in drinking water posed no **apparent** risk at current levels. In February 2025 a Nature Medicine study reported plastic in brain **tissue**, soon **hyped** as a **spoonful**. Others doubted its **methodology**, suspecting fat mistaken for plastic.

**Detection** has run ahead of understanding. Microplastics are easier to find than to judge, and until the **verdict** is in, **sober** **measurement** may serve better than **panic**.
