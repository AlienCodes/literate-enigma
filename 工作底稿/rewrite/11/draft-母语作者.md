# EN: Microplastics: Everywhere, but How Harmful?

In May 2004 Richard Thompson, a **marine** **biologist** at the University of Plymouth, and colleagues reported in Science on **plankton** samples **preserved** since the 1960s. They found **microscopic** **fragments** and **fibres** of plastic growing more **abundant**, and **labelled** these **particles** microplastics.

Plastic seldom **rots**; it **crumbles** into ever-finer **debris**. Microplastics have since turned up on **shorelines**, in deep-sea **sediment**, in **rainfall** and in Arctic ice. In December 2015 America **outlawed** one **culprit**: microbeads, tiny plastic **beads** in face **scrubs** and other **rinse-off** **cosmetics**.

Even the body is not **immune**. In March 2022 a small Dutch study found plastic in the blood of about three-quarters of 22 healthy **donors**, the first such **measurement**. In March 2024 a New England Journal of Medicine study linked plastic in the **fatty** **plaque** of patients' neck **arteries** with a higher risk of heart attack, **stroke** or death. Its authors **cautioned** that this was **correlation**, not **causation**.

Yet the most **sensational** claims are the **shakiest**. In June 2019 a report **commissioned** by WWF, a **conservation** group, **reckoned** people may swallow about five grams a week, a credit card's worth; **critics** call that **grossly** **inflated**. In February 2025 a Nature Medicine paper reported plastic in brain **tissue**, an amount **hyped** as a spoonful; its **methodology** has been **queried**. The World Health Organization had **concluded** in August 2019, on **sparse** evidence, that microplastics in drinking water posed no **apparent** risk at current **concentrations**.

Two decades on, microplastics are far easier to find than to **assess**. Until a firmer **verdict** arrives, it may be **prudent** to measure more and **panic** less.
