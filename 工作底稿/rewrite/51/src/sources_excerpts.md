# 第51篇 来源摘要（检索摘要，不得直接引用原文）
- S1 Wikipedia "Penny Black" / Linda Hall Library: before 1840, British postage was based on the size (number of sheets) of a letter and the distance it travelled, and the fee was usually collected from the recipient on delivery.
- S2 In 1837 Rowland Hill published the pamphlet "Post Office Reform: Its Importance and Practicability", proposing reform. In 1839 Parliament passed a bill turning his reform into law; the Uniform Penny Post began on 10 January 1840 (one penny per half-ounce letter anywhere in Britain, prepaid by the sender).
- S3 The Penny Black was issued on 1 May 1840 for use from 6 May (a few covers dated 1–2 May are known). It showed a profile of Queen Victoria aged about 15 (portrait engraved from a sketch of the young queen) — [note: sources say "profile of the young Queen Victoria"; one source: 21-year-old queen at issue]. 68,808,000 were printed. Sheets had to be cut with scissors (no perforations).
- S4 Mail volume: about 76–82 million items in 1839; more than 169 million in 1840.
- S5 The red cancellation mark was hard to see on the black stamp and the red ink was easy to remove, allowing re-use; in February 1841 the Treasury switched to the Penny Red and black cancellation ink.
- S6 As the originator of postage stamps, the UK is the only country whose stamps do not name the country; by agreement, the monarch's head suffices.
