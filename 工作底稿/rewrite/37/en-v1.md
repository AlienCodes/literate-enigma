# EN: The Curse of the Pharaoh

On 4 November 1922, in the Valley of the Kings, **labourers** **hired** by the archaeologist Howard Carter **uncovered** a **staircase** **carved** into the rock. On 26 November, with his **wealthy** **sponsor** Lord Carnarvon beside him, Carter made a small hole in the sealed **doorway** and glimpsed the **glittering** gold of the boy king Tutankhamun. The **spectacular** find **riveted** the world.

Carnarvon sold **exclusive** rights to The Times, a **monopoly** that left **competing** reporters **starved** of news. Many **resorted** to **speculation** or sought out outside **commentators**. In March 1923 the best-selling novelist Marie Corelli warned publicly that **dire** **punishment** awaited anyone who entered a sealed **tomb**. Within days, her words would seem **uncanny**.

That month a **mosquito** bit Carnarvon. He **nicked** the bite with a **razor** while **shaving**, it turned **septic**, and blood poisoning led to **pneumonia**. He died in Cairo on 5 April 1923. Newspapers reported that the city's lights failed at that **instant** and that his dog in England **howled** and died. Yet **blackouts** were almost a daily **occurrence** there.

No curse **inscription** was ever found in the tomb. In December 2002 a BMJ study traced the **fate** of 44 Westerners then in Egypt. The 25 **exposed** to the tomb died at an average age of 70, **versus** 75 for the rest, a **margin** that was not statistically **significant**. The **mortality** figures lent the **ominous** **rumour** no **credibility**.

Carnarvon's **untimely** **demise** had **imparted** **impetus** to the tale. Carter, the **excavator** who had **unsealed** the **burial** place, died in March 1939, aged 64, almost sixteen years after his **patron**. The legend had left him **conspicuously** **unharmed**.
