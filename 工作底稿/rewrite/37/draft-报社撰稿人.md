# EN: The Curse of the Pharaoh

On 4 November 1922, in the Valley of the Kings, **labourers** **hired** by the archaeologist Howard Carter **uncovered** the top of a **staircase** **carved** into the rock. On 26 November, his wealthy **sponsor** Lord Carnarvon beside him, Carter **drilled** a small hole in the sealed **doorway**. Beyond it **gleamed** the gold of the young king Tutankhamun, and the world was **riveted**.

Carnarvon sold **exclusive** rights to The Times, a **monopoly** that left other reporters little to report. They turned to **conjecture** and outside **commentators**. In March 1923 the best-selling novelist Marie Corelli warned publicly that **dire** **punishment** awaited anyone who entered a sealed **tomb**. Her **sinister** **prophecy** soon looked like an **omen**.

That month Carnarvon was **bitten** by a **mosquito**. He **nicked** the bite with his **razor** while **shaving**, it turned **septic**, and blood poisoning and **pneumonia** followed. He died in Cairo on 5 April 1923. Reportedly, the lights went out across the city at that moment, and his dog in England **howled** and died. Yet **blackouts** were **commonplace** there.

The **superstition** had no **anchor** in the tomb, where no curse **inscription** was ever found. In December 2002 a BMJ study **contrasted** the **fates** of 44 Westerners then in Egypt. The 25 exposed to the tomb died at an average age of 70, against 75 for the rest, a **margin** not statistically **significant**. The **mortality** record lent the **rumour** no **credibility**.

Carnarvon's **premature** **demise** had given the legend its **impetus**. Carter, the **excavator** who had opened the **burial** place, died in March 1939, aged 64. He had outlived his **patron** by sixteen years, **conspicuously** **unharmed** and **thoroughly** **mortal**.
