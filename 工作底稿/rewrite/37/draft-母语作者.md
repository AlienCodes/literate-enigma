# EN: The Curse of the Pharaoh

On 4 November 1922, in the Valley of the Kings, workers hired by the archaeologist Howard Carter **uncovered** a **staircase** **carved** into the rock. On 26 November, with his **wealthy** **sponsor** Lord Carnarvon beside him, Carter **chiselled** a hole in the **doorway** and saw the **glittering** gold of the young king Tutankhamun. The **excavation** **riveted** the world.

Carnarvon then sold **exclusive** rights to The Times, a **profitable** **monopoly** that left **competing** reporters **starved** of news. Many **resorted** to **fanciful** **speculation** and the views of outside **commentators**. In March 1923 the best-selling novelist Marie Corelli warned publicly that **dire** **punishment** awaited anyone who entered a sealed tomb.

That month a **mosquito** bit Carnarvon. He **nicked** the bite with a **razor** while **shaving**, it turned **septic**, and blood poisoning led to **pneumonia**. He died in Cairo on 5 April 1923, and **superstition** **seized** on his death. The city's lights reportedly failed at that moment and his dog in England allegedly **howled** and died, yet **blackouts** were almost a daily **occurrence** there.

No curse **inscription** was ever found in the tomb, and the **ominous** lines later **quoted** from it were **fabricated**. In December 2002 a BMJ study followed the **fate** of 44 Westerners then in Egypt. The 25 **exposed** to the tomb died at an average age of 70, against 75 for the rest, a **margin** judged not **significant**. The **mortality** data showed nothing **sinister**.

**Ironically**, the man who had **unsealed** the tomb met no **untimely** end. Carter died in March 1939, aged 64. The **prime** **candidate** for the **prophecy**'s **vengeance** **comfortably** **survived** his **patron** by sixteen years.
