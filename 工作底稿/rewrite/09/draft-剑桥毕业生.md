# EN: Apollo 13: The Successful Failure

On April 11th 1970 NASA, America's space agency, launched Apollo 13, its third **mission** to land men on the Moon. On April 13th, some 200,000 miles out, an **oxygen** tank **exploded** in the service **module**, which **generated** the ship's power. The **blast** **crippled** the **spacecraft**; the landing was **aborted**.

The crew moved into the **lunar** module, Aquarius, their lifeboat. Meant to **sustain** two men for about two days, it kept three alive for four. Its **descent** engine set a **trajectory** around the Moon and home, while the astronauts **rationed** water and **conserved** power in a **chilly** **cabin**.

A quieter **hazard** **emerged**: **carbon dioxide** was **accumulating**. The command module's **filters** were square; the lunar module's **sockets** were round. Ground engineers **devised** an **adapter** from plastic bags, **cardboard** and **duct** tape on board, and **dictated** the **procedure** to the crew, who **assembled** it. On April 17th their **capsule** **splashed** down in the Pacific.

Yet the **culprit** lay on the ground. In June 1970 a NASA review board **traced** the **explosion** to the tank's **thermostat** switches. Built for 28 **volts**, they were never **upgraded** when a 1965 **specification**, issued five years earlier, let the **heaters** use 65-volt ground power; no review **detected** the **mismatch**. Weeks before launch, a heater ran for about eight hours to **drain** the tank. The switches probably failed, **scorching** the **insulation** on **wiring** that later **ignited**.

NASA calls the mission a "successful failure", an **apt** **paradox**. A **trivial** **oversight** on the ground had cost the Moon; people on the ground who knew **precisely** what the ship carried brought the crew home.
