# EN: Apollo 13: The Successful Failure

Apollo 13 lifted off on April 11th 1970, the third **mission** meant to land on the Moon. On April 13th, some 200,000 miles out, an **oxygen** tank **exploded** in the service module, carrying **vital** oxygen and power. The **blast** **crippled** the **spacecraft**; the landing was **aborted**.

The **weary** crew of three **huddled** in the **lunar** **module**, Aquarius, built for two men for about two days. Its engine **propelled** them onto a **trajectory** around the Moon, whose gravity turned them towards Earth. Power and water were **rationed**; the **cabin** grew **bitterly** cold.

Then a **subtler** **peril** **accumulated**: **toxic** **carbon dioxide** **exhaled** by the **astronauts**. The command module, their **capsule**, held spare **filters**, but its square **cartridges** would not fit Aquarius's round **sockets**. Engineers on the ground **devised** an **adapter** from plastic bags, **cardboard** and tape aboard, and **relayed** the steps. On April 17th the capsule **splashed** down in the Pacific.

Yet the **explosion** was **assembled** on the ground. In June 1970 a review board from NASA, America's space agency, **traced** it to the tank's **thermostat** switches, a **safeguard** built for 28 **volts**. In 1965, five years earlier, a new **specification** let the **heaters** run on 65-volt ground power, but the switches stayed **unaltered**, an **oversight** the **contractor** and NASA missed. Weeks before launch, engineers **draining** the tank left its heater on for about eight hours. The switches probably **fused**, **scorching** the **insulation** on **wiring** inside. In flight, it **sparked**.

NASA came to call the mission a "successful failure". A **mismatch** on paper had **betrayed** it years earlier; engineers who knew **precisely** what the ship carried brought its crew home.
