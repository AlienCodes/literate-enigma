# EN: Apollo 13: The Successful Failure

On April 11th 1970 NASA, America's space agency, sent Apollo 13 to make the third crewed **lunar** landing. Two days later, some 200,000 miles from Earth, an **oxygen** tank **exploded** in the service module, the ship's **source** of air and power. The **blast** **crippled** the **craft**; the landing was **aborted**.

The three **astronauts** took **refuge** in the lunar **module**, Aquarius, **equipped** for two men and about two days. Its **descent** engine **steered** them onto a **trajectory** looping round the Moon and home. They **rationed** water and **electricity**, **shivering** in a **bitterly** cold **cabin**.

Then the air grew **toxic** with **carbon dioxide**. The command module's square **filter** **cartridges** would not fit Aquarius's round **sockets**. Engineers on the ground **devised** an **adapter** from plastic bags, **cardboard** and tape on board, and talked the crew through **assembling** it. On April 17th the astronauts **splashed** down in the Pacific.

Yet the **disaster** began on the ground. In June 1970 a NASA **investigation** **traced** the blast to the tank's 28-**volt** **thermostat** switches. In 1965 a new **specification** let the **heaters** run on 65-volt ground power, but nobody **upgraded** the switches, and no **inspection** caught the **mismatch**. Weeks before launch, a heater ran some eight hours to **drain** the **stubborn** tank; the switches probably **fused** shut, and the heat **stripped** **insulation** from the **wiring**. That **bare** wiring later **sparked** a fire that **ruptured** the tank.

NASA came to call Apollo 13 a successful failure. The **paradox** suits a flight lost to a **paperwork** **oversight** five years before launch and saved by **ingenuity** on the ground in four days.
