# EN: The First Shot Against Smallpox

**Contagious** smallpox killed about three in ten of its **sufferers**, and an estimated 300 million in the 20th century. One **safeguard**, **deliberately** **implanting** matter from **sores**, was **practised** in China by the 16th century, in India, the Ottoman Empire and, from 1721, England. The **procedure** itself could cause **grave** **sickness** or death, though less **frequently**.

**Dairy** **lore** held that cowpox, a **mild** **livestock** disease, **exempted** milkmaids from smallpox. In 1774 Benjamin Jesty, a Dorset farmer, had **harnessed** this, **employing** cowpox on his wife and two sons. On 14 May 1796 Edward Jenner, a **provincial** **practitioner** in Berkeley, Gloucestershire, **inserted** **fluid** from the milkmaid Sarah Nelmes's cowpox sore into the arm of James Phipps, aged 8.

On 1 July 1796 Jenner gave the boy smallpox matter, and he proved **immune**. Yet in 1797 the Royal Society declined to **publish** his **submission**, judging his **proof** insufficient. **Undaunted**, he **funded** his Inquiry **privately**, issuing it with **supplementary** cases in June 1798. The word vaccine **derives** from the **Latin** vacca, cow.

By today's standards, **experimentation** on a child without **informed** **consent** would be **unethical**. Jenner was not the idea's **originator**, nor did he **abolish** the disease. His **systematic** test, made **accessible** in print, let others **replicate** and **disseminate** the **technique**. He died on 26 January 1823, with smallpox far from **eliminated**.

In 1967 the WHO launched an **intensified** **initiative** to **eradicate** smallpox. The last **naturally** **occurring** case was **diagnosed** in Somalia on 26 October 1977. On 8 May 1980 the World Health Assembly **formally** declared smallpox eradicated, the only human disease so **vanquished**. Its **extinction** came 184 years after Phipps's **vaccination**.
