# EN: The First Shot Against Smallpox

Smallpox, a **contagious** **affliction**, killed about three in ten of its **sufferers** and an estimated 300 million in the 20th century. An early **safeguard**, **deliberately** **implanting** matter from its **sores** in the healthy, was **practised** in China by the 16th century and reached England in 1721. The **procedure** itself could cause **grave** **sickness** or death, if less **frequently** than natural smallpox.

**Rustic** **lore** held that milkmaids with cowpox, a **mild** **ailment** of **livestock**, were **immune** to smallpox. In 1774 Benjamin Jesty, a Dorset farmer, **employed** cowpox to protect his wife and two sons. On 14 May 1796 Edward Jenner, a **provincial** **practitioner**, **inserted** **fluid** from a milkmaid's cowpox sore into a **scratch** on eight-year-old James Phipps.

On 1 July 1796 Jenner exposed the boy to smallpox; no **symptoms** **ensued**. In 1797 the Royal Society declined to **publish** his paper, judging his evidence **unconvincing**. In June 1798 he **privately** **funded** his Inquiry's publication, with **supplementary** cases. The word **vaccine** **derives** from the **Latin** vacca, cow.

Jenner was not the idea's **originator**. His contribution was a test, with results **accessible** in print, so other doctors could **replicate** and **disseminate** the **technique**. By today's standards, **experimentation** on a child without **informed** **consent** would be **unethical**. He died on 26 January 1823, with smallpox far from **eliminated**.

In 1967 the WHO launched an **intensified**, **concerted** campaign to **eradicate** smallpox. The last **naturally** **occurring** case was **diagnosed** in Somalia on 26 October 1977. On 8 May 1980 the World Health Assembly **formally** declared the world free of smallpox, the only human disease ever **extinguished**. That **pronouncement** came almost 184 years after Phipps was **vaccinated**.
