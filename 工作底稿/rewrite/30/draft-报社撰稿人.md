# EN: The First Shot Against Smallpox

Smallpox, a **contagious** **affliction**, killed about three in ten of its **sufferers**, an estimated 300 million in the 20th century. An older **technique** **deliberately** **implanted** **pus** from its **sores** in healthy people. Known in China by the 16th century, it reached England in 1721, yet could kill, if far more **rarely** than smallpox caught **naturally**.

Rumour held that **dairy** maids who **acquired** cowpox, a **mild** cattle **ailment**, were **immune** to smallpox. In 1774 a Dorset farmer, Benjamin Jesty, **employed** cowpox on his wife and two sons. On 14 May 1796 Edward Jenner, a **provincial** **practitioner** in Berkeley, Gloucestershire, **deposited** **fluid** from a milkmaid's cowpox **eruption** into the arm of eight-year-old James Phipps.

On 1 July 1796 Jenner exposed the boy to smallpox matter, and no **symptoms** **ensued**. In 1797 the Royal Society declined to **publish** his paper, judging the **proof** **deficient**. In June 1798 he issued his self-**funded** Inquiry, **supplemented** with **additional** cases. The word **vaccine** **derives** from the **Latin** vacca, cow.

By today's standards, **experimentation** on a child without modern **consent** would be **unethical**. Jenner was not the first to **anticipate** that cowpox might **safeguard** people. His **achievement** was to test the idea **systematically**, **disseminate** the results and let others **adopt** and **replicate** the **procedure**. He died on 26 January 1823.

The **ultimate** **conquest** came long **afterwards**. In 1967 the WHO **initiated** an **intensified**, **concerted** campaign to **eliminate** smallpox **globally**. Somalia recorded the last naturally **occurring** case on 26 October 1977. On 8 May 1980 the World Health Assembly declared smallpox **eradicated**, still the only human disease **extinguished**. The boy had received cowpox 184 years before.
