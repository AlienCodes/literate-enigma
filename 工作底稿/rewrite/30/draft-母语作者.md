# EN: The First Shot Against Smallpox

Smallpox, **notoriously** **contagious**, killed about three in ten **sufferers** and an estimated 300 million people in the 20th century alone. An older **safeguard**, **deliberately** infecting the healthy with matter from **sores**, was **practised** in China by the 16th century and reached England in 1721. It **occasionally** killed, a **grave** **drawback**, though more rarely than smallpox.

**Rustic** **lore** held that milkmaids with cowpox, a **mild** **ailment** of cattle, were **exempt** from smallpox. In 1774 a farmer, Benjamin Jesty, had set a **precedent**, using cowpox on his wife and two sons. On 14 May 1796 Edward Jenner, a Gloucestershire **practitioner**, **inserted** **fluid** from a milkmaid's cowpox sore into a **scratch** on James Phipps, aged 8.

On 1 July 1796 Jenner exposed the boy to smallpox; no disease **ensued**. **Unconvinced**, the Royal Society declined in 1797 to publish his paper, judging the evidence insufficient. **Undaunted**, he **gathered** **supplementary** cases and in June 1798 issued his Inquiry, **funding** it **privately**. Vaccine **derives** from the Latin vacca, cow, **commemorating** its **dairy** **lineage**.

By today's standards, testing a **procedure** on a child without modern **consent** from a **guardian** would be **unethical**. With the results **accessible** in print, other doctors could **replicate** the **technique**. **Consequently** it was **embraced** and **diffused** far beyond Gloucestershire. Its **originator**, Jenner, died on 26 January 1823.

Others **ultimately** **accomplished** the **endeavour**. In 1967 the WHO **mounted** an **intensified**, **concerted** campaign. The last naturally occurring case was **diagnosed** in Somalia on 26 October 1977. On 8 May 1980 the World Health Assembly **formally** declared smallpox **eradicated**, the only human disease ever **eliminated**. Phipps had been **vaccinated** 184 years before that **pronouncement**.
