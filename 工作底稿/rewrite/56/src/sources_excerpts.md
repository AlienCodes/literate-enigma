# 第56篇 来源摘录（两轮 WebSearch，2026-10-02）
S1 IEEE Spectrum; PetaPixel; TechRadar; historyofinformation.com: in 1975 Kodak engineer Steven Sasson (aged about 24) built the first digital camera at Eastman Kodak in Rochester, New York. It weighed about 8 pounds (about 3.6 kg) and was the size of a toaster. In December 1975 it took its first picture, of lab technician Joy Marshall: a black-and-white image of 0.01 megapixels (100 x 100 pixels, 10,000 pixels) that took 23 seconds to record onto a cassette tape and was then shown on a television screen through a playback unit.
S2 New York Times interview with Sasson (via Harvard D3, PetaPixel): technical people liked it, but because it was filmless photography, management's reaction was roughly "that's cute — but don't tell anyone about it" (paraphrase only). Kodak's business depended on selling film. Sasson received a US patent in 1978; the camera was never put into production.
S3 AP / Washington Times / Fox News / Innovation Trail (19 Jan 2012): Kodak, the 131-year-old photography pioneer, filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection on 19 January 2012, weighed down by pension costs and a years-long delay in embracing digital technology. It had about 19,000 employees left, against about 145,000 at its peak in the 1980s; since 2003 it had cut 47,000 jobs and closed 13 factories and about 130 processing labs. It secured $950 million in financing from Citigroup.
S4 IndustryWeek; SEC 8-K (Dec 2012): Kodak sold/licensed its roughly 1,100 digital imaging patents for about $525 million to a group of 12 companies including Apple, Google, Samsung, Microsoft, Facebook and Amazon.
S5 Wikipedia "Kodachrome"; MPR (22 Jun 2009): Kodak announced in June 2009 that it would stop making Kodachrome film, citing declining demand in the digital age; processing ended in December 2010.
S6 Wikipedia "Steven Sasson"; USPTO 2009 laureates: on 17 November 2009, President Barack Obama awarded Sasson the National Medal of Technology and Innovation at the White House for inventing the digital camera.
S7 Bloomberg / Financier Worldwide (3 Sep 2013): Kodak emerged from bankruptcy on 3 September 2013 after 19 months as a smaller company focused on commercial printing, having shed cameras, consumer film sales and photo developing.
