# EN: The Company That Invented Its Own Killer

In 1975 Kodak engineer Steven Sasson built the first digital camera in Rochester. The **hefty**, **cumbersome**, **toaster**-sized **gadget** weighed about eight pounds. Its first picture, that December, was a **rudimentary** black-and-white image of technician Joy Marshall in 10,000 **pixels**. Recording it on a **magnetic** **cassette** took 23 seconds; **retrieval** required a **playback** unit and television.

The technical staff were **approving**; **management** was not. The camera was filmless, yet Kodak's **revenues** and **earnings** meant **dependence** on film. Sasson's **superiors**, he recalls, **reacted** by calling it cute and urging **secrecy**. Despite that **dismissive** response, he won a 1978 patent; the camera was never **commercially** produced.

The **ascent** of digital photography proved **disruptive**. In June 2009 Kodak announced the **discontinuation** of Kodachrome, its **analogue** **colour** film, citing **lessened** demand. The **shrinkage** was steep. From about 145,000 employees at its 1980s **apex**, Kodak had **slashed** 47,000 jobs since 2003 in **layoffs**, closing 13 factories and some 130 labs.

In January 2012 the 131-year-old firm filed for Chapter 11 **restructuring**, **sapped** by **pension** costs and years of digital **slowness**. Some 19,000 staff remained; its **lender**, Citigroup, provided a $950 million **loan**. Its **intellectual** **portfolio** of some 1,100 imaging patents fetched about $525 million from a **consortium** of twelve **buyers** including **giants** Apple, Google, Samsung, Microsoft, Facebook and Amazon.

In November 2009 President Obama **commended** the **innovative** engineer at the White House with national **laurels** for inventing the digital camera. After 19 months of **reorganisation**, Kodak left bankruptcy in 2013 as a **downsized**, **streamlined** firm **refocused** on **residual** commercial printing. The year of Sasson's medal also saw Kodak begin the **phased** **termination** of Kodachrome.
