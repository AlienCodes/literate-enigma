# EN: Saving the Sky

An **ozone** **layer** high in the **atmosphere** **shields** Earth from **hazardous** **ultraviolet** **radiation**. In June 1974 Mario Molina and F. Sherwood Rowland, chemists at the University of California, Irvine, warned that **inert** chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs), **emitted** by fridges and **spray** cans, could rise and free **chlorine**. Each **liberated** atom, a **catalyst**, would wreck ozone **molecule** after molecule: a **gradual** **erosion**.

The **alarm** came from the ground. In May 1985 Joseph Farman and colleagues at the British Antarctic Survey reported that over Halley Bay, their Antarctic station, ozone had **plummeted** every southern spring since the late 1970s. Their Dobson spectrophotometer was a **humble**, **vintage** **gauge**; their **routine**, kept through **harsh** winters, was **painstaking** and **persistent**.

NASA's **cutting-edge** Nimbus-7 **satellite** had **detected** the same low **measurements**. Yet its software flagged readings that low as **dubious**, a **probable** **glitch**: none had ever been reliably observed. NASA was still **verifying** them when Farman published. The **sensor** had worked; **expectation** had not.

**Remarkably**, the world **cooperated**. In September 1987 **negotiators** signed the Montreal **Protocol**, a **binding** **accord** to **curb** CFCs. A London **amendment** in June 1990 **tightened** it: rich countries would **phase** out CFC **manufacture** by 2000. In September 2009 the Protocol and the Vienna Convention became the first treaties in UN history ratified by every country.

**Recovery** will be **sluggish**. A UN-backed **assessment** in January 2023 **forecast** the layer's return to 1980 levels around 2066 over Antarctica, 2045 over the Arctic and 2040 elsewhere. The sky's **healing** will **span** decades; it began with people on the ice who trusted **unlikely** readings, and a world that **heeded** them.
