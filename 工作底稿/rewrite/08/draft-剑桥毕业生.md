# EN: Saving the Sky

A thin **layer** of **ozone** **shields** the Earth from **hazardous** **ultraviolet** **radiation**. In June 1974 Mario Molina and F. Sherwood Rowland, chemists at the University of California, Irvine, argued in Nature that chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs), **inert** gases in fridges and **spray** cans, could **release** **chlorine** in the stratosphere. There, as a **catalyst**, each atom would destroy many ozone **molecules**. They **anticipated** a **gradual** **erosion**.

The **alarm**, when it came, was **abrupt**. In May 1985 Joseph Farman's team at the British Antarctic Survey, Britain's polar research body, reported in Nature that ozone over their Halley Bay base was **plummeting** each southern spring. They **relied** on a **vintage**, ground-based Dobson spectrophotometer and years of **painstaking**, **routine** **measurement**.

Yet **sophisticated** eyes in **orbit** had seen it too. NASA's Nimbus-7 **satellite** **detected** the same low values, but its software flagged them as **dubious**, since none so low had been reliably measured. NASA was still **verifying** them when Farman's paper **emerged**. The **defect** lay not in the **sensor** but in an **assumption**.

**Diplomats** moved **swiftly**. In September 1987 governments signed the Montreal Protocol, a **binding** **accord** to **curb** CFCs. **Amendments** **tightened** it: in London in June 1990 **affluent** countries **pledged** to **phase** them out by 2000. In September 2009 the protocol and its parent treaty became the first treaties in UN history to win **universal** ratification.

A UN-backed **assessment** in January 2023 **forecast** **recovery** to 1980 levels around 2066 over Antarctica, 2045 over the Arctic and 2040 elsewhere. Full **healing** lies decades off; the **rescue** began when a **humble** instrument's **diligent** **custodians** took its most **baffling** readings seriously.
