# EN: The Man Who Sold the Eiffel Tower Twice
# ZH: 两次卖掉埃菲尔铁塔的骗子

## 英文
In 1925 six **unsuspecting** scrap-metal dealers met **secretly** at the Hôtel de Crillon in Paris. **Confidentially**, a **deputy** director-general of the Ministry of Posts and Telegraphs told them that the Eiffel Tower was to be sold for scrap. He invited **tenders**, warning that **disclosure** would provoke public **outrage**, and made each **trader** **solemnly** **vow** **secrecy**.

The deputy was Victor Lustig, a **crook** whose **ministerial** stationery was a **forgery**. His **pretext**, **nevertheless**, was **credible**. Built for the 1889 World's Fair, the tower had **originally** been **destined** to come down after twenty years. Lustig had also read a newspaper article in which the authorities were **lamenting** the **hefty** cost of its **upkeep**.

André Poisson, an **entrepreneur** **keenly** seeking a **foothold** in the **mercantile** life of the **metropolis**, emerged **victorious**. **Obliquely**, Lustig suggested that an official's **remuneration** needed **supplementing**, and Poisson paid an **additional** **bribe**. Lustig left for Vienna with the **takings**.

The **fleeced** buyer was too **ashamed** to approach the police, and the newspapers printed not a **whisper** of the **deceit**.

About a month later Lustig was back, **brazenly** staging a **replay** of his **script** for fresh **prey**. This time the chosen **contestant** grew **uneasy** and took his **distrust** to the police. The **affair** reached the newspapers, and Lustig made his **getaway**. In other words, the second sale of legend is **mythical**.

Later, in the United States, Lustig was caught for counterfeiting, broke out of **detention** and was **recaptured** twenty-seven days afterwards. After **incarceration** on the **penal** island of Alcatraz, he died of pneumonia in 1947. Of the two **transactions** credited to him, only the first **actually** earned him a franc.

## 中文

1925年，六名**毫无防备**(unsuspecting)的废铁商在巴黎克里雍酒店**秘密**(secretly)碰头。邮电部一位**副**(deputy)总长**私下**(confidentially)告诉他们，埃菲尔铁塔将作为废铁出售。他请大家**投标**(tenders)，提醒说一旦**走漏风声**(disclosure)，就会激起公众的**愤慨**(outrage)，还让每位**商人**(trader)都**郑重**(solemnly)地**立誓**(vow)严守**秘密**(secrecy)。

这位副总长其实是维克多·卢斯蒂格，一个**骗子**(crook)，他那套**部里**(ministerial)的信笺是**伪造品**(forgery)。**不过**(nevertheless)，他的**托词**(pretext)倒也**可信**(credible)。铁塔是为1889年世博会而建的，**原本**(originally)**注定**(destined)二十年后就要拆除。卢斯蒂格还读到过一篇报道，当局在文中**抱怨**(lamenting)铁塔的**维护**(upkeep)费用**高昂**(hefty)。

安德烈·普瓦松是一位**生意人**(entrepreneur)，正**一心**(keenly)想在这座**大都会**(metropolis)的**商界**(mercantile)站稳**脚跟**(foothold)，结果他**胜出**(victorious)了。卢斯蒂格**拐弯抹角**(obliquely)地暗示，官员的**薪酬**(remuneration)需要**补贴**(supplementing)一下，普瓦松便**额外**(additional)付了一笔**贿赂**(bribe)。卢斯蒂格带着这笔**进账**(takings)去了维也纳。

**上当受骗**(fleeced)的买家**羞于**(ashamed)报警，报纸上对这场**骗局**(deceit)**只字未提**(whisper)。

大约一个月后，卢斯蒂格又回来了，**明目张胆**(brazenly)地照着原来的**剧本**(script)，向一批新的**猎物**(prey)**重演**(replay)这一幕。这一次，被选中的**中标者**(contestant)感到**不安**(uneasy)，带着**疑虑**(distrust)报了警。**事情**(affair)见了报，卢斯蒂格**溜之大吉**(getaway)。换句话说，传说中的第二次买卖纯属**虚构**(mythical)。

后来，卢斯蒂格在美国因伪造货币被捕，从**羁押**(detention)中逃脱，二十七天后**再次落网**(recaptured)。他在恶魔岛这座**监狱**(penal)岛上**服刑**(incarceration)，1947年死于肺炎。在算到他名下的两笔**交易**(transactions)中，**真正**(actually)让他赚到一法郎的，只有第一次。

## 史实与来源
- 【T1】埃菲尔铁塔为1889年世博会而建，原计划二十年后拆除（英文 originally destined to come down）；1925年卢斯蒂格读到报道，当局抱怨铁塔维护费用高昂（hefty upkeep）。S1
- 【T2】卢斯蒂格伪造政府信笺（ministerial stationery…forgery），冒充邮电部副总长，邀六名废铁商到克里雍酒店密谈（secretly, confidentially），称铁塔将作为废铁出售，要他们发誓保密以免公众抗议（disclosure…outrage, solemnly vow secrecy），并请他们投标（tenders）。"credible"是对他们信以为真的概括。S2
- 【T3】普瓦松急于在巴黎商界立足（keenly seeking a foothold），中标；卢斯蒂格暗示官员薪水微薄需要补贴（obliquely, remuneration, supplementing），普瓦松在价款之外另付贿赂（additional bribe）；卢斯蒂格带钱去了维也纳。不写金额。S3
- 【T4】普瓦松羞于报警（ashamed），报上没有任何消息（not a whisper）。S4
- 【T5】约一个月后卢斯蒂格回来，对另一批废铁商故技重施（replay of his script）；这次被选中的买家起疑报警（uneasy, distrust），事情见报，卢斯蒂格逃走（getaway）。第二次并未成交，英文写明"传说中的第二次买卖纯属虚构"。S5
- 【T6】后来他在美国因伪造货币被捕，越狱（broke out of detention），27天后再次落网，关进恶魔岛，1947年死于肺炎。结尾"只有第一次真正让他赚到钱"据T3、T5。S6
- 【说明】正文无直接引语；金额不写数字，"a franc"仅为修辞性最小单位，不代表具体金额。中文不添英文以外的信息。
