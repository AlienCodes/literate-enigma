# EN: Dynamite for the Heart

Around 1847 the Italian chemist Sobrero first made nitroglycerin, an **oily**, **colourless** **liquid** of **acute** **instability**. A **mere** **droplet** on the tongue caused a **fierce**, **hours-long** headache. On 3 September 1864 a shed preparing nitroglycerin at Heleneborg, Stockholm, exploded. The **tragic** blast killed five, **notably** Alfred Nobel's younger brother, Emil.

In the **aftermath**, Nobel **blended** the liquid with kieselguhr, a **porous** earth; the resulting **paste** could be shaped into **rods**. Patented in 1867 as dynamite, this **tamed** form was **harnessed** for mining, **quarrying**, construction and demolition, **enriching** him **immensely**. His 1895 **testament** **dedicated** more than 31 million kronor to prizes, first awarded in 1901.

Factory **operatives** handling nitroglycerin suffered **racking** headaches. Their **tolerance** **strengthened** over the working week and **evaporated** at weekends, **hence** Monday disease, a weekly **relapse** of headaches and **dizziness**. In 1879 William Murrell reported in The Lancet a **therapeutic** use for nitroglycerin: **relieving** angina pectoris, **recurrent** attacks of chest pain.

On 25 October 1896 Nobel, suffering from angina, wrote to his **aide** Sohlman that being prescribed nitroglycerin **internally** was an irony of fate. **Disguised** under the **alias** Trinitrin, it would not **scare** **pharmacists** or the public, he added. He did not **comply**. **Partially** **paralysed** by a stroke, he died at his San Remo **villa** on 10 December 1896.

In 1977 Murad showed that nitroglycerin releases nitric oxide, which **widens** vessels. **Vascular** **linings**, Furchgott found in 1980, **generate** an **unidentified** relaxing **factor**, which Ignarro **ascertained** in 1986 was nitric oxide. The **trio** shared the 1998 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine, and, on the anniversary of Nobel's death, its **presentation** speech **quoted** his letter.
