# 第53篇 来源摘录（两轮 WebSearch 核查，2026-10-02）
S1 Chemistry World "Dynamite cure"; encyclopedia.pub (GTN history): nitroglycerin (glyceryl trinitrate) first made by Italian chemist Ascanio Sobrero around 1847; Sobrero reported that a very minute quantity on the tongue produced a violent headache lasting several hours. He considered it too dangerous.
S2 Wikipedia "Dynamite"/"Alfred Nobel"; nobelprize.org "Alfred Nobel – his life and work": on 3 September 1864 a shed preparing nitroglycerin at Heleneborg, Stockholm, exploded, killing five people including Nobel's younger brother Emil. Nobel set out to make a safer explosive: mixing nitroglycerin with kieselguhr (a porous diatomaceous earth) turned the liquid into a paste that could be shaped into rods; patented in 1867 as dynamite. Dynamite was used for mining, quarrying, construction and demolition. Nobel made a vast fortune.
S3 The Local / AFP: his 1895 will left his fortune to a fund for prizes; about 31.5 million kronor created the Nobel Foundation; first prizes awarded 1901.
S4 Chemistry World / encyclopedia.pub: explosives workers suffered severe headaches ("bang head"); tolerance built over the working week and faded at weekends, so Mondays brought headaches, dizziness and a racing heart ("Monday disease"). In 1879 William Murrell described in The Lancet the use of nitroglycerin to treat angina pectoris (attacks of chest pain). [Popular claim that workers' angina vanished on weekdays is less well attested — do not write it as Murrell's motive.]
S5 nobelprize.org "Alfred Nobel's health and his interest in medicine": in his last years Nobel suffered angina pectoris. Letter to his assistant Ragnar Sohlman, 25 October 1896: he called it the irony of fate that his doctors had prescribed nitroglycerin to be taken internally, and said they called it Trinitrin so as not to alarm the pharmacists and the public (paraphrase only). He refused / did not follow the prescription. He suffered a stroke (cerebral haemorrhage), was partly paralysed, and died at his villa in San Remo, Italy, at 2 a.m. on 10 December 1896.
S6 nobelprize.org 1998 press release; Britannica: 1998 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine to Robert Furchgott, Louis Ignarro and Ferid Murad for discoveries concerning nitric oxide as a signalling molecule in the cardiovascular system. Murad (1977) showed nitroglycerin releases nitric oxide, which relaxes/widens blood vessels; Furchgott (1980) found the vessel lining produces an unknown relaxing factor (EDRF); Ignarro (1986, independently) identified it as nitric oxide. A gas made by one cell passing through membranes to regulate another was an entirely new principle of signalling.
S7 nobelprize.org 1998 ceremony speech: delivered by Sten Lindahl on 10 December 1998; it noted Nobel suffered angina and quoted his "irony of fate" letter. Prize ceremonies are held on 10 December, the anniversary of Nobel's death.
S1b Wikipedia "Nitroglycerin" / Britannica: nitroglycerin is a dense, colourless (to pale yellow), oily liquid; highly unstable and shock-sensitive in pure form (supports "colourless, oily liquid of acute instability").
