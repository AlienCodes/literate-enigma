# EN: A Handful of Wormwood That Saved Millions

It began as an **urgent**, **confidential** **military** order. Project 523, a **covert** **assignment** named after its launch date, 23 May 1967, targeted chloroquine-**resistant** **malaria** that **afflicted** troops on Vietnam's **battlefields**. In January 1969 Tu Youyou, without a **doctorate** or foreign training, was **entrusted** with its research group in Beijing.

In an **exhaustive** search, her team **combed** more than 2,000 **medicinal** **formulae**, **compiled** 640 and tested about 380 extracts from some 200 **herbs** on **rodents**. One **contender**, qinghao, or sweet wormwood, showed **flashes** of promise, yet its effect **fluctuated**, and the reason was an **enigma**.

The **clue** lay in Ge Hong's **handbook** of **prescriptions**, which around AD 340 advised **sufferers** to take "a handful of qinghao immersed in two litres of water". Nothing was boiled. Tu suspected heat **degraded** a **delicate** **component** and switched to ether, a **volatile** **solvent** boiling at about 35 degrees Celsius. On 4 October 1971 sample 191 **inhibited** the mouse **parasite** completely.

In July 1972 Tu and two colleagues **consumed** the extract to **assess** its **toxicity**. From August to October it was **administered** to 21 **infected** patients in Hainan. On 8 November her team **obtained** pure **crystals** of the **substance**, later named artemisinin. Many institutions **collaborated**; her group's **extraction** proved **pivotal**.

In September 2011 Tu became a Lasker Award **recipient**; its **jury** credited the drug with saving millions of lives. On 5 October 2015 she won half of the Nobel Prize in **Physiology** or Medicine, the first Chinese woman so honoured. Since 2006 WHO **guidelines** have **endorsed** artemisinin-based **combination** **therapies** for malaria's **deadliest** form. The **essence** of Ge Hong's line was a step it **skipped**.
