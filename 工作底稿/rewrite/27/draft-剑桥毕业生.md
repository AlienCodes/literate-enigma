# EN: A Handful of Wormwood That Saved Millions

Project 523, a **covert** **military** **initiative** spanning many **institutions**, was named after its launch date, 23 May 1967. Its **urgent** **mission** was to **confront** drug-**resistant** **malaria** among **troops** in the Vietnam War. In January 1969 Tu Youyou was **appointed** to lead her institute's team, without a **doctorate** or study abroad.

Her team **combed** China's medical **heritage**. They examined over 2,000 recipes, **compiled** 640, and tested about 380 extracts from some 200 **herbs**. Qinghao, or sweet wormwood, showed promise, but its results were **erratic**. Each **setback** fed **mounting** **frustration**; the **clue** stayed **elusive**.

Tu **revisited** Ge Hong's **handbook** of **prescriptions** from around AD 340. It advised "a handful of qinghao immersed in two litres of water", without boiling. She **suspected** heat during **extraction** was **degrading** the active **component**, so her team switched to **ether**, a **solvent** boiling at about 35 degrees Celsius. On 4 October 1971 sample 191 **inhibited** the **parasite** in mice completely.

**Efficacy** in mice did not **confirm** human safety. In July 1972, to **assess** its **toxicity**, Tu and two colleagues **courageously** took the extract themselves. From August to October 1972 it was **administered** to 21 patients in Hainan. On 8 November the team **obtained** **pure** **crystals** of the **medicinal** **substance** later named artemisinin.

Her Lasker Award came in September 2011; its **jury** credited the drug with saving millions of lives. On 5 October 2015 she **secured** half the Nobel Prize in **Physiology** or Medicine, the first Chinese woman to win any Nobel. Since 2006 the WHO has **endorsed** artemisinin-based **combination** **therapies** as **first-line** treatment for malaria's **deadliest** form. The answer had **resided** in an **omission**.
