# EN: A Handful of Wormwood That Saved Millions

Project 523, a **covert** **military** **mission** involving many institutes, was named after its launch date, 23 May 1967. Its **urgent** target was chloroquine-**resistant** **malaria** **weakening** troops in the Vietnam War. In January 1969 Tu Youyou was **entrusted** with her institute's research group, **despite** having no **doctorate** or foreign training.

In an **exhaustive** search, her team **combed** more than 2,000 **folk** recipes, **compiled** 640 **prescriptions** and tested about 380 extracts from some 200 **medicinal** **herbs**. Qinghao, or sweet wormwood, showed **flashes** of promise, yet its results **fluctuated** **wildly**. Seeking a **clue**, Tu **revisited** the **classical** texts.

Around AD 340, Ge Hong's **handbook** advised taking "a handful of qinghao immersed in two litres of water". Nothing was boiled. Tu suspected heat during **extraction** was **degrading** a **delicate** **component**, so she switched to **ether**, a **volatile** **solvent** boiling at about 35 degrees Celsius. On 4 October 1971 sample 191 **inhibited** the **parasite** in **infected** mice completely.

**Efficacy** in mice did not guarantee human safety. In July 1972, to **assess** its **toxicity**, Tu and two colleagues **consumed** the extract themselves. **Thereafter**, from August to October 1972, it was **administered** to 21 **feverish** patients in Hainan, and on 8 November her team **obtained** pure **crystals** of the **substance** later named artemisinin.

In September 2011 Tu became a Lasker Award **recipient**; its **jury** credited the drug with saving millions. On 5 October 2015 she won half the Nobel Prize in **Physiology** or Medicine, the first Chinese woman to receive a Nobel. Since 2006 WHO **guidelines** have **endorsed** artemisinin-based **combination** **therapies** against malaria's **deadliest** form. The **essence** of Ge Hong's line was a step it **skipped**.
