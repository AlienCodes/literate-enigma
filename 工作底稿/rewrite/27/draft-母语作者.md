# EN: A Handful of Wormwood That Saved Millions

Project 523, a **covert** **military** **mission** **involving** many institutes, was named after its launch date, 23 May 1967. Its **urgent** target was chloroquine-**resistant** **malaria** among Vietnam War troops. In January 1969 Tu Youyou was **assigned** to lead her institute's team, **despite** having no **doctorate** and never having studied abroad.

Her team **combed** more than 2,000 traditional recipes, **compiled** 640 **prescriptions** and tested about 380 extracts from some 200 **medicinal** **herbs**. Qinghao, or sweet wormwood, looked **potent**, yet its results were **erratic**, **effective** one time and **ineffective** the next. Seeking a **clue**, Tu **revisited** the **classical** texts.

Around AD 340 Ge Hong's **treatise** had **specified** "a handful of qinghao immersed in two litres of water", with no boiling. Suspecting heat during **extraction** was **degrading** the **critical** **component**, Tu switched to ether, a **volatile** **solvent** boiling at about 35 degrees Celsius. On 4 October 1971 sample 191 achieved a **remarkable** 100 per cent **inhibition** of the **parasite** in **infected** mice.

To **evaluate** its **toxicity**, in July 1972 Tu and two colleagues **courageously** **consumed** it themselves. **Thereafter**, from August to October 1972, it was **administered** to 21 malaria **sufferers** in Hainan. On 8 November 1972 her team **obtained** pure **crystals** of the **substance**, the drug later called artemisinin.

In September 2011 Tu won the Lasker Award, whose **jury** credited the drug with saving millions. On 5 October 2015 she won half the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine, becoming China's first female Nobel **recipient**. Since 2006 the WHO has **endorsed** artemisinin-based **combination** **therapies** as **frontline** treatment for the **deadliest** malaria. Ge Hong's **counsel** to **soak**, not boil, had **illuminated** the way.
