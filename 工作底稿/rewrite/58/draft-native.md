# EN: The Virus That Saved a Life
# ZH: 病毒盟友：噬菌体

## 英文
In late 2015 Tom Patterson, a 69-year-old psychiatry professor at UC San Diego, fell ill on holiday in Egypt. He was flown home via Frankfurt. His Acinetobacter baumannii infection **shrugged** off every available **antibiotic**. When a drain slipped, **bacteria** spread into his bloodstream, and he went into septic shock and a **coma**.

Patterson's rescuer had been found a century earlier. In 1917 Félix d'Hérelle, at the Institut Pasteur, studied a dysentery patient's samples and found a virus that infects bacteria. He called it a **bacteriophage**, literally a "bacteria-eater", and proposed it as a treatment. Frederick Twort had independently reported a similar agent two years before.

After the Second World War, with penicillin at hand and early preparations less reliable, the West largely abandoned phage therapy. It persisted in Soviet Georgia, where George Eliava founded a Tbilisi institute in 1923. D'Hérelle worked there in 1933–35. Eliava was executed in 1937, yet phage preparations came to be sold even **over-the-counter**.

Patterson's wife, the epidemiologist Steffanie Strathdee, sought researchers with phages against his strain. Teams at Texas A&M and the US Navy found matching ones. In March 2016 he became America's first patient given intravenous phages for a **systemic** multidrug-resistant infection. About three days later he woke. He was **discharged** in August and returned to work.

Antibiotic resistance, now a growing global threat, has **stirred** fresh interest in phage therapy. Patterson's case does not prove phages can replace antibiotics, only that an old idea deserves another look. A remedy the West had discarded returned through a Navy collection drawn partly from **sewage**, which cities try to **flush** away.

## 中文
2015年末，加州大学圣迭戈分校69岁的精神病学教授汤姆·帕特森在埃及度假时病倒，随后经法兰克福被送回国。感染他的鲍曼不动杆菌对所有可用的**抗生素**(antibiotic)都**满不在乎**(shrugged)。一根引流管滑脱后，**细菌**(bacteria)进入血液，他陷入感染性休克和**昏迷**(coma)。

救帕特森的东西，早在一百年前就已被发现。1917年，巴斯德研究所的费利克斯·德埃雷勒研究一名痢疾病人的样本，发现了一种感染细菌的病毒。他把它叫作**噬菌体**(bacteriophage)，字面意思是"吃细菌者"，并提出可以用它治病。弗雷德里克·特沃特在两年前已独立报道过类似的病原。

第二次世界大战后，青霉素唾手可得，早期噬菌体制剂又不够可靠，西方基本放弃了噬菌体疗法。它在苏联的格鲁吉亚延续下来。1923年，乔治·埃利亚瓦在第比利斯创办了一家研究所。德埃雷勒1933至1935年在那里工作。埃利亚瓦于1937年被处决，但噬菌体制剂后来甚至可以在**柜台直接购买**(over-the-counter)。

帕特森的妻子、流行病学家斯特芬妮·斯特拉斯迪四处寻找手中有对付这株细菌的噬菌体的研究者。德州农工大学和美国海军的团队找到了匹配的噬菌体。2016年3月，他成为美国第一位静脉注射噬菌体治疗**全身性**(systemic)多重耐药感染的病人。大约三天后，他醒了过来。他于8月**出院**(discharged)，并重返工作岗位。

抗生素耐药如今已成为日益严重的全球威胁，也**激起**(stirred)了人们对噬菌体疗法的新兴趣。帕特森的病例并不能证明噬菌体可以取代抗生素，只说明这个老办法值得再看一眼。一种被西方丢弃的疗法，借由一批部分取自**污水**(sewage)的海军藏品重新归来，而污水正是城市想方设法要**冲走**(flush)的东西。
