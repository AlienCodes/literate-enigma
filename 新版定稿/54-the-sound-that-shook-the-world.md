# EN: The Sound That Shook the World
# ZH: 震动世界的一声巨响
## 英文
Shortly after ten in the morning on 27 August 1883, Krakatoa, a **volcanic** island in the Sunda **Strait**, **unleashed** its most **thunderous** explosion. About four hours later, **islanders** on Rodrigues, nearly 4,800 kilometres across the Indian Ocean, took the **roar** for **distant** **gunfire**. **Barometers** worldwide recorded the **atmospheric** pressure wave, some of them seven times in about five days.

**Restless** since 20 May, the volcano reached a **catastrophic** **climax** on 26–27 August. About two-thirds of the island collapsed into its **emptied** **magma** chamber, vanishing beneath the sea. **Tsunamis**, **reportedly** over 30 metres high in places, **swamped** **coastal** **settlements** on Java and Sumatra. Dutch **colonial** **administrators** counted 36,417 dead, mostly **drowned**, perhaps **underestimating** the toll.

**Telegrams** from Batavia (now Jakarta), **relayed** by **submarine** **cable**, reached London within hours. Western newspapers soon began **chronicling** what is often called the first global news event. Yet early **dispatches** were **terse**; the **magnitude** of the **devastation** emerged **piecemeal** over days.

**Ash** and **sulphur** dioxide, **hurled** tens of kilometres high, formed a **haze** that circled the **equator** in about two weeks. In November 1883, **crimson** sunsets **glowed** over Europe and North America, **summoning** **firefighters** in New York and Connecticut to **phantom** **blazes**. Later climate **reconstructions** suggest global temperatures **dipped** a few tenths of a degree the following year.

Some experts **discounted** the volcanic explanation, blaming the sunsets on **moisture** **at high altitude** or **cosmic** dust. In February 1884, Britain's Royal Society appealed in The Times for dated observations, **eliciting** worldwide replies; the Society's 1888 report tied the glow to Krakatoa. Through barometers, cables, and crimson skies, **far-flung** **observers** began to **glimpse** how **interconnected** the planet already was.

**电报**(Telegrams)从巴达维亚（今雅加达）发出，经**海底**(submarine)**电缆**(cable)**转发**(relayed)，传到了伦敦，前后只用了几小时。（relayed by submarine cable：过去分词短语作后置定语，修饰 Telegrams）西方报纸很快便开始**记述**(chronicling)这起常被称为首个全球新闻事件的灾难。（what is often called…：what 引导的宾语从句，作 chronicling 的宾语）然而，早期的**电讯**(dispatches)都十分**简短**(terse)；**浩劫**(devastation)的**规模**(magnitude)**一点一点地**(piecemeal)显露出来，前后历经数日。（the magnitude of the devastation：of 结构，浩劫的规模；over days：在几天的时间里）

**火山灰**(Ash)和二氧化**硫**(sulphur)被**抛射**(hurled)到数十公里的高空，形成一层**薄雾**(haze)，它绕**赤道**(equator)一圈约用了两个星期。（hurled tens of kilometres high：过去分词短语作后置定语，修饰 Ash and sulphur dioxide）（that circled…：定语从句，修饰 haze）1883年11月，**深红色的**(crimson)晚霞**灼灼发亮**(glowed)，照耀着欧洲和北美，**招来**(summoning)纽约州和康涅狄格州的**消防员**(firefighters)赶去扑救**子虚乌有的**(phantom)**大火**(blazes)。（summoning…：现在分词短语作状语，补充说明结果；summon A to B：把 A 召到 B 处）后来的气候**重建**(reconstructions)研究推测，全球气温**下降了**(dipped)零点几度，就在次年。（climate reconstructions：气候重建，根据各种证据推算过去的气候）（suggest 后面省略了 that，引导宾语从句；a few tenths of a degree：零点几度）

一些专家**不予采信**(discounted)火山之说，而把晚霞归因于**高空**(at high altitude)的**水汽**(moisture)或**宇宙**(cosmic)尘埃。（blaming…：现在分词短语作状语；blame A on B：把 A 归咎于 B）1884年2月，英国皇家学会发出呼吁，在《泰晤士报》上征集注明日期的观测记录，**引来了**(eliciting)世界各地的回信；学会1888年发表的报告把这片红霞归于喀拉喀托火山。（appeal for…：呼吁，征求……；dated：注明日期的）（eliciting…：现在分词短语作状语，补充说明结果）（tie A to B：把 A 与 B 联系起来）借助气压计、电缆和深红色的天空，**相隔遥远的**(far-flung)**观察者**(observers)开始**隐约看出**(glimpse)，这颗星球早已何等**紧密相连**(interconnected)。（how interconnected…：how 引导的宾语从句，作 glimpse 的宾语，语序为 how＋形容词＋主语＋谓语）（简述：1883年8月27日，喀拉喀托火山猛烈爆炸，近4,800公里外的岛民以为是炮声，世界各地的气压计都记录到了压力波。约三分之二的岛体塌入海中，海啸淹没爪哇岛和苏门答腊岛沿岸，官方统计死亡36,417人。电报经海底电缆几小时内传到伦敦，这常被称为首个全球新闻事件。火山灰和二氧化硫形成的薄雾绕赤道扩散，欧美出现深红色晚霞，消防员误以为失火，次年全球气温略降。有专家不信火山之说，皇家学会征集各地观测，1888年的报告把红霞归于喀拉喀托，人们由此看到这颗星球早已紧密相连。）
